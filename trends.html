Year-over-year analysis

Migration, Not Growth

The most honest interpretation of the 2024-to-2025 data is not "themes grew" but "themes migrated." The core problems that AI hardware research identified in 2024 — KV cache bandwidth, quantization math, near-data computation, specialized silicon efficiency — did not go away. They matured, and as they matured, they moved to different venues and deeper into the stack.

T3 and T1: stable plurality, deepening problems

T3 (Memory hierarchy, PIM, HBM, CXL) is the largest theme in 2025 by matched-venue count: 201 papers. The 2024 matched sample had 15. Memory was the plurality theme in 2024, and it remains the plurality theme in 2025. This is the one comparison that holds up across the coverage gap — the weight was there before and the weight is still there. The field's central bet on near-data processing and CXL-attached memory appears in every matched venue: HPCA (39 papers, up from 2), DAC (77, up from 2), ASPLOS (40, up from 0), and MICRO (13, up from 3).

T1 (Attention, LLM serving, KV-cache) had the largest raw 2024 presence: 17 matched papers, the single biggest theme in that sample. In 2025 it reaches 164, still the second-largest theme. The MLSys signal is clean: T1 grew from 11 to 31 papers (30% to 51% share) as the venue expanded from 37 to 61 analyzed papers. The serving stack — prefill/decode disaggregation, KV cache compression, speculative decoding — is concentrating, not dispersing.

MLSys has effectively become the primary venue for LLM inference systems research, with T1 now accounting for more than half its output.

T2: quantization matured and migrated

T2 (Quantization) was 12 papers in the 2024 matched sample, second only to T1. In 2025 it is 76. But here is where the coverage gap bites hardest: MLSys 2024 had 9 T2 papers out of 37 analyzed (24%), while MLSys 2025 has only 4 T2 papers out of 61 (7%). This is a real signal, not a coverage artifact. MLSys has deprioritized pure quantization research, likely because the W4A8 recipe is now established. The quantization papers moved downstream to DAC (35 papers, where they become circuit-level problems) and HPCA (10 papers, where they become microarchitecture problems). The theme did not shrink; it matured and migrated.

ASPLOS: the security turn

ASPLOS shows the most dramatic theme shift in the matched set. The 2024 sample had zero security papers and zero reliability papers. In 2025 there are 24 security papers (15% of venue output) and 13 reliability papers (8%). ASPLOS has absorbed a significant slice of hardware security research that previously concentrated at security-specific venues. The driver is the intersection of confidential computing — SGX, TrustZone, ARM CCA — with LLM deployment: when you serve a model inside a TEE, you need both the security guarantee and the performance of near-memory computation, and ASPLOS sits at exactly that intersection.

DAC: the integration venue

DAC had even coverage across all themes in 2024 (about 1 paper per theme). In 2025 it is the most evenly distributed venue, with T9 (18%), T3 (17%), T6 (16%), T1 (9%), T2 (8%), T8 (8%), and T7 (9%) all represented. DAC in 2025 is the venue that covers the widest cross-section of AI hardware — effectively the systems integration conference where the problems that were solved at HPCA and MICRO get turned into manufacturable circuits. No theme dominates DAC because DAC is where everything converges before fabrication.

SC: HPC absorbs the LLM workload

SC in 2024 had only T3, T4, and T6 represented — memory, interconnect, compiler, the classic HPC stack. In 2025, T0 (catch-all systems) is the plurality at 35%, followed by T9 (13%), T4 (10%), T5 (7%), T3 (6%), and T8 (7%). SC has absorbed large language model workloads into the HPC scheduling and reliability stack: running training on 65,000-GPU clusters requires the same fault tolerance and collective communication infrastructure as traditional HPC, and SC is where that work lives. The appearance of T5 (sparsity/MoE) at SC — absent entirely in 2024 — reflects MoE training runs entering the HPC workload mix.

MICRO: broadening beyond the memory wall

MICRO in 2024 was led by T3 (memory) and T5 (sparsity). In 2025 T3 remains dominant at 28%, but T9 (specialized silicon, 15%) and T7 (security, 13%) grew significantly. MICRO is broadening beyond the memory wall to include the correctness and security implications of heterogeneous memory designs — a natural trajectory once the baseline hardware innovation is established. When a new memory technology ships, the next questions are always: what are the side-channel implications, and what formal guarantees do we have about the timing behavior?

Forward projection

What this suggests about 2026

  • 01 T3 and T1 deepen, not shift. Memory hierarchy research and attention acceleration are not approaching saturation — they are approaching commercial deployment, which generates more research problems, not fewer. The CXL memory tiering papers at HPCA 2025 will become silicon validation papers at DAC 2026 and production case studies at MLSys 2026.
  • 02 T7 (security) grows significantly at architecture venues. Every major cloud provider is now deploying confidential AI inference. The hardware architecture of a TEE-protected inference system — where the integrity tree lives, how you bound HBM access patterns, what memory encryption costs at KV-cache bandwidth — is a set of open questions that HPCA and MICRO will absorb over the next two years. The 24 security papers at ASPLOS 2025 are the leading edge.
  • 03 T6 (compiler) at ASPLOS will keep growing. The compiler layer for non-standard numeric formats — MX, FP8, block floating point — is unsolved. Neither is the HLS gap at FCCM, nor the auto-tuning problem for heterogeneous memory hierarchies. Compilers are the glue between hardware innovations and application workloads, and the hardware is currently innovating faster than the compilers can follow.
  • 04 The number to watch is energy efficiency's share as a primary metric. The shift from "how fast?" to "how efficiently fast?" — from ms/token to TOPS/W as the headline metric — is the leading indicator that the field is entering deployment phase, where edge and embedded constraints matter as much as datacenter throughput. When energy efficiency becomes primary at ISCA (currently a latency/throughput venue), the transition from research innovation to deployment engineering will be complete. That is probably 18 months away.
