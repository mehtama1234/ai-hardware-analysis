# Asymmetric Predictive Testing for Aging in SRAMs

**Venue:** DAC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only analysis)

## Problem
Symmetric aggressive testing for aging-induced SRAM failures causes excessive over-testing, spuriously marking functional chips as defective.

## Method
SRAM cells storing zeros experience asymmetric aging—they fail differently depending on whether you read them as zeros or ones. The method exploits this by testing each operation at a tailored voltage: writes at normal voltage, reads of zeros at aggressive voltage, and reads of ones at less-aggressive voltage. This replaces the uniform over-testing of standard approaches with targeted stress only where needed. Statistical sampling and simulation then quickly identify which chips actually fail.

## Key Novelty
Using different test voltages for each SRAM operation based on how it actually ages, rather than uniform aggressive conditions, reduces false rejections by 3-5× while still catching real failures.

## Results
- **3-5× over-testing reduction** for low under-testing limits
