# Multi-Sensor Data Fusion for Enhanced Detection of Laser Fault Injection Attacks in Cryptographic Hardware: Practical Results

**Venue**: DATE  
**Primary Theme**: laser-attack detection  

## Problem
Detecting laser fault injection attacks on cryptographic hardware deployed in FPGAs. Laser attacks offer high precision and temporal control, making them difficult to detect with single sensors.

## Method
The detection system uses cheap sensors called Time-to-Digital Converters (TDCs) that measure the voltage changes caused by laser heating of the chip. By combining data from at least two of these sensors instead of just one (multi-sensor fusion), the system can reliably detect laser attacks while avoiding false alarms.

## Key Novelty
The key idea is combining measurements from multiple Time-to-Digital Converters (sensors that measure voltage timing) to reliably detect laser attacks on cryptographic chips with minimal false alarms.

## Contributions
- TDC-based sensing of laser-induced transients
- Multi-sensor fusion scheme for reliable detection
- Practical validation on Xilinx Artix-7 FPGA with real laser setup
