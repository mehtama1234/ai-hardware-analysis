# isscc-2025-085: Megapixel Global-Shutter CMOS Sensor

**Venue:** ISSCC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Rolling-shutter dominance due to limitations of global-shutter approaches (charge/voltage-domain); scaling pixel-parallel ADC to 25Mpixel while maintaining quality/power is hard.

## Motivation
Professional photographers demand large-format global-shutter sensors matching rolling-shutter image quality and DR.

## Method
The sensor places a separate analog-to-digital converter (ADC — a circuit that converts light signals to digital numbers) directly on each pixel in a stacked 3D structure. Because every pixel converts its signal simultaneously (a "pixel-parallel" architecture), all 25.2 million pixels digitize at the same instant rather than row-by-row, creating a "global shutter" that eliminates the rolling-shutter distortion of conventional cameras. This design maintains high dynamic range (visible detail in bright and dark areas) and low random noise even at 120 frames per second.

## Key Novelty
Stacking an analog-to-digital converter on each pixel in a 3D structure enables 25 million-pixel global-shutter imaging with high dynamic range and minimal noise.

## Contributions
- 25.2Mpixel global-shutter sensor
- 120fps full-frame capture
- Stacked pixel-parallel ADC
- Professional imaging quality (high DR, low RN)

## Metrics
- Resolution: 25.2 Mpixel
- Frame rate: 120 fps
- Shutter: global
- Architecture: pixel-parallel ADC
