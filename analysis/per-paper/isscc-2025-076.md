# isscc-2025-076: Rectifier-Less Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting

**Venue:** ISSCC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Conventional PEH systems (bias-flip rectifiers + MPPT) work well for periodic vibrations but fail under shocks due to slow impedance-matching speed for fast amplitude variations.

## Motivation
Scaling edge IoT sensors requires efficient energy harvesting from real-world mixed vibration+shock environments; battery power is not scalable.

## Method
The system removes the traditional rectifier that converts piezoelectric vibrations into electrical power, eliminating the energy wasted in that conversion step. Instead, it uses a sense-and-track algorithm that continuously measures the vibration and adjusts the electrical impedance (the load resistance) in analog electronics to match the current vibration amplitude. This adjustment completes in a single vibration cycle—fast enough to keep up with sudden shock impacts where the amplitude changes rapidly. By skipping the rectifier and adapting this quickly, it extracts more power than traditional systems, especially under shock conditions.

## Key Novelty
Single-cycle maximum power point convergence using a rectifier-free design and sense-and-track algorithm allows instant adaptation to fast-changing shock vibrations.

## Contributions
- 568% shock power improvement
- Single-cycle MPP convergence
- Rectifier-less topology (lower losses)
- Handles both periodic and shock inputs
