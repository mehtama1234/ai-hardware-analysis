# isscc-2025-087: Flexible Ka-Band Satellite Phased-Array Receiver

**Venue:** ISSCC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Small satellite constellations require diverse RF receivers: lightweight satellites need low-power single-polarization; heavier satellites need high-speed dual-polarization from independent sources.

## Motivation
Cost-effective LEO satellite constellations demand flexible, adaptable RF front-ends for diverse mission profiles and power budgets.

## Method
The receiver uses 256 antenna elements arranged in a phased array—a technique where multiple antennas work together and their signal timing is adjusted to focus reception in particular directions. It operates in the Ka-band microwave frequencies. The design positions switches and quadrature hybrids (circuits that split signals into two versions shifted 90 degrees apart) early in the signal chain, allowing the same hardware to flexibly switch between receiving single circular-polarized waves (where the wave rotates in one direction), dual circular-polarized waves (receiving two circular rotations simultaneously), or linear-polarized waves (where the wave vibrates in a fixed plane)—different encoding schemes satellites use depending on their mission.

## Key Novelty
The switch-type quadrature-hybrid-first design enables flexible polarization switching without duplicating hardware, reducing power consumption by 50% when operating in single-polarization mode.

## Contributions
- 256-element Ka-band phased-array
- Switch-type quadrature hybrid
- 50% power reduction for single-polarization
- Dual-independent signal reception

## Metrics
- Array elements: 256
- Frequency: Ka-band
- Power reduction: 50% for single-polarization
