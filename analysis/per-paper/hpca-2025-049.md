# hpca-2025-049 — Revisiting Reliability in Large-Scale Machine Learning Research Clusters

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)  
**Primary Theme:** ML cluster reliability and failure characterization

## Problem
Large-scale ML training clusters suffer from frequent job failures whose impact on training efficiency is not well characterized across different job sizes and cluster configurations. Existing failure models do not adequately capture workload diversity or provide actionable metrics like Effective Training Time Ratio (ETTR).

## Motivation
As ML models scale to thousands of GPUs, even rare hardware or software faults cause disproportionate disruption, yet no rigorous taxonomy or quantitative framework exists to guide infrastructure and algorithmic reliability investments.

## Method
The researchers tracked 4 million jobs over 11 months across two large GPU clusters (using A100 GPUs) with over 150 million GPU-hours of data, categorizing the different types of failures and calculating how often jobs fail on average (Mean Time to Failure, or MTTF) based on the number of GPUs each job uses. They built a mathematical model of when and why jobs fail, then created a formula called the Effective Training Time Ratio (ETTR) that estimates how much useful training time a job actually gets based on checkpoint frequency (how often you save progress), restart delays, and failure rate. They used this formula to show how strategies like saving progress more often would reduce the impact of failures.

## Key Novelty
The key idea is the Effective Training Time Ratio (ETTR), a formula that estimates what fraction of a training job's time is spent actually training rather than failing and recovering, enabling engineers to predict the real impact of software improvements like more frequent checkpointing across different cluster sizes.

## Contributions
1. An 11-month empirical dataset covering 4 million jobs and 150 million+ A100 GPU-hours with a structured failure taxonomy.
2. A fitted failure model that projects MTTF as a function of GPU job scale.
3. An analytical ETTR estimator parameterized by job size, checkpoint interval, and restart overhead.
4. Operational insights showing smaller jobs dominate job count and must be included in reliability optimization objectives.
