# Snake-3D: Differentiable Learning for Cross-Tier Logic Path Snaking Optimization in 3D ICs

**Venue:** ICCAD

## Problem
3D IC designs suffer from excessive snaking along critical paths and require many bond pads, complicating production and degrading timing.

## Motivation
Post-Moore 3D ICs offer superior timing and lower power but current flows rely on bin-based FM partitioning that overlooks inter-bin connections.

## Method
It divides a 3D circuit into functional sections (partitioning) using gradient-based optimization (calculating the best partition by following slopes of errors), running on GPU with PyTorch. The cost function accounts for snaking — the winding paths signals take through layers — and timing constraints (how quickly signals must travel). It also spaces circuit elements evenly to avoid clustering. This early-stage optimization reduces wasteful winding on speed-critical paths and cuts the number of bond pads (metal connections between layers) needed.

## Key Novelty
It combines snaking and timing awareness into gradient-based partitioning at the earliest design stage, accelerated on GPU.

## Contributions
- 29% reduction in final bond pad count
- 34% average snaking reduction
- 20% maximum snaking reduction
- 17% WNS improvement and 42% TNS improvement
- 47% bond pad reduction vs GNN-based methods

## Hardware Target
SoC (3D IC)

## Technique Categories

## Metrics
- **Speedup:** 7.4x faster than GNN-based, similar to default flow
- **Area:** 29% bond pad reduction
- **PPA:** WNS 17% better, TNS 42% better
- **Snaking:** 34% average, 20% maximum reduction

## Baselines

- GNN-based methods

## Limitations
Evaluated on limited technology nodes (7nm and 28nm); applicability to other nodes unclear.
