# Variable Read Disturbance: An Experimental Analysis of Temporal Variation in DRAM Read Disturbance

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
DRAM read disturbance (RowHammer/RowPress) mitigation techniques rely on accurate per-row read disturbance threshold (RDT) characterization, but the RDT of individual DRAM rows is not static: it varies significantly and unpredictably over time, making accurate characterization with a limited number of measurements infeasible.

## Motivation
If mitigations are calibrated based on an inaccurately characterized (non-minimum) RDT, they will fail to prevent bitflips when the actual RDT drops below the assumed value, creating exploitable security vulnerabilities in modern DRAM systems.

## Method
The researchers tested 160 DDR4 and 4 HBM2 memory chips from three manufacturers by repeatedly measuring each row's read disturbance threshold—the number of times a row can be read before it corrupts its neighbors—watching how this threshold changed over time. They discovered that the minimum threshold only appeared after up to 94,467 measurements and could be 3.5 times lower than the highest observed value. They examined how the threshold variability changed based on chip density, manufacturing process, data patterns, how long the attacking row stayed active, and temperature. They evaluated whether guardbanding (reserving safety margins) or ECC error correction methods like SECDED and Chipkill could defend against this varying vulnerability.

## Key Novelty
This is the first experimental demonstration that DRAM rows' sensitivity to read disturbance (how quickly repeated reads can corrupt neighboring rows) varies dramatically and unpredictably over time—termed Variable Read Disturbance—undermining the assumptions that existing RowHammer protection methods rely on.

## Contributions
- Discovery and characterization of Variable Read Disturbance (VRD) phenomenon across 160 DDR4 and 4 HBM2 chips
- Empirical evidence that minimum RDT requires up to 94,467 measurements and is 3.5x below maximum observed RDT
- Analysis of density, node, data pattern, temperature, and open time effects on RDT variability
- Evaluation of RDT guardbanding + ECC as mitigations, showing >10% guardband with SECDED/Chipkill needed at ~45% performance cost
