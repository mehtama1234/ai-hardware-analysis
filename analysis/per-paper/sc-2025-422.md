# Advancing Quantum Many-Body GW Calculations on Exascale Supercomputing Platforms

**Paper ID:** sc-2025-422
**Venue:** SC
**Authors:** Benran Zhang, Daniel Weinberg, Chih-En Hsu, Aaron R. Altman, Yuming Shi, James B. White, Derek Vigil-Fowler, Steven G. Louie

## Problem

Quantum many-body GW calculations for electronic structure require high computational accuracy but face prohibitive numerical complexity, limiting ab initio materials simulations to small systems despite need for exascale modeling.

## Motivation

State-of-the-art electronic excited states and couplings require costly GW methods; heterogeneous systems with thousands of atoms demand exascale computing to achieve both accuracy and scale for rational quantum technology design.

## Method

The GW method (a quantum simulation of electron interactions) is implemented in the BerkeleyGW package to run efficiently on different GPU types—the AMD MI250X on Frontier and Intel Xe on Aurora. The approach splits the calculation into two levels and optimizes how computing nodes communicate with each other to minimize data-transfer delays.

## Key Novelty

The GW implementation scales nearly linearly to 9,408+ nodes on Frontier with 59.45% of peak performance, and the same code runs efficiently on both AMD MI250X and Intel Xe GPUs without modification.

## Contributions

- Exascale-optimized GW kernel implementations in BerkeleyGW for both AMD and Intel GPUs
- Demonstrated strong and weak scaling to thousands of nodes (17,574-atom systems)
- Performance characterization on Frontier (1.069 ExaFLOP/s) and Aurora (707.52 PetaFLOP/s)
- Predictive capabilities for quantum technology material design
