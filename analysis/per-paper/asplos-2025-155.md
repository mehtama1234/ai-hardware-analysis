# asplos-2025-155: Optimizing Quantum Circuits, Fast and Slow

**Venue:** ASPLOS  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Quantum circuit optimization separates fast rewrite-rule passes (local, low quality) from slow unitary synthesis (global, high quality, exponential cost) without a unified framework to exploit their synergies.

## Motivation
Minimizing gate count is critical for NISQ execution; existing optimizers either sacrifice quality or scalability, leaving a large optimization gap.

## Method
The algorithm guoq alternates between two steps: fast pattern-matching rewrites that simplify local regions of the circuit and identify which parts would benefit from deeper optimization, and targeted exhaustive synthesis that finds the best possible equivalent circuit for those identified regions. Since these targeted subcircuits are small enough to search exhaustively, this division breaks down the otherwise exponential problem into manageable pieces. This fast-slow interleaving works because rewriting guides synthesis to high-value targets, while synthesis achieves improvements that local pattern-matching alone could never reach.

## Key Novelty
The guoq algorithm unifies fast rewriting and slow synthesis: rewriting identifies which subcircuits deserve expensive global optimization by simplifying locally, then synthesis exhaustively optimizes those identified regions, achieving stronger results than either technique alone because each guides the other.

## Contributions
- Unifying framework: rewriting and resynthesis as abstract circuit transformation operators
- guoq algorithm interleaving fast rewrite passes and synthesis on high-gain subcircuits
- Strong outperformance of existing quantum circuit optimizers across wide benchmarks
- Radically simple algorithm achieving superior optimization through fast-slow synergy
