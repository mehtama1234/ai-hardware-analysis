# TetrisLock: Quantum Circuit Split Compilation with Interlocking Patterns
**Venue:** DAC

## Problem
Untrusted quantum compilers can steal circuit intellectual property (IP) and reverse-engineer proprietary quantum algorithms.

## Motivation
As quantum computing becomes practical, protection against compiler-level IP theft is critical for competitive quantum algorithm developers.

## Method
TetrisLock splits a quantum circuit into two pieces that interlock like puzzle parts—each one depends on the other to function. Neither piece by itself reveals the complete circuit's behavior or design, making it impossible to steal or reverse-engineer the intellectual property from just one piece. When both pieces are combined, the original circuit works as designed, and this eliminates the need to trust the compiler with the full, unprotected circuit.

## Key Novelty
Splitting quantum circuits with interlocking patterns so that neither individual piece alone can be used to reverse-engineer the original design.

## Contributions
1. Quantum circuit obfuscation against untrusted compilers
2. Defense against collusion attacks with mismatched qubit counts
3. Minimal circuit depth overhead (<1% accuracy loss)
