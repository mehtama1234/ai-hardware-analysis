# Constant-Rate Entanglement Distillation for Fast Quantum Interconnects

**ID:** isca-2025-088 | **Venue:** ISCA | **Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
Existing quantum interconnect protocols cannot simultaneously achieve high fidelity and high rate of entanglement generation under realistic noisy local operations, blocking distributed quantum computing. Current distillation methods either have vanishing rate or cannot tolerate physical noise levels.

## Motivation
Fault-tolerant distributed quantum computing requires constant-rate, high-fidelity entanglement across interconnects; all practical proposals fall short, preventing modular large-scale quantum computer construction.

## Method
The protocol develops constant-rate entanglement distillation (conversion of noisy quantum pairs to high-fidelity ones at a steady rate) through repeated two-way distillation rounds (cycles where partners exchange information to identify and eliminate errors). It uses quantum error-detecting codes with increasing code rate (starting with strict error-checking, then relaxing rules as pairs improve), combined with standard fault-tolerance techniques (ways to keep systems working reliably even when errors occur) to ensure that a constant fraction of raw entanglement pairs become high-fidelity logical pairs (very-high-quality quantum states for computation). Numerical optimization minimizes practical overhead within finite memory constraints.

## Key Novelty
This is the first constant-rate (in expectation) entanglement distillation protocol for noisy quantum interconnects, achieved by chaining quantum error-detecting codes (methods that identify errors without destroying quantum information) with increasing rate in a two-way distillation ladder (back-and-forth refinement cycles).

## Contributions
1. Constant-rate entanglement distillation scheme tolerating noisy local operations
2. Theoretical proof of constant rate in expectation
3. Numerical optimization to reduce overhead under memory constraints
4. Demonstrated improvement over existing quantum interconnect methods for fault-tolerant regimes
