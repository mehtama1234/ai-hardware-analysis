# Circuit Folding: Scalable and Graph-Based Circuit Cutting via Modular Structure Exploitation

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**ID:** iccad-2025-086

## Problem
Circuit cutting for quantum circuit partitioning on NISQ hardware relies on graph-based methods that face prohibitively high computational complexity when identifying optimal cutting locations, especially under realistic hardware constraints.

## Motivation
Executing large quantum circuits on NISQ hardware requires effective circuit cutting that leverages both quantum and classical resources, but existing approaches struggle with scalability and computational efficiency.

## Method
CiFold finds repeated sequences of quantum operations within a circuit and merges them into single building blocks, creating a condensed representation (meta-graph) of the full circuit. Using this smaller map, it can quickly identify where to optimally split the circuit across multiple quantum processors without the computational expense of checking every possible cut location. Two metrics—folding factor and variance—ensure the compression is balanced and the circuit's structural properties are maintained.

## Key Novelty
By merging repeated patterns in quantum circuits through circuit folding, the method enables efficient, scalable identification of cutting locations that would otherwise require prohibitive exhaustive searching.

## Contributions
- Circuit folding technique identifying and merging common gate sequences for compact meta-graph representation
- Folding factor and variance metrics quantifying circuit compression and balance
- Scalable cut location identification without exhaustive global search
- Average 31.6% reduction in required cuts and 3.55× 10⁹ reduction in sampling overhead
