# ρHammer: Reviving RowHammer Attacks on New Architectures via Prefetching

**Venue:** MICRO 2025  
**Confidence:** High (analyzed from full text)

## Problem
Conventional load-based (MOV instruction) RowHammer attacks have become ineffective on the most recent Intel architectures (Alder Lake / Raptor Lake, 12th–14th gen), producing zero bit flips, primarily because insufficient DRAM row activation rates and more aggressive OoO/speculative execution disrupt the hammering order.

## Motivation
DRAM densification continues to increase RowHammer vulnerability, but attack techniques have lagged behind architectural defenses and microarchitectural complexity of newer platforms, leaving a critical gap in understanding real-world exploitability.

## Method
ρHammer introduces three coordinated techniques:
1. **Layout-agnostic DRAM address mapping reverse-engineering** using selective pairwise SBDR (Same-Bank Different-Row) timing measurements in a three-step Duet/Trios/Quartet structured deduction, recovering full bank functions in polynomial time without prior knowledge of bank bit counts or layouts — completes in under 10 seconds.
2. **Prefetch-based hammering paradigm** exploiting the asynchronous nature of x86 PREFETCHT2/PREFETCHNTA instructions (retire after forwarding to L1 LFB without stalling the CPU), combined with multi-bank parallelism to maximize per-tREFI activation rate while bank-interleaving latency also alleviates disorder.
3. **Counter-speculation technique** using runtime control-flow obfuscation (indirect addressing) and NOP-based pseudo-barriers tuned to neutralize OoO execution and branch prediction reordering, since explicit memory barriers cannot order prefetch instructions per Intel SDM.

## Key Novelty
A prefetch-based Rowhammer hammering paradigm that exploits asynchronous x86 software prefetch instructions to bypass load-based activation rate bottlenecks, enabling the first successful RowHammer attacks on Raptor Lake where all prior methods completely fail.

## Contributions
- Generic DRAM address mapping reverse-engineering via Duet/Trios/Quartet SBDR deduction — recovers mappings in <10s where all existing tools fail on Alder/Raptor Lake.
- Prefetch-based hammering with multi-bank parallelism: 112x higher flip rates vs load-based on Comet/Rocket Lake; 187K flips/min on Comet Lake.
- Counter-speculation technique via control-flow obfuscation + NOP pseudo-barriers to handle prefetch-specific disorder.
- First RowHammer attacks on Raptor Lake (14th-gen): 2,291 flips/min; baselines produce zero. Open-sourced implementation.

## Metrics
- Comet Lake: 187K flips/min (112.4x over load baseline); 200K+ bit flips in 2-hour fuzzing
- Rocket Lake: 47K flips/min (47.1x over baseline)
- Raptor Lake: 2,291 flips/min (baseline: 0)
- Address mapping recovery: <10 seconds (baseline tools fail entirely on Alder/Raptor Lake)

## Baselines
Load-based Rowhammer (MOV + clflushopt), BlackSmith-style non-uniform hammering frameworks, SMASH

## Limitations
Requires root privileges (Linux pagemap) for virtual-to-physical translation; does not address DDR5 or TRR-equipped DDR5 DIMMs; RowPress effects are explicitly out of scope.
