# isscc-2025-161: Outphasing Sub-THz Phase Shifter

**Problem:** Sub-THz phased-array transceivers (125–170GHz) require broadband, power-efficient phase shifters. Conventional designs struggle with phase accuracy, amplitude balance, and efficiency at high frequencies.

**Motivation:** Sub-THz comms enable fiber-like data rates with beamforming; silicon-based phased arrays reduce cost vs. III-V; efficient phase control critical for beam steering.

**Method:** The phase shifter uses outphasing, a technique that splits the signal into two constant-amplitude paths—this allows the device to handle amplitude and phase changes without wasting power in traditional amplifiers. Built-in gain and phase adjustments compensate for manufacturing variations and temperature changes. The design uses SiGe BiCMOS (a silicon-germanium process combining bipolar and CMOS transistors), which can operate reliably at 125–170GHz while keeping multiple components on a single chip.

**Key Novelty:** Outphasing-based phase shifter with integrated gain/phase correction achieving power efficiency at sub-THz.

**Contributions:**
- 125–170GHz broadband
- Outphasing gain + phase control
- Integrated gain/phase correction
- Power-efficient SiGe BiCMOS

**Hardware Target:** ASIC

**Technique Categories:** circuit-design, analog, rf-design

**Metrics:** 125–170GHz, SiGe BiCMOS, outphasing, power-efficient

**Baselines:** Passive phase shifters, conventional active designs, prior SiGe sub-THz
