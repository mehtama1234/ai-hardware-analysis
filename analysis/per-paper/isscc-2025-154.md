# isscc-2025-154: Current-Mode Neurostimulator with Electrode Scalability

**Problem:** Miniaturized wireless neurostimulators lose received power as coil shrinks; rising electrode-tissue impedance degrades PCE and charge accuracy in traditional voltage-regulated designs.

**Motivation:** Minimal invasiveness requires tiny receiver coils; yet multi-site high-impedance electrode arrays demand scalable, precise stimulation across impedance variations.

**Method:** Instead of receiving power as voltage (the standard approach), the system receives power directly as current from the wireless coil, which maintains high efficiency even when the electrical resistance between electrode and tissue (electrode-tissue impedance) changes—no separate voltage regulation needed. A feedback loop continuously monitors the current flowing into the tissue and adjusts it to stay constant, ensuring precise delivery of charge regardless of how this resistance varies. This design works with different electrode sizes because it can maintain steady output current while tolerating high voltages.

**Key Novelty:** Current-mode receiver with integrated current feedback achieving PCE and SEF efficiency independent of electrode-impedance variation.

**Contributions:**
- High PCE with miniaturized receiver
- High SEF independent of ETI
- Precise charge delivery via current feedback
- Electrode impedance scalability

**Hardware Target:** ASIC

**Technique Categories:** power, circuit-design, wireless, feedback-control

**Metrics:** Optimized PCE, high SEF, precise charge, scalable ETI

**Baselines:** Voltage-regulated stimulators, impedance-adaptive designs
