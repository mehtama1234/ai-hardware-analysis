# hpca-2025-026: CORDOBA: Carbon-Efficient Optimization Framework for Computing Systems

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Optimizing the carbon footprint of computing systems across large hardware design spaces is intractable with existing methods because it requires simultaneously trading off operational carbon, embodied carbon, performance, power, and area (PPA) under uncertainty in carbon intensity parameters.

## Motivation
The semiconductor industry's growing contribution to global carbon emissions demands design-time carbon optimization, but existing approaches focus on energy or PPA independently, ignoring embodied carbon from chip manufacturing.

## Method
CORDOBA uses a single metric called tCDP (total Carbon Delay Product)—the total lifecycle carbon (manufacturing plus operation) multiplied by execution time—to optimize hardware designs by balancing carbon and performance in one goal. The framework explores different hardware configurations by varying core counts, memory hierarchy, and chip stacking approaches (2D vs. 3D), then eliminates designs that are inferior in all dimensions (Pareto-dominated), discarding up to 98% of candidates. Two case studies demonstrate this on real CPU systems: reducing processor cores from 8 to 4, and adopting 3D memory-logic stacking.

## Key Novelty
The tCDP metric unites operational carbon, embodied carbon, and performance into a single optimization target, enabling systematic carbon-efficient hardware design exploration.

## Contributions
- Definition and justification of total Carbon Delay Product (tCDP) as a carbon-efficiency metric.
- CORDOBA framework for large-scale carbon-aware design-space exploration, pruning 98% of configurations.
- Case study: 1.25x tCDP improvement by reducing CPU cores from 8 to 4.
- Case study: 6.9x tCDP improvement via 3D-stacked logic+memory versus conventional 2D.

## Metrics
- tCDP improved 1.25x (8→4 cores); tCDP improved 6.9x (3D stacking vs. 2D); 98% design-space elimination

## Limitations
Carbon intensity parameters carry significant uncertainty and the framework's conclusions are sensitive to regional grid carbon assumptions not always publicly available.
