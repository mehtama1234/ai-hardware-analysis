# Neoscope: How Resilient Is My SoC to Workload Churn?

**Venue:** ISCA  
**ID:** isca-2025-094  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Hardware lifetimes are increasing while software workloads evolve rapidly, so SoCs optimized for today's workloads may perform poorly against future workloads; architects lack quantitative tools to design for long-term performance resilience.

## Motivation
The growing mismatch between hardware specialization and workload evolution (churn) threatens the useful lifetime of SoCs and increases the risk of premature hardware obsolescence.

## Method
Neoscope defines two quantitative measures—Magnitude and Disruption—that track how a workload changes over time, sorting the churn into four categories: Minimal, Perturbing, Escalating, or Volatile. It then frames finding the best chip design as an integer linear program (a mathematical technique that systematically finds the best combination of discrete design choices) combined with job-shop scheduling principles (concepts from factory scheduling that help arrange complex, competing tasks). This lets it find near-optimal chip designs in a single pass rather than exhaustively testing thousands of design combinations. The framework simultaneously optimizes five objectives: performance, chip area, energy consumption, cost, and carbon emissions.

## Key Novelty
Neoscope is the first pre-silicon chip-design framework that explicitly measures workload churn (how workloads change over time) and uses integer linear programming to find designs resilient to this churn, while simultaneously optimizing for performance, area, energy, cost, and carbon emissions.

## Contributions
1. Formal definitions of workload Magnitude and Disruption metrics to quantitatively characterize workload churn.
2. Churn span taxonomy classifying evolution as Minimal, Perturbing, Escalating, or Volatile.
3. Neoscope ILP+job-shop-scheduling optimizer that finds near-optimal SoC designs in a single invocation.
4. Multi-objective optimization for performance, area, energy, cost, and carbon efficiency under diverse churn scenarios.

## Metrics
- Small SoCs require high specialization but face higher churn risk; Neoscope finds global optimum in one shot vs. parameter sweeps.

## Baselines
- Parameter sweep design space exploration methods

## Limitations
Neoscope's churn model relies on representative workload traces that may not capture all real-world evolution patterns; ILP scalability for very large SoC design spaces is not discussed.
