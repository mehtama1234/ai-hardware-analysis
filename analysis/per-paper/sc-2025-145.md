# Inverse Design for Generating Initial Conditions in Scientific Simulations

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Leslie A. Horace, Christin Whitton, Vanessa Job, William M. Jones, Nathan DeBardeleben

## Problem
Scientific simulations governed by PDEs with time-evolving heterogeneous material interactions require appropriate initial conditions to reproduce observed phenomena. Finding initial conditions matching target output states (transient or steady) constitutes a challenging many-to-one inverse problem with potentially many valid solutions.

## Motivation
Many scientific applications require initial conditions that produce specific observed outputs. Traditional approaches rely on trial-and-error or expensive forward simulations. Generative models could efficiently explore the space of valid initial conditions while quantifying uncertainty.

## Method
Conditional normalizing flow (CNF) surrogate model for inverse problem solving:

1. **Model Architecture:** Conditional normalizing flow trained to map from target output states to plausible initial condition distributions

2. **Case Study 1:** Electrostatic potential inverse problem - generate initial field configurations producing target potentials

3. **Case Study 2:** Heat diffusion inverse problem - generate initial temperature distributions reproducing steady or transient heat profiles

4. **Uncertainty Quantification:** Quantify confidence in generated samples

5. **Comprehensive Workflow:** Document dataset preparation, model specification, training procedures, and evaluation protocols

## Key Novelty
Demonstrates conditional normalizing flows for inverse design in scientific simulations, enabling efficient generation of initial conditions with uncertainty quantification.

## Contributions
1. Conditional normalizing flow framework for generative inverse problems
2. Two application case studies: electrostatic and thermal systems
3. Complete workflow documentation: synthetic data generation, model specification, training, evaluation
4. Uncertainty quantification methods for generated samples
