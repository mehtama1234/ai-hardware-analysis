# Hardware-aware Calibration Protocol for Quantum Computers

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Quantum computer calibration—optimizing control parameters to ensure accurate gate implementation—does not account for physical qubit heterogeneity, and existing methods serialize calibration operations, causing excessive calibration overhead and suboptimal gate fidelity as qubit counts scale.

## Motivation
As quantum processors scale to hundreds of qubits, calibration time and gate error rates become bottlenecks to achieving fault-tolerant operation, yet current one-size-fits-all calibration strategies ignore qubit-pair-specific responses to different control waveforms.

## Method
The protocol operates in two stages. First, a profiling phase evaluates qubit-pair responses to different waveform candidates, then assigns one of three calibration policies to each pair based on the profiling data (hardware-aware, fine-grained policy selection). Second, a graph-traversal algorithm identifies sets of compatible (non-interfering) calibration operations that can execute in parallel, converting the sequential calibration schedule into a parallel one. The protocol was validated on real IBM quantum hardware with up to 127 qubits.

## Key Novelty
Fine-grained, per-qubit-pair waveform profiling that selects the best calibration policy per pair, combined with graph-traversal-based parallel scheduling, jointly reduces calibration time and two-qubit gate error simultaneously.

## Contributions
- Hardware-aware calibration profiling that evaluates qubit-pair responses to waveform candidates and selects per-pair calibration policies.
- Graph-traversal method for identifying compatible parallel calibration operations, reducing total calibration overhead 8-25x over sequential calibration.
- Demonstrated 1.84x reduction in median two-qubit gate error rate and 2x increase in quantum volume on real 127-qubit quantum hardware.
- 1.26x reduction in pulse duration and an average 2.12x additional reduction in calibration overhead from profiling-based policy selection.
