# isscc-2025-155: WF-Bonding 3D-NAND with High Speed

**Problem:** High-layer-count 3D-NAND faces: dummy-hole removal increases power; pass-voltage disturbance degrades reliability; rising WL capacitance slows program/read; high-speed I/O signal-integrity challenges.

**Motivation:** AI/data workloads need both extreme density and fast I/O; conventional 3D-NAND cannot scale both simultaneously.

**Method:** This 3D-NAND design bonds wafers together at the manufacturing level (WF-bonding) and structures voltage signals through stacked layers to prevent interference. It removes or optimizes dummy holes (non-data structural spaces) that would consume power, redesigns cell packing to reduce wear from unavoidable stray voltages on inactive cells, and maintains clean signal patterns on high-speed data pins (5.6 gigabits per second per pin) with sufficient electrical margin. The result: 28 gigabits per square millimeter of density, with 3 bits per memory cell.

**Key Novelty:** WF-bonding 3D-NAND with optimized multi-layer WL architecture enabling simultaneous density, speed, and reliability.

**Contributions:**
- 28Gb/mm2 density
- 1Tb capacity
- 5.6Gb/s/pin I/O
- 3bits/cell TLC with high-layer-count reliability

**Hardware Target:** ASIC
