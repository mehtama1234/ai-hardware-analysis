# isca-2025-043: Fair-CO2: Fair Attribution for Cloud Carbon Emissions

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)  
**Primary Theme:** fair game-theoretic carbon footprint attribution for cloud workloads

## Problem
Existing cloud carbon attribution methods unfairly allocate operational and embodied carbon footprints to tenant workloads by ignoring dynamic demand effects on embodied carbon amortization and interference effects in colocated scenarios. This leads to mispriced carbon signals that fail to incentivize workload-level carbon optimization.

## Motivation
Cloud data centers are a significant and growing source of carbon emissions, and accurate per-workload carbon attribution is essential for users to make informed deployment decisions and for cloud providers to drive accountability-based efficiency.

## Method
Fair-CO2 uses the Shapley value—a mathematical principle that fairly divides shared costs by measuring how much each workload contributes under every possible combination—to attribute cloud carbon responsibility. The system captures embodied carbon effects: instead of splitting a server's manufacturing emissions based on flat time allocation, each workload's share is based on its actual utilization rate, so a heavily-used server's manufacturing carbon is proportionally attributed to the workloads that actually used it. It also captures interference effects from colocation, where performance degradation caused by shared resource contention directly reduces the attributed costs of the workloads experiencing that degradation. Because computing the exact Shapley attribution is mathematically intractable (grows exponentially with workload count), Fair-CO2 approximates using two components: demand-aware embodied carbon (adjusting hardware depreciation by real-time utilization) and interference-aware operational costs (accounting for performance loss from co-tenancy), validated through Monte Carlo simulations to confirm the approximations' fidelity to the exact Shapley solution.

## Key Novelty
Fair-CO2 uses Shapley value—a mathematical fairness principle—to attribute cloud carbon, with scalable approximations that account for actual hardware usage (embodied carbon) and performance loss from resource sharing (interference).

## Contributions
1. Formal identification of two systematic unfairness sources in existing cloud carbon attribution: dynamic demand effects on embodied carbon and colocation interference effects
2. Proposal of the Shapley value as an attribution ground truth with theoretical fairness guarantees for cloud carbon accounting
3. Scalable Fair-CO2 approximation via demand-aware embodied carbon attribution and interference-aware resource cost attribution, validated by Monte Carlo simulations
4. Demonstration that users provided fair carbon estimates can dynamically optimize workload deployment for measurable carbon savings
