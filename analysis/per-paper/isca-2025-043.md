# isca-2025-043: Fair-CO2: Fair Attribution for Cloud Carbon Emissions

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)  
**Primary Theme:** fair game-theoretic carbon footprint attribution for cloud workloads

## Problem
Existing cloud carbon attribution methods unfairly allocate operational and embodied carbon footprints to tenant workloads by ignoring dynamic demand effects on embodied carbon amortization and interference effects in colocated scenarios. This leads to mispriced carbon signals that fail to incentivize workload-level carbon optimization.

## Motivation
Cloud data centers are a significant and growing source of carbon emissions, and accurate per-workload carbon attribution is essential for users to make informed deployment decisions and for cloud providers to drive accountability-based efficiency.

## Method
Fair-CO2 adopts the Shapley value from cooperative game theory as a theoretically fair ground truth for shared-cost attribution, capturing both dynamic demand effects on embodied carbon (hardware amortized proportionally to actual utilization, not flat time allocation) and interference effects from workload colocation. Because exact Shapley computation scales exponentially with the number of workloads, Fair-CO2 approximates it via two tractable components: demand-aware embodied carbon attribution (adjusting hardware depreciation by real-time utilization) and interference-aware resource cost attribution (accounting for performance degradation from co-tenancy). Monte Carlo simulations over workload schedules and colocation scenarios are used to validate the approximation's fidelity to the exact Shapley solution.

## Key Novelty
Applying the Shapley value as a principled fairness criterion for cloud carbon attribution and introducing scalable approximations that decompose it into demand-aware embodied and interference-aware operational components.

## Contributions
1. Formal identification of two systematic unfairness sources in existing cloud carbon attribution: dynamic demand effects on embodied carbon and colocation interference effects
2. Proposal of the Shapley value as an attribution ground truth with theoretical fairness guarantees for cloud carbon accounting
3. Scalable Fair-CO2 approximation via demand-aware embodied carbon attribution and interference-aware resource cost attribution, validated by Monte Carlo simulations
4. Demonstration that users provided fair carbon estimates can dynamically optimize workload deployment for measurable carbon savings
