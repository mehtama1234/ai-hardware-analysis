# Online Learning for Dynamic Structural Characterization in Electron Energy Loss Spectroscopy

**Venue**: DATE  
**Primary Theme**: online SNN-VAE on FPGA for materials characterization  

## Problem
High-speed in-situ EELS data acquisition (400 fps) with low SNR requires real-time processing for dynamic structural tracking without offline training bottleneck.

## Method
A spiking neural network (a type of AI inspired by how brains work, where neurons fire sparsely rather than constantly) embedded in a variational autoencoder (a learning technique that finds patterns in compressed data representations) processes spectral measurements from high-speed electron microscopy at 400 frames per second, predicting material structure as new data arrives. Unlike traditional approaches that require offline training on pre-recorded data first, this system learns continuously from the data stream as it comes in. The entire process runs on an FPGA (field-programmable gate array—customizable hardware), achieving signal clarity of 28.3 dB and processing speeds 25.2 to 93.7 times faster than standard methods.

## Key Novelty
This is the first electron energy loss spectroscopy system that learns continuously from incoming data using brain-inspired neural networks combined with a data-compression learning method, all running directly on hardware without needing any pre-recorded training data.

## Contributions
- SNN-VAE framework for EELS spectral denoising and structural prediction
- 28.3 dB SNR achievement in MXene annealing data
- FPGA hardware implementation with full online learning capability
- 25.2× latency reduction, 93.7× on full hardware, 4.5× energy savings vs baseline
