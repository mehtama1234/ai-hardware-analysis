# VLSID 2025-034: Re-Convergent Clock Mesh for AI Compute Clusters

## Summary
Novel clock mesh topology for large AI compute clusters achieving 20% power reduction at target frequency or 20% performance gain at target voltage compared to flex-H tree and forwarded spine architectures.

## Problem
AI ICs with large synchronous compute clusters require robust, low-noise global clock distribution. Traditional H-tree and forwarded spine architectures cannot meet power and performance targets for modern AI processors.

## Method
- Re-convergent clock mesh topology
- Designed for globally synchronous compute clusters
- HSPICE simulation and PrimeTime timing verification
- Silicon implementation validation
- Comparison: flex-H tree, forwarded spine

## Key Results
- **20% power reduction** at target operating frequency (alternative: 20% performance gain at target voltage)
- Low clock noise
- Minimized clock skew
- Silicon implementation demonstrates feasibility

## Applications
- AI/ML processors and accelerators
- Large compute cluster designs
- High-performance neural network accelerators
- Tensor processors (TPUs, GPUs)

## Limitations
- Power and performance metrics are alternatives, not both achieved simultaneously
- No absolute numbers (GHz, mW, area)
- Silicon results lack detail (area, power, frequency)
- Limited to clock distribution only
- No discussion of clock distribution power fraction

## Hardware Targets
- AI/ML accelerators
- Large compute clusters
- High-performance processors

## Analysis Notes
**Confidence: LOW** (abstract only)

Sound contribution addressing real AI accelerator pain point. Clock distribution is typically 20-30% of chip power in high-performance designs, so 20% improvement is substantial. However, lack of absolute metrics and single design point limit assessment.
