# ArbiterQ: Improving QNN Convergency and Accuracy by Applying Personalized Model on Heterogeneous Quantum Devices

**Venue:** DAC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Distributed QNN training on heterogeneous NISQ devices suffers from device noise, limited qubits, and online/offline transitions; naïve approaches degrade accuracy.

## Key Idea
Personalize QNN models per QPU using behavioral vectors (hardware features) and share gradients among similar-behavior devices with fine-grained shot scheduling.

## Method
ArbiterQ customizes a quantum neural network (machine-learning model on quantum hardware) for each quantum processor by building two profiles: model vector (the mathematical parameters the network learns) and behavioral vector (measurements of how that specific processor performs). During training, it shares gradient updates (improvements to the model) between processors with similar behavioral profiles, allowing them to work in parallel while accounting for hardware differences. It also optimizes inference—running the trained model—by scheduling tasks (shot-oriented inference scheduling) to balance workload and improve accuracy.

## Contributions
1. Dual representation (model + behavioral vector) for device personalization
2. Gradient sharing for heterogeneous parallelism
3. Fine-grained shot-oriented scheduling
4. 4.03x speedup with 7.87% loss reduction vs. EQC
