# Make LLM Inference Affordable to Everyone: Augmenting GPU Memory with NDP-DIMM

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Deploying large LLMs (e.g., LLaMA2-70B) on consumer-grade GPUs with limited VRAM is impractical; offloading model weights to host DRAM or NVMe storage creates severe bandwidth bottlenecks on the PCIe interconnect, resulting in very low inference throughput.

## Motivation
As LLM-assisted services proliferate, democratizing LLM inference on budget consumer hardware (rather than expensive server-grade HBM-equipped GPUs) is a pressing need.

## Method
Hermes partitions LLM weights into 'hot' neurons (~20% of parameters, ~80% of compute) and 'cold' neurons (~80% of parameters, ~20% of compute) based on activation sparsity. Hot neurons are mapped to the consumer GPU for compute-efficient execution, while cold neurons are offloaded to near-data processing (NDP) units embedded in commodity DRAM DIMMs. A lightweight runtime predictor dynamically classifies neurons as hot or cold based on input activations. A window-based online scheduler maintains load balance across multiple NDP-DIMM modules, enabling pipelined heterogeneous execution between GPU and NDP-DIMMs.

## Key Novelty
Exploiting LLM activation sparsity to drive a hot/cold neuron partition that maps cold weights to NDP-DIMMs, bypassing the PCIe bandwidth bottleneck by keeping computation near DRAM.

## Contributions
- Hot/cold neuron classification leveraging ~80/20 activation sparsity split in LLMs
- Heterogeneous GPU + NDP-DIMM execution strategy with a lightweight real-time neuron predictor
- Window-based online load-balancing scheduler across NDP-DIMM modules
- 75.24x average speedup over state-of-the-art offloading inference systems; LLaMA2-70B at 13.75 tokens/s on consumer hardware
