# SAGE: Saliency-Aware Grouping for Efficient Mapping of LLMs on Analog Compute-in-Memory

**Venue:** ICCAD

## Problem
LLM inference on ACIM systems suffers from long-tailed data distributions with salient values that degrade signal quality under quantization and system noise.

## Motivation
ACIM architectures offer high bandwidth and low power by co-locating storage and computation, but hardware non-idealities challenge accurate LLM execution.

## Method
The strategy identifies weight and input channels by measuring two statistical properties: kurtosis (how much their values cluster at extremes) and saliency (how important they are to the network's output). It groups channels with high kurtosis or high saliency together and assigns them to higher-precision computational units, while grouping less critical channels for lower-precision processing, using reconfigurable hardware tiles that can switch between precision modes. This targeted grouping reduces extreme values in the output and improves signal-to-noise ratio when facing quantization and analog hardware noise. The entire process works without retraining the model.

## Key Novelty
Group weight and input channels by their statistical properties (kurtosis and saliency) and assign them to hardware with appropriate precision levels, improving noise robustness without retraining.

## Contributions
- Reduced output kurtosis enhancing signal-to-noise ratio
- Improved inference accuracy on ACIM systems
- Mixed-precision execution with reconfigurable tile design
- Training-free optimization method
