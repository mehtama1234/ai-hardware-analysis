# hpca-2025-035: EXION: Exploiting Inter- and Intra-Iteration Output Sparsity for Diffusion Models

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** high (full text)  
**Authors:** Jaehoon Heo, Adiwena Putra, Jieon Yoon, Sungwoong Yune, Hangyeol Lee, Ji-Hoon Kim, Joo-Young Kim (KAIST)

## Problem
Diffusion model inference requires up to 1000 denoising iterations through transformer blocks (38-100% of operations per model), causing 23.6x higher energy and 9.8x higher latency than GANs on identical GPUs. Prior transformer accelerators target only QKV and attention within a single iteration, ignoring inter-iteration redundancy and the dominant FFN layer overhead (up to 67% of per-iteration ops in transformer blocks).

## Motivation
Diffusion models are increasingly deployed for text-to-image, -video, -audio, and -motion generation, but their energy and latency are prohibitive; the iterative denoising structure creates a unique and unexploited source of temporal data redundancy in FFN outputs across iterations.

## Method
EXION combines software and hardware acceleration to skip redundant calculations in diffusion models. The FFN-Reuse algorithm recognizes that the feedforward network layer (a key component of transformer models) produces similar outputs across neighboring iterations; it computes the first iteration fully, then reuses those outputs in subsequent iterations where values are too small to affect results, achieving 70-97% sparsity in the first layer and reducing feedforward operations by 52-85%. A second algorithm predicts which attention scores (the mathematical weights that determine which input parts matter most) will be negligible using a two-step leading-one detection method, enabling it to skip those computations within each iteration (20-95% sparsity, skipping an average of 26% of query projections and 22% of key-value projections). The ConMerge mechanism converts the scattered sparse outputs into compact dense matrices suitable for efficient execution on the specialized Diffusion-Sparsity Aware Core hardware—a chip with a 16x16 processing array, synthesized at 14nm, 800MHz, 0.8V.

## Key Novelty
FFN-Reuse exploits output redundancy in feedforward networks across iterations by reusing computation, while ConMerge converts the resulting sparse outputs into compact dense matrices executable at high hardware utilization.

## Contributions
1. FFN-Reuse algorithm achieving 70-97% inter-iteration FFN output sparsity and 52-85% FFN op reduction across 7 diffusion model types without retraining.
2. Improved eager prediction with TS-LOD enabling accurate intra-iteration attention sparsity (20-95%) without accuracy degradation in iterative diffusion context.
3. ConMerge mechanism (condensing + conflict-vector-guided merging) converting unstructured output sparsity to compact form; Stable Diffusion: 77.4%->8.4% remaining columns.
4. EXION ASIC achieving 3.2-379.3x speedup and 45.1-3067.6x energy efficiency over RTX 6000 Ada server GPU; 42.6-1090.9x speedup and 196.9-4668.2x energy efficiency over Jetson Orin Nano edge GPU; 5.2x vs. Cambricon-D on DiT (transformer-only model).

## Hardware Target
ASIC (14nm synthesis, commercial PDK)

## Techniques
sparsity, dataflow, approximation, kernel-fusion

## Workloads
Evaluated on 7 diffusion models: MLD, MDM (text-to-motion), EDGE (music-to-motion), Stable Diffusion (text-to-image), DiT (class-to-image), Make-an-Audio (text-to-audio), VideoCrafter2 (text-to-video)

## Baselines
- NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada (server GPU, 91.1 TFLOPS, 960 GB/s, 300W)
- NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (edge GPU, 40 TOPS, 68 GB/s, 15W)
- NVIDIA A100 GPU (for Cambricon-D comparison)
- Cambricon-D (ISCA 2024 diffusion accelerator)

## Limitations
ResBlock-containing models (Make-an-Audio, Stable Diffusion) show reduced efficiency gains because sparsity optimizations are applied only to transformer blocks; ResBlocks are not addressed. Accuracy exceptions exist for MDM and EDGE on one of two metrics under out-of-dataset inputs.
