# isca-2025-077 — Synchronization for Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers

**Venue:** ISCA 2025  
**Authors:** Satvik Maurya, Swamit Tannu (University of Wisconsin-Madison)  
**Confidence:** high (full text)

## Problem
Logical qubits in FTQC systems using the surface code and Lattice Surgery can become desynchronized across their syndrome generation cycles due to heterogeneous QEC codes (color, qLDPC), fabrication defects, and modular chip-to-chip coupling latencies. Forced synchronization by idling the leading qubit accumulates decoherence errors, increasing logical error rates.

## Motivation
Idling errors are the second-highest error contributor in Google's FTQC experiments even with dynamical decoupling, and 1–11 synchronization events occur per QEC cycle in practical workloads — making this unavoidable at scale.

## Method
In fault-tolerant quantum computers, logical qubits (bits encoded across many physical qubits for error correction) sometimes run at different speeds and must synchronize before merging via Lattice Surgery (a qubit-merging operation). This paper proposes three synchronization strategies: Passive idles the fast qubits for their entire time deficit right before the merge—one long wait that lets decoherence errors build up; Active spreads this idle time into small chunks inserted after each error-correction round, preventing long waits; and Hybrid runs extra error-correction rounds on slower qubits to shrink the time deficit, then applies Active to the remainder. A hardware controller (Patch Counter Table, Phase Calculators, and Synchronization Slack Calculator) tracks when each qubit region finishes its cycles and injects timing directives into the control schedule without adding latency.

## Key Novelty
Active synchronization distributes idle time across multiple error-correction rounds rather than all at once, reducing peak decoherence per round and cutting the logical error rate by up to 3.4x.

## Contributions
1. Characterization of desynchronization sources and frequency (1–11 syncs/cycle) across real workloads.
2. Active policy: up to 2.4x LER reduction vs. Passive.
3. Hybrid policy: up to 3.4x LER reduction and 2.2x decoding latency speedup.
4. Open-source stabilizer circuit generator integrated with Stim for Lattice Surgery simulation.

## Metrics
- Logical error rate: 3.4x reduction (Hybrid vs. Passive), 2.4x (Active vs. Passive)
- Decoding latency: 2.2x speedup
- Validated on IBM Brisbane (127-qubit) and IBM Sherbrooke quantum hardware

## Baselines
- Passive synchronization (full-slack idle before Lattice Surgery)

## Limitations
Evaluations restricted to surface code + Lattice Surgery; color code and qLDPC code generalization is conceptual only.
