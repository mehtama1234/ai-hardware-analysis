# ZRAID: Leveraging Zone Random Write Area (ZRWA) for Alleviating Partial Parity Tax in ZNS RAID

**Venue:** ASPLOS 2025  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
ZNS RAID partial parity for incomplete stripes is logged in reserved zones, creating throughput bottlenecks and write amplification that reduce device lifetime.

## Motivation
ZNS SSDs reduce conventional block-interface overhead, but RAID construction introduces a "partial parity tax" negating key efficiency benefits.

## Method
ZRAID uses ZRWA—a small random-write buffer built into ZNS SSDs (solid-state drives with sequential write zones)—to temporarily hold partial parity (information needed to rebuild a failed drive). Instead of writing partial parity to reserved zones, which causes throughput slowdowns and extra wear, ZRAID accumulates multiple partial writes in ZRWA until they combine into complete data blocks (called stripes) ready for sequential writing to the drive. This eliminates both the throughput bottleneck and the accelerated wear that come from logging fragments. ZRAID coordinates this buffering across multiple drives to maintain data consistency.

## Key Novelty
ZRAID uses ZNS SSDs' built-in random-write buffers (ZRWA) for temporary parity storage instead of reserved zones, eliminating the throughput bottlenecks and extra wear from partial parity logging.

## Contributions
- Characterization of the partial parity tax problem in ZNS RAID
- ZRAID design exploiting ZRWA for partial parity staging
- Elimination of reserved-zone throughput bottleneck and write amplification
