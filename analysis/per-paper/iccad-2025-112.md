# MMPack: Multi-Mask Co-Design for Ultra-Large Wafer-Scale Package Integration

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Ultra-large wafer-scale integration requires multi-mask lithography for manufacturing, introducing physical design constraints, mask stitching discontinuities, and thermo-mechanical stress near TSVs and stitching regions that conventional floorplanning methods fail to address.

## Motivation
Advanced AI/HPC systems demand large-scale chiplet integration; reliable physical design methods are critical for managing complexity, yield, and thermal reliability in wafer-scale systems.

## Method
MMPack partitions an ultra-large chip into smaller sections (chiplets) arranged to minimize communication crossing mask boundaries—the seams where different manufacturing masks connect. Using hierarchical layout planning with alternating optimization (iteratively refining different parts of the design), it respects manufacturing constraints imposed by these seams. Finally, it applies physics models to identify high-stress regions near mask seams and through-silicon vias (vertical connections between layers), then redesigns them to reduce thermal and mechanical stress.

## Key Novelty
MMPack is the first framework that jointly optimizes for both the manufacturing constraints of splitting chips across multiple production masks and the thermal-mechanical stress (damage from heat-induced material expansion) that concentrates at mask seams in ultra-large wafer-scale integration.

## Contributions
- Performance-driven partitioning algorithm for multi-mask integration
- Stitching-aware hierarchical floorplanning strategy
- Stress-aware post-processing reducing critical stress
- Demonstrated scalability for large wafer-scale systems
