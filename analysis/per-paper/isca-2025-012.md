# Accelerating Simulation of Quantum Circuits under Noise via Computational Reuse

**Venue:** ISCA 2025  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Noisy quantum circuit simulation requires thousands of near-identical circuit re-executions across noise model trials, recomputing the same intermediate quantum states from scratch each time, creating a major HPC bottleneck.

## Motivation
Noise-aware simulation is the primary validation tool for quantum error mitigation, yet its iterative trial structure makes it computationally intractable at scale.

## Method
TQSim (Tree-Based Quantum Circuit Simulation) breaks a quantum circuit into smaller subcircuits and saves the intermediate quantum states (density matrices or statevectors—the two formats for tracking quantum information) produced by each one. When running the circuit multiple times with different noise, the system reuses the saved states for subcircuits unaffected by the noise, and only recalculates the ones the noise changes. This avoids redundant recomputation—otherwise each noise trial would recalculate all intermediate states from scratch. The system is designed for distributed HPC clusters and maintains tight fidelity bounds, ensuring approximations stay accurate.

## Key Novelty
Tree-based partitioning of quantum circuits enables reuse of intermediate quantum states across noise simulations, reducing total computation proportional to how much can be reused.

## Contributions
1. TQSim: tree-based intermediate reuse framework for noisy quantum simulation
2. Dynamic circuit partitioning algorithm for identifying reusable subcircuit boundaries
3. Up to 3.89x speedup over Qulacs-based baseline
4. Multi-node scalable design with tight fidelity bounds

## Hardware Target
CPU (HPC clusters)

## Key Metrics
- Speedup: up to 3.89x over Qulacs-based noisy simulator

## Baselines
Qulacs-based noisy quantum circuit simulator

## Limitations
Not discussed; fidelity bounds under high noise rates and partition granularity trade-offs are not elaborated.
