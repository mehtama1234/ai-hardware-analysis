# Benchmark-driven Models for Energy Analysis and Attribution of GPU-Accelerated Supercomputing

## Overview
Extensible microbenchmark-driven energy attribution model for GPUs, enabling fine-grained energy analysis by functional unit, memory level, and control/datapath for four GPU generations.

## Problem & Motivation
- **Challenge**: GPU energy consumption opaque; coarse-grained power metrics insufficient. Practitioners lack granular energy understanding limiting optimization and procurement.
- **Opportunity**: Microbenchmark-derived fine-grained models enable targeted optimization, architecture innovation, and informed procurement
- **Scale**: Four generations of GPUs analyzed with validation on supercomputing and ML/AI workloads

## Architecture & Method
**Energy Attribution Dimensions**

1. **Functional Units**
   - FPU (floating-point units)
   - Tensor cores (specialized matrix units)
   - Integer ALU (integer operations)

2. **Memory Hierarchy**
   - L1 cache
   - L2 cache
   - HBM (high-bandwidth memory)

3. **Energy Type**
   - Control energy (instruction decode, scheduling, power management)
   - Datapath energy (actual computation, data movement)

**Microbenchmark Methodology**
- Parameterized kernels isolating individual components
- Multi-generation comparison (4 GPU generations)
- Energy-per-operation metrics (Joules/FLOPs, Joules/memory-access, etc.)

**Validation**
- Supercomputing application workloads
- ML/AI training and inference workloads
- Real procurement/deployment scenarios

## Key Contributions
1. **Granular energy attribution model** (functional unit + memory level + control/datapath)
2. **Multi-generation analysis** revealing energy-per-operation trends
3. **Tensor core energy** characterization (important for ML)
4. **Memory energy** breakdown (L1/L2/HBM differentiation)
5. **Actionable insights** for:
   - Algorithm optimization (which operations cost most energy)
   - Architecture research (bottleneck identification)
   - Power-constrained optimization strategies
   - Procurement decisions (energy-efficiency comparisons)

## Performance Insights
- **Energy per operation** varies dramatically by unit type
- **Memory energy** potentially dominant in communication-bound workloads
- **Control energy** vs datapath tradeoff important at small problem sizes
- **Tensor cores** highly efficient (vs FPU) but specialized

## System Design Implications
- Tensor core utilization critical for ML energy efficiency
- Memory hierarchy design decisions (L1 vs L2 vs HBM bandwidth) impact energy
- Control overhead analysis enables better scheduling/batching
- Multi-generation trends inform architecture roadmap

## Limitations
- **NVIDIA-only**: AMD, other vendors not covered
- **Microbenchmark representativeness**: Synthetic kernels may not reflect real application patterns
- **Extrapolation**: Future generation prediction methodology not discussed
- **Absolute values**: Energy values (Joules) not specified in abstract
- **Model complexity**: Abstract-only prevents assessment of model fidelity

## Relevance to Hardware/AI Systems
**High relevance**: Essential for understanding GPU energy consumption patterns, critical for power-constrained supercomputing and ML/AI systems optimization.
