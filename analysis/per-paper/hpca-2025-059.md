# Reuse-Aware Compilation for Zoned Quantum Architectures Based on Neutral Atoms

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Neutral-atom quantum computers use heterogeneous zoned architectures (storage, entangling, readout zones), and compiling quantum circuits for them requires efficiently managing qubit movement between zones to minimize fidelity-degrading data movement overhead.

## Motivation
Zoned architectures significantly improve computation fidelity by shielding idling qubits from noise, but the increased structural complexity makes efficient compilation much harder than for monolithic single-zone architectures.

## Method
ZAC (Zoned Architecture Compiler) optimizes quantum circuits for zoned quantum computers by keeping qubits in the entanglement zone when they'll be used there immediately, avoiding wasteful round-trips to and from storage. It also predicts future operations to decide optimal qubit placement and schedules operations more efficiently for zone-specific constraints using ZAIR, an intermediate representation that tracks which zone each qubit needs to be in. The compiler uses a flexible specification language to adapt to different zoned quantum architectures. On real circuits, this approach achieves 22x better signal quality (fidelity) compared to monolithic (non-zoned) quantum computers.

## Key Novelty
The key insight is that keeping qubits in the entanglement zone when they're about to be used there cuts out wasteful movement between zones, which is the primary source of signal quality loss in zoned quantum computers.

## Contributions
1. ZAC compiler with reuse-aware scheduling retaining qubits in the entanglement zone across consecutive entangling gates
2. ZAIR intermediate representation for flexible specification of diverse zoned neutral-atom architectures
3. Novel data placement and instruction scheduling strategies tailored to multi-zone architectures
4. 22x fidelity improvement over monolithic architectures; within 10% of ideal solution on average
