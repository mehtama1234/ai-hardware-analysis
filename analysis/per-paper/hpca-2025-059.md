# Reuse-Aware Compilation for Zoned Quantum Architectures Based on Neutral Atoms

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Neutral-atom quantum computers use heterogeneous zoned architectures (storage, entangling, readout zones), and compiling quantum circuits for them requires efficiently managing qubit movement between zones to minimize fidelity-degrading data movement overhead.

## Motivation
Zoned architectures significantly improve computation fidelity by shielding idling qubits from noise, but the increased structural complexity makes efficient compilation much harder than for monolithic single-zone architectures.

## Method
ZAC introduces qubit reuse as a core optimization: qubits already in the entanglement zone are kept there if an immediate entangling gate is pending, avoiding round-trip movement to storage. ZAC additionally uses novel data placement strategies anticipating future gate scheduling needs, a new instruction scheduling algorithm, a flexible specification language for diverse zoned architectures, and ZAIR (an intermediate representation capturing zoned-architecture-specific constraints).

## Key Novelty
Reuse-aware compilation that keeps qubits in the entanglement zone when subsequent entangling operations are imminent, dramatically reducing inter-zone data movement which is the dominant fidelity-degrading cost in zoned neutral-atom architectures.

## Contributions
1. ZAC compiler with reuse-aware scheduling retaining qubits in the entanglement zone across consecutive entangling gates
2. ZAIR intermediate representation for flexible specification of diverse zoned neutral-atom architectures
3. Novel data placement and instruction scheduling strategies tailored to multi-zone architectures
4. 22x fidelity improvement over monolithic architectures; within 10% of ideal solution on average
