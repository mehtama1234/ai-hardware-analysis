# isscc-2025-084: High-Speed Distributed Voltage Droop Detector

**Venue:** ISSCC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Voltage droop detection needs PVT tolerance + remote distribution; TDC-based detectors lack robustness; analog detectors have large area/routing constraints.

## Motivation
Per-core voltage scaling critical for power efficiency in modern SoCs; requires accurate, fast droop detection.

## Method
The detector spreads tiny voltage-sensing circuits (called remote droop detectors, or RDD) across the chip that continuously sample at up to 4GHz. A central control circuit (droop detection controller, or DDC) coordinates all these distributed sensors and automatically adjusts for manufacturing variations and environmental conditions (PVT tolerance), keeping them synchronized. Together, they detect voltage droop in under 2 clock cycles with 0.69% accuracy, implemented in 2nm transistor technology.

## Key Novelty
Instead of a single bulky centralized detector, this design distributes tiny voltage sensors across the chip with centralized coordination that adapts to manufacturing and temperature variations, enabling high-speed detection everywhere without routing problems.

## Contributions
- 0.69% detection accuracy
- <2-cycle latency at 4GHz
- Distributed remote sensing
- PVT-tolerant centralized control
