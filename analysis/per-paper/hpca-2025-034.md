# hpca-2025-034: GSArch: Breaking Memory Barriers in 3D Gaussian Splatting Training via Architectural Support

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
3DGS training is bottlenecked by three memory inefficiencies during backward propagation: redundant off-chip DRAM loads for large hot Gaussians, expensive atomic gradient write operations, and severe bank conflicts during on-chip buffer reads.

## Motivation
Online 3DGS training for autonomous driving and embodied AI requires much higher training throughput; existing 3DGS accelerators address only rendering, not the backward pass.

## Method
GSArch introduces: (1) Hybrid memory management classifying Gaussians as hot (large, high pixel overlap) or cold, caching hot ones on-chip to eliminate redundant DRAM fetches; (2) Gradient informativeness filtering skipping low-contribution atomic gradient writes; (3) A rearrangement unit packing conflicting on-chip memory reads into non-conflicting bundles. All three target the 3DGS training backward pass.

## Key Novelty
Gaussian 'hotness' classification for hybrid on-chip/off-chip memory management eliminating the dominant redundant DRAM load pattern in 3DGS training backward propagation.

## Contributions
- Characterization of three 3DGS training backward-pass bottlenecks.
- Hybrid memory management with hot/cold Gaussian classification and on-chip buffering.
- Gradient informativeness filtering to skip low-contribution atomic writes.
- Rearrangement unit eliminating bank conflicts; up to 6.49x speedup over A100, 15.42x over Jetson AGX Xavier with negligible quality loss.

## Metrics
- speedup: up to 6.49x over A100; up to 15.42x over Jetson AGX Xavier
- energy: substantially lower (not quantified in abstract)
