# PFASware: Quantifying the Environmental Impact of Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) in Computing Systems

**Venue**: DATE  
**Primary Theme**: PFAS reduction in computing  

## Problem
Computing systems manufacturing uses PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl forever chemicals) extensively. Need to quantify environmental impact and identify design optimizations to reduce PFAS use.

## Method
The researchers measured how much PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances—persistent chemicals used extensively in semiconductor manufacturing) is needed for different computer chip designs. They tested this across chips built with different feature sizes (7nm, 16nm, and 28nm process nodes, where smaller numbers mean denser circuits) and different lithography methods: EUV (extreme ultraviolet light, a more advanced technique) versus DUV immersion (an older approach using deep ultraviolet light with special fluids). By comparing PFAS amounts across all these manufacturing variations, they could see which design and manufacturing choices result in the least environmental chemical usage.

## Key Novelty
The work shows that semiconductor manufacturing's PFAS environmental impact can be substantially reduced by optimizing the choice of lithography method and production process node.

## Contributions
- Quantified PFAS volume for IC designs at multiple technology nodes
- 20% PFAS reduction achievable with EUV vs DUV at 7nm
- 15% PFAS reduction at 16nm vs 28nm
- Design guidance for PFAS reduction
