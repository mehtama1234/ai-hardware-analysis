# Synthesis of Standard Cells of Minimum Delay

**ID:** iccad-2025-033  
**Venue:** ICCAD

## Problem
Conventional standard cell synthesis prioritizes area minimization at the top level, yielding suboptimal timing for high-performance chips where delay is the critical constraint.

## Motivation
At advanced technology nodes, minimizing cell delay is essential for implementing high-performance chips and improving clock frequency.

## Method
The approach uses a search-tree algorithm that tests different transistor arrangements and eliminates dead-end options, focusing on transistors in the speed-critical path that determine overall chip speed. It then reorganizes transistors using dynamic programming to maximize diffusion sharing—reusing electrical connections between transistors to improve performance. Finally, it optimizes the wiring between transistors by formulating the speed-critical connections as a mathematical constraint-satisfaction problem.

## Key Novelty
Prioritizing speed (delay) at the cell level over area (size), inverting the traditional synthesis approach.

## Contributions
1. Critical-path-driven transistor placement with effective pruning for enumeration
2. Optimal transistor folding via dynamic programming for diffusion-sharing maximization
3. Optimal critical-net routing via SMT formulation
4. Cell delay improvements of up to 9.3% enabling 7.2% average frequency increase while maintaining power and area
