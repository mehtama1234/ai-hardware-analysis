# QuaLITi: Quantum Machine Learning Hardware Selection for Inferencing with Top-Tier Performance

**Paper ID:** vlsid-2025-062
**Venue:** VLSID
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
NISQ computers suffer from noise, long access queues, and high costs. QML algorithms are iterative and require multiple executions with defined shot counts, making single-hardware optimization impractical.

## Motivation
QML presents acceleration opportunity but hardware constraints (noise, queue depth, cost) limit practical deployment. Current practice relies on local simulators rather than real hardware due to access timing overhead.

## Method
The researchers train and test quantum machine learning models on several different quantum computers, each with different qubit types, connection patterns between qubits, and noise levels (errors in computation). They measure both how well the models work and how long users have to wait to access each machine, then analyze which hardware choice completes the training job fastest overall.

## Key Novelty
By switching between multiple quantum computers based on queue wait times, you can train quantum machine learning models with nearly the same accuracy but significantly faster than waiting for a single machine.

## Contributions
- Multi-hardware QML training strategy reducing total wall-clock time
- Empirical analysis of noise impact across different quantum hardware
- Framework for optimal hardware selection based on queue depth and coupling maps
- 45X reduction in training wait time with only 3-4% accuracy impact
