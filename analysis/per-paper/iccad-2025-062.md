# Designing and Training Neural Networks for Analog In-Sensor Deployment: A Hardware-Aware Analysis

**Venue:** ICCAD

## Problem
Deploying CNNs on analog in-sensor hardware faces power, area, and latency constraints alongside noise, quantization errors, and lack of digital ISP preprocessing.

## Motivation
Edge AI and IoT require ultra-low latency and energy efficiency by processing directly in analog domain to minimize data movement.

## Method
The paper analyzes how to design and train image-recognition neural networks (CNNs) that work on analog camera chips—sensors that process light directly as analog signals before digital conversion. It derives design guidelines by working backward from hardware constraints: which network topologies, training methods, and data-processing strategies let the network handle real-world analog challenges like noise (random signal variations), quantization errors (information loss in the analog-to-digital conversion), and raw unrefined sensor output. The key recommendations cover network architecture choices, training recipes that make the network robust to noise and quantization, and techniques to process raw sensor data directly without special digital image preprocessing (ISP).

## Key Novelty
The core idea is to design neural networks and their training specifically for the constraints of analog in-sensor hardware—accounting for noise, quantization errors, and raw sensor data—to enable efficient image recognition directly on the chip.

## Contributions
- Design recommendations for AIS-friendly network topologies
- Training recipes improving noise and quantization robustness
- Strategies for learning from raw sensor data without digital ISP
- Complementary software-based techniques to circuit and architecture approaches
