# Finding the Pareto Frontier of Low-Precision Data Formats and MAC Architecture for LLM Inference

**Venue:** DAC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Complex design space of low-precision formats and MAC implementations makes tradeoff identification difficult.

## Key Idea
Systematic evaluation of 35,000+ MAC designs across formats (INT, FP, posit, LNS, MX, VSQ) to identify Pareto-optimal efficiency/accuracy tradeoffs.

## Method
The researchers evaluated over 35,000 multiply-accumulate (MAC) circuits—the basic arithmetic components in neural networks—using six different number formats (INT, FP, posit, LNS, MX, VSQ) and various design parameters like operation size and accumulation width. Each design was tested for three qualities: chip area, power efficiency (measured in calculations per watt), and arithmetic accuracy (using signal-to-quantization noise ratio). The analysis then identified the Pareto frontier—the optimal designs where you can't improve one metric without worsening another.

## Contributions
1. Comprehensive MAC design space evaluation
2. Pareto-optimal identification across formats
3. 1.8x-2.2x TOPs/W improvement (LNS16/MXINT8/VSQINT4 vs. FP)
