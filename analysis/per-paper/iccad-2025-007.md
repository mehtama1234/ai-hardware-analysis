# Waferscale Silicon Photonics Systems: A Cost-Benefit Analysis and Optimization

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**ID:** iccad-2025-007

## Problem
Waferscale systems suffer from long-reach electrical interconnect overheads; silicon photonics offers potential but implementation costs and feasibility across topologies are unclear.

## Motivation
Quantifying silicon photonics benefits and overheads for waferscale enables informed architecture choices balancing latency reduction against power/area costs.

## Method
The paper evaluates large-scale chips using silicon photonics—light signals replacing electrical ones—built from tiles connected in different topologies (network layouts). It tests various waveguide implementations (optical paths) and how well they work for different applications. It specifically measures the power-consumption limits of microring resonators (small rings that direct light signals) and evaluates the performance of custom-designed waveguide networks.

## Key Novelty
This is the first comprehensive comparison of silicon photonic versus electrical interconnects for waferscale chips, showing that the benefits and implementation challenges depend on the application and topology.

## Contributions
1. Performance comparison of silicon photonics vs. electrical interconnects at waferscale
2. Analysis showing benefits primarily from reduced communication latency
3. Identification of high power/area overheads, especially for high connectivity
4. Custom waveguide network approach addressing microring resonator power limits
