# isca-2025-026 — Variational Quantum Algorithms in the era of Early Fault Tolerance

**Venue:** ISCA 2025  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
Emerging 10,000-qubit devices with ~0.1% two-qubit error rates enable lightweight QEC but cannot afford full surface-code overhead near threshold, creating an Early Fault Tolerance (EFT) gap. VQAs must be adapted to exploit EFT resources without the hardware budget of full fault tolerance.

## Motivation
Quantum roadmaps predict EFT devices within 3–5 years; VQAs are prime near-term applications but lack a principled EFT compilation/error-correction strategy, wasting qubits and fidelity.

## Method
The approach implements partial quantum error correction (a strategy to catch and fix quantum computation errors without excessive hardware overhead) by protecting certain operations (Clifford operations) with lightweight error codes while handling more complex operations (Rz rotations) through magic state injection, which is more efficient than an alternative method called T-state distillation. Architectural optimizations cut circuit latency by 2x and achieve 66% qubit efficiency. It was validated by comparing quantum state fidelity (accuracy) improvements to standard algorithms without error protection, across different Early Fault Tolerance device configurations.

## Key Novelty
This work adapts partial quantum error correction—protecting Clifford operations with lightweight codes and complex operations via magic state injection—to variational quantum algorithms in the Early Fault Tolerance regime, achieving a 9.27x fidelity improvement while avoiding the prohibitive qubit overhead of full surface-code error correction.

## Contributions
1. EFT-VQA problem definition bridging NISQ and full fault tolerance.
2. pQEC strategy: Clifford QEC + magic state injection for Rz (vs. T-state distillation).
3. Architectural optimizations reducing EFT-VQA circuit latency by 2x.
4. 9.27x fidelity improvement; 66% qubit packing efficiency.
