# Characterizing the Impact of GPU Power Management on an Exascale System

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Toledo Costa, Georgiadou, White, Villasenor Alvarez, Polo, Shin, Navaux, Messer

## Problem
Exascale GPU systems require fine-grained energy management, but frequency and power capping employ different hardware control mechanisms and produce different effects on workloads. No clear guidance exists for choosing between strategies.

## Approach
**Empirical characterization on Frontier** (AMD MI250X-based exascale supercomputer):
- Multiple HPC proxy applications representing production workloads
- Diverse application profiles (compute-bound, memory-bound, bursty, steady)
- Frequency capping vs. power capping at multiple levels
- Single-node (1) and multi-node (32) experiments
- Measure: performance, energy efficiency, scalability

## Key Findings
1. **Frequency capping** achieves higher energy efficiency and scalability (~13.2% gain without performance loss)
2. **Power capping** more effective for single-node or bursty GPU utilization
3. Different hardware control paths create distinct workload effects
4. Trade-offs vary significantly by application profile and scale

## Hardware Target
- **AMD MI250X** GPU (Frontier node)
- Multi-node cluster (32-node scale)
- Leadership-class exascale system

## Metrics
| Metric | Frequency Capping | Power Capping |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| Energy efficiency gain | Up to 13.2% | Application-dependent |
| Performance loss | Minimal | More significant |
| Single-node effectiveness | Good | Good for bursty workloads |
| Scalability | High | Mixed |

## Practical Implications
- Frequency capping preferred for most large-scale workloads
- Power capping valuable for single-node or highly dynamic applications
- System-specific tuning essential for exascale deployments

## Limitations
- Frontier-specific results; MI250X behavior may not generalize to other GPUs
- "Diverse proxy applications" not detailed in abstract
- No comparison with dynamic voltage/frequency scaling (DVFS) on CPU side
