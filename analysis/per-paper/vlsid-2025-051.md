# A 0.5pJ/bit 7.2Gbps HBM3 PHY on Intel4 with EMIB Packaging and Unmatched Receiver Architecture on PHY Side with Per Bit Deskew Correction

**ID**: vlsid-2025-051 | **Venue**: VLSID | **Confidence**: low (abstract only)

## Problem Statement
High-bandwidth memory (HBM3) PHY design at sub-5nm technology requires extreme energy efficiency while maintaining signal integrity at 7.2 Gbps per pin. Multi-die interconnect (EMIB) introduces latency and variability. Supply-induced jitter and temperature drift degrade receiver eye margin.

## Motivation
AI accelerators and hyperscale data-center systems demand memory interfaces combining ultra-high bandwidth, low latency, and extreme power efficiency (pJ/bit range). HBM3 with advanced packaging (EMIB) requires novel PHY techniques to realize these requirements simultaneously.

## Method
- **Technology**: Intel 4 (advanced process node)
- **Packaging**: Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge (EMIB)
- **Transmitter**: Unterminated N-N low-swing design eliminating termination power
- **Receiver**: Unmatched receive paths (lower power than conventional matched impedance)
- **Jitter Mitigation**: 
  - Internal linear voltage regulator (LVR) for supply-noise management
  - Deterministic jitter suppression
- **I/O Training**: 
  - Per-bit deskew (PBD) for per-pin eye optimization
  - On-the-fly periodic sampling-point update (no traffic interruption)
  - Temperature-drift adaptation in both read and write directions

## Key Results
**Energy & Performance**:
- **0.5 pJ/bit** at 7.2 Gbps (state-of-the-art efficiency for HBM3)
- **5-cycle latency** for per-bit deskew correction
- **Robust operation** across temperature and process variation

## Novelty & Contributions
1. **Ultra-low-power transmitter** via unterminated low-swing signaling
2. **Unmatched receiver architecture** trading impedance matching for power efficiency
3. **Integrated LVR** eliminating external voltage regulation
4. **Per-bit deskew (PBD)** enabling fine-grained eye optimization per lane
5. **Non-disruptive thermal adaptation** via dynamic sampling-point updates
