# isscc-2025-067

**Title:** 32.4 A Dual-LC-Resonant Isolated DC-DC Converter Achieving 65.4% Peak Efficiency and Inherent Backscattering

**Venue:** ISSCC

**Problem:** Integrated isolated DC-DC converters (with on-chip transformers) suffer low efficiency (~52%) due to TX LC-oscillator and RX full-bridge rectifier losses; feedback requires digital isolators.

**Key Contribution:** Dual-LC resonant topology with inherent backscatter feedback achieves 65.4% peak efficiency (+6–13% over state-of-the-art), eliminating digital isolators.

**Method:** The circuit uses two LC resonators (coil-capacitor circuits tuned to vibrate at a set frequency), optimized separately for the input and output sides. The input side drives oscillation through an optimized LC circuit, while the output side converts this to DC power using resonant rectification—a rectifier tuned to that frequency—instead of a traditional full-bridge rectifier that wastes energy across multiple diodes. The output side provides voltage regulation by modulating how much current it draws, which modulates the input resonator through backscattering (a technique where load changes couple back through the resonator), eliminating the need for an expensive digital isolator.

**Hardware:** ASIC

**Metrics:** 65.4% peak efficiency

**Techniques:** Power delivery, circuit design

**Primary Theme:** Dual-LC resonant isolated DC-DC with backscatter
