# A Practical Quantum Solver for Multidimensional Partial Differential Equations

**ID:** sc-2025-061 | **Venue:** SC

## Problem Statement
Solving multidimensional PDEs with low accuracy and high execution time on quantum devices

## Motivation
VQA-based quantum PDE solvers suffer from low accuracy, scalability issues on both simulators and real quantum hardware; classical methods don't leverage quantum advantages

## Method
The solver uses the finite difference method—which breaks a complex mathematical problem into calculations on a grid of small points—and converts these calculations into quantum operations using the C2Q compiler. It provides two algorithm variants: one directly converts the grid calculations into a quantum circuit, while the other breaks the work into column-by-column operations to improve accuracy and performance on larger problems. This design allows the solver to run on both quantum computer simulators and real quantum hardware.

## Key Novelty
The solver uses direct conversion of grid-based calculations to quantum operations instead of variational quantum algorithms (which adjust quantum circuits through repeated trial-and-error), achieving better results on quantum simulators and real quantum devices.

## Contributions
- Novel quantum algorithm variants for PDE solving
- Higher accuracy than VQA-based approaches
- Demonstrated on Poisson, heat, Black-Scholes, Navier-Stokes equations
- Results on both noise-free/noisy IBM simulators and real hardware
