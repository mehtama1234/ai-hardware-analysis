# Orion: A Fully Homomorphic Encryption Framework for Deep Learning

**Venue:** ASPLOS 2025  
**ID:** asplos-2025-042  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
Mapping arbitrary deep neural networks to FHE primitives is intractable with existing tools due to vector-packing complexity, noise budget management, and the limited FHE instruction set—limiting FHE neural inference to shallow or small networks.

## Motivation
Neural inference in the cloud exposes user data to service providers; FHE enables inference on encrypted inputs but no automated, scalable compiler has existed for large models.

## Method
Orion is an automated compiler that translates deep neural networks from PyTorch into programs that compute directly on encrypted data (fully homomorphic encryption). The core problem is that encrypted computation requires packing numbers into encrypted slots, and different packing arrangements cause combinatorial explosion—Orion's single-shot multiplexed packing strategy efficiently handles arbitrary convolutions without generating all those possibilities. It also automatically decides where to insert bootstrap operations (which refresh the noise budget in encrypted computation) by tracking noise accumulation across layers and placing bootstraps where they cost the least. This approach enables end-to-end compilation of large networks including ResNet-20, ResNet-50, and YOLO-v1 (139 million parameters) without manual FHE engineering.

## Key Novelty
Orion combines a single-shot multiplexed packing strategy for arbitrary convolutions with automated bootstrap placement that minimizes noise-refresh costs, enabling fully automatic compilation of large convolutional neural networks to fully homomorphic encryption (computation on encrypted data).

## Contributions
- Single-shot multiplexed packing for FHE-mapping of arbitrary convolutions.
- Automated bootstrap placement and noise/scale management for deep networks.
- Orion open-source framework (PyTorch input → FHE output).
- 2.38x speedup over state-of-the-art on ResNet-20; first FHE YOLO-v1 and ResNet-50 on ImageNet.
