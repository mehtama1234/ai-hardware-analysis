# Multiscale Light-Matter Dynamics in Quantum Materials: From Electrons to Topological Superlattices

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Taufeq Mohammed Razakh, Thomas Linker, Ye Luo, Nariman Piroozan, Simon John Pennycook, Nalini Kumar, Albert Musaelian, Anders Johansson

## Problem & Motivation

Light-induced control of topological properties in quantum materials offers transformative applications in ultralow-power, ultrafast devices ("topotronics"). However, simulating light-matter dynamics at realistic scales (millions of electrons, trillions of atoms) over long timescales overwhelms traditional computational approaches. The challenge compounds when targeting exascale systems with heterogeneous hardware and competing precision requirements.

## Approach

**MLMD (Multiscale Light-Matter Dynamics)** framework using **DC-MESH** (divide-conquer-mesh) strategy:

1. **Problem Decomposition:** Divides multiscale/multiphysics problems into spatial and physical subproblems with small dynamic ranges and minimal mutual information.
2. **Hardware-Aware Mapping:** Assigns subproblems to hardware best-suited for their characteristics (CPUs for control, specialized GPUs for dense compute).
3. **Metamodel-Space Algebra:** Reduces communication overhead and precision requirements through problem reformulation.
4. **Neural-Network QMD:** XS-NNQMD module leverages neural networks for excited-state quantum molecular dynamics.

## Key Novelty

- **Divide-Conquer-Recombine:** Systematic decomposition enabling heterogeneous hardware exploitation.
- **Precision-Aware Design:** Metamodel-space algebra reduces low-precision arithmetic demands.
- **Neural-Network Integration:** First application of NN-based quantum molecular dynamics at exascale.

## Contributions

- DC-MESH multiphysics algorithm for light-matter dynamics
- Hardware-heterogeneity-aware algorithm design principles
- Metamodel-space algebra for communication reduction
- Excited-state neural-network quantum molecular dynamics (XS-NNQMD)
- Unprecedented exascale performance demonstration

## Hardware Target

- Aurora supercomputer (60,000 GPUs, Argonne Leadership Computing Facility)
- Heterogeneous GPU clusters
- Exascale computing systems

## Workloads

- Light-induced switching of topological superlattices
- Quantum molecular dynamics on ferroelectrics (PbTiO3)
- Multiscale materials simulations
- Long-timescale quantum dynamics

## Key Results

- **Performance:** 1.87 EFLOP/s achieved on 60,000 GPUs
- **DC-MESH Speedup:** 152x faster than SOTA for 15.4M-electron system
- **XS-NNQMD Speedup:** 3780x faster than SOTA for 1.23T-atom system
- **First Application:** Simulated light-induced topological superlattice switching (topotronics)

## Limitations & Questions

- Aurora-specific optimizations; generalizability to other exascale systems unclear
- Single material (PbTiO3) case study limits scope
- Accuracy vs. low-precision trade-offs not quantitatively analyzed
- Communication reduction mechanisms and metamodel algebra details missing
- Scalability beyond 60,000 GPUs not explored
- Applicability to other quantum materials and phenomena unexplored
