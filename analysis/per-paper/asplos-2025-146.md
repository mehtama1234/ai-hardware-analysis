# asplos-2025-146: Using Analytical Performance/Power Model and Fine-Grained DVFS to Enhance AI Accelerator Energy Efficiency

**Venue:** ASPLOS  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
DVFS on AI processors is applied at coarse granularity (entire program or sub-phases) because control latency exceeds individual operator duration, leaving per-operator energy optimization unexploited.

## Motivation
Deep learning is driving rapidly increasing AI processor energy consumption, making fine-grained power optimization critical.

## Method
Most voltage/frequency scaling happens at the program level because the switching overhead exceeds individual operation duration. But operations have different needs: memory-bound operations (waiting for data) and compute-bound operations (needing raw speed) benefit from different settings. The paper uses an analytical model to predict each operation's type, plus a hardware mechanism to reduce switching overhead, enabling per-operation voltage/frequency scaling: memory-bound operations run slower (saving energy during waits) and compute-bound operations run at full speed.

## Key Novelty
An analytical model predicts the energy-speed trade-off for each AI operation (how much slower it gets if you run at lower voltage/frequency), enabling per-operation voltage/frequency scaling without hardware profiling.

## Contributions
- Analytical performance/power model for per-operator compute/memory boundedness characterization
- Fine-grained DVFS control at AI-operator granularity overcoming latency constraints
- Energy efficiency improvements across deep learning workloads
