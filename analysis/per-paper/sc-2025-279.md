# Modeling the Carbon Footprint of HPC: The Top 500 and EasyC

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Varsha Rao, Andrew A. Chien

## Problem & Motivation

The HPC community lacks standardized, accurate carbon footprint accounting despite significant environmental impact. GHG protocol methodologies, while rigorous, are difficult to apply to individual HPC systems and infeasible for comparing collections of systems. This absence of transparency hinders sustainability efforts and informed decision-making about HPC infrastructure.

## Approach

**EasyC:** A simplified carbon footprint modeling tool that:

1. **Minimal Data Requirements:** Models carbon with only a few data metrics from public sources.
2. **Operational Carbon:** Estimates power consumption and operational emissions from Top500.org data.
3. **Embodied Carbon:** Models manufacturing and deployment carbon costs.
4. **Interpolation:** Extends coverage to systems with incomplete data.
5. **Projection:** Extends estimates to full Top 500 and projects through 2030.

## Key Novelty

- **Scalable Methodology:** First HPC-wide carbon accounting system with minimal data requirements.
- **Coverage Enhancement:** Achieves 98% operational and 80.8% embodied carbon coverage through intelligent interpolation.
- **Actionable Tool:** EasyC enables practical carbon reporting for HPC facilities.

## Contributions

- EasyC tool for simplified carbon footprint modeling
- Operational and embodied carbon assessment framework
- First comprehensive carbon accounting of Top 500 HPC systems
- Data scarcity mitigation through interpolation methodology
- Carbon projections enabling future planning

## Key Findings

- **Operational Carbon:** 1.4 million MT CO2e/year for Top 500
- **Embodied Carbon:** 1.9 million MT CO2e for Top 500
- **Coverage:** 391/500 systems (operational), 283/500 systems (embodied)
- **Enhanced Coverage:** 98% operational, 80.8% embodied via interpolation
- **Trend:** Projected carbon increase through 2030

## Limitations & Questions

- Data completeness varies significantly; interpolation assumptions not detailed
- Embodied carbon methodology less complete than operational
- Specific carbon profiles of individual systems not provided
- Uncertainty bounds and confidence intervals missing
- Projection methodology and underlying assumptions not specified
- Sensitivity to data quality and estimation errors unclear
