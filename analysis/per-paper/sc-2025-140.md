# The First Star-by-star N-body/Hydrodynamics Simulation of Our Galaxy Coupling with a Surrogate Model

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Keiya Hirashima, Michiko S. Fujii, Takayuki R. Saitoh, Naoto Harada, Kentaro Nomura, Kohji Yoshikawa, Yutaka Hirai, Tetsuro Asano

## Problem
Resolving individual stars in Milky Way simulations requires hundreds of billions of particles. However, short-timescale phenomena (supernova explosions) impose tiny timestep requirements, creating severe computational bottleneck and limiting scalability to ~1 billion particles.

## Motivation
Star-by-star galaxy simulations enable unprecedented detail in studying stellar populations, star formation, and galactic structure. Previous simulations capped at ~1 billion particles due to supernova-imposed timestep constraints. Integration of machine learning surrogates can bypass short-timescale bottlenecks while preserving physical fidelity at larger scales.

## Method
The challenge is that simulating hundreds of billions of individual stars requires extremely small computational timesteps when supernovae (stellar explosions) occur, which makes the simulation run very slowly. To overcome this, the method pairs a physics simulator with a machine learning surrogate model—essentially a neural network trained to predict the effects of supernova explosions without running the full physics calculation. Instead of simulating every supernova event in detail, the surrogate model predicts how each explosion affects nearby stars on short timescales, letting the main simulation use much larger timesteps. This avoids the bottleneck of computing short-timescale physics explicitly, making it possible to simulate the full galaxy of hundreds of billions of stars.

## Key Novelty
A machine-learning surrogate model coupled to the physics simulator predicts supernova effects instead of computing them explicitly, enabling the first star-by-star simulation of the Milky Way (300 billion particles), up from the previous computational limit of 1 billion.
