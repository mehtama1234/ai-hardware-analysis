# The First Star-by-star N-body/Hydrodynamics Simulation of Our Galaxy Coupling with a Surrogate Model

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Keiya Hirashima, Michiko S. Fujii, Takayuki R. Saitoh, Naoto Harada, Kentaro Nomura, Kohji Yoshikawa, Yutaka Hirai, Tetsuro Asano

## Problem
Resolving individual stars in Milky Way simulations requires hundreds of billions of particles. However, short-timescale phenomena (supernova explosions) impose tiny timestep requirements, creating severe computational bottleneck and limiting scalability to ~1 billion particles.

## Motivation
Star-by-star galaxy simulations enable unprecedented detail in studying stellar populations, star formation, and galactic structure. Previous simulations capped at ~1 billion particles due to supernova-imposed timestep constraints. Integration of machine learning surrogates can bypass short-timescale bottlenecks while preserving physical fidelity at larger scales.

## Method
Novel hybrid N-body/hydrodynamics solver integrating physics simulation with machine learning:

1. **Physics Engine:** Standard N-body/hydrodynamics equations for galaxy-scale evolution

2. **ML Surrogate:** Trained to predict effects of supernova explosions on short timescales without explicit computation

3. **Adaptive Timestep:** ML surrogate enables larger timesteps by abstracting short-timescale physics

4. **Scalability:** Removes supernova-imposed bottleneck, enabling massive particle counts

## Key Novelty
First simulation of Milky Way Galaxy at star-by-star resolution (300 billion particles). Physics-ML integration overcomes multi-scale challenge that previously limited simulations to 1 billion particles.

## Contributions
1. 300 billion particle N-body/hydrodynamics simulation of Milky Way Galaxy
2. Novel physics-ML hybrid integration scheme for multi-scale phenomena
3. Demonstrated scalability to 148,900 nodes and 7.1 million CPU cores
4. Breakthrough surpassing billion-particle barrier in computational astrophysics
