# isscc-2025-133: Wideband Full-Duplex Receiver with Allpass-Filter SIC

**Problem:** Full-duplex (FD) receiver self-interference cancellation (SIC) techniques face a tradeoff between delay-bandwidth product (DBW), noise figure, and power consumption; most existing cancellers achieve <80MHz cancellation bandwidth with >30dB depth, while frequency-domain equalization-based approaches require power-hungry high-Q filters with noise degradation.

**Motivation:** Full-duplex systems double spectrum efficiency; however, simultaneous TX-RX self-interference cancellation over wide signal bandwidth (toward 320MHz for Wi-Fi 7) is essential to enable next-generation wireless standards.

**Method:** The receiver uses cascaded allpass filters (circuits that pass all frequencies but shift their phase response differently)—starting with a low-noise first-order filter in the RF canceller stage for flat frequency response, followed by a second-order filter using delay-peaking (a phase adjustment technique) to extend the cancellation bandwidth. These stages are combined with multi-domain Hilbert-transform-equalization, which applies phase-shifting operations across multiple frequency domains to cancel self-interference—the unwanted signal from the transmitter leaking into the receiver. The system achieves 65 decibels of self-interference cancellation across 120 megahertz of bandwidth, with the RF delay varying by less than 10%.

**Key Novelty:** Multi-stage allpass filter architecture with Hilbert-transform equalization enabling wideband self-interference cancellation with superior noise and power efficiency.

**Contributions:**
- Compact full-duplex receiver with wideband self-interference cancellation
- 65dB SIC achieved across 120MHz modulated signal
- Multi-stage APF-based Hilbert-transform equalization
- Low-noise (6dB NF) RF cancellation with wide delay range support
