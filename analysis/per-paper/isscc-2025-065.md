# isscc-2025-065

**Title:** 15.4 A Neuroprosthetic SoC with Sensory Feedback Featuring Frequency-Splitting-Based Wireless Power Transfer with 200Mb/s 0.67pJ/b Backscatter Data Uplink and Unsupervised Multi-Class Spike Sorting

**Venue:** ISSCC

**Problem:** High-channel neural implants lack closed-loop sensory feedback due to latency and complexity; electrical stimulation requires artifact rejection to prevent feedback loops.

**Key Contribution:** Frequency-splitting WPT + backscatter uplink enable simultaneous power and 200 Mb/s data transmission with on-chip spike sorting.

**Method:** The system uses different radio frequencies to simultaneously deliver power and transmit data wirelessly without interfering with each other (called frequency-splitting). The implant sends neural signals back using backscatter—reflecting external radio waves with minimal power—at 200 megabits per second using just 0.67 trillionths of a joule per bit. A processor inside the implant automatically identifies and groups neuron firing patterns (spike sorting) without needing training data, so it only transmits categorized signals instead of raw data. A circuit filters out electrical noise from stimulation pulses during simultaneous recording, preventing false feedback loops.

**Hardware:** ASIC, SoC

**Metrics:** 200 Mb/s, 0.67 pJ/b

**Techniques:** Circuit design, wireless power, signal processing

**Primary Theme:** Implantable prosthetic SoC with closed-loop feedback
