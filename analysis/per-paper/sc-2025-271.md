# DPAR: High-Performance, Secure, and Scalable Differential Privacy-based AllReduce

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Hao Qi, Weicong Chen, Chenghong Wang, Xiaoyi Lu

## Problem & Motivation

AllReduce is a fundamental collective communication pattern in distributed HPC and AI systems, but privacy remains a critical concern. Current state-of-the-art (SOTA) solutions using Homomorphic Encryption (HE) introduce significant overhead, require secure key exchanges, and are vulnerable to collusion attacks. This limits the deployment of privacy-preserving aggregation at scale.

## Approach

DPAR proposes a differential privacy-based AllReduce framework with three key innovations:

1. **Differential Privacy Integration:** Eliminates collusion risks without requiring secure key exchanges, addressing fundamental vulnerabilities in HE approaches.
2. **Scalable Noise Growth:** Mechanisms to preserve aggregation accuracy across distributed settings with growing noise.
3. **Noise Pooling Optimization:** Performance optimization that enables strong privacy guarantees with minimal overhead.

The framework is designed as an MPI AllReduce drop-in replacement for seamless integration into existing HPC/AI applications.

## Key Novelty

- **First DP-based AllReduce:** Combines differential privacy with collusion-resistance without key exchange requirements.
- **Noise Pooling:** Novel optimization strategy reducing the performance cost of adding privacy noise.
- **Production-ready:** MPI compatibility enables direct deployment in existing infrastructure.

## Contributions

- Novel differentially private, collusion-resistant AllReduce framework
- Scalable noise growth algorithm maintaining accuracy in large-scale aggregation
- Noise pooling mechanism minimizing privacy overhead
- MPI-compatible implementation enabling broad adoption

## Hardware Target

- HPC supercomputers (Delta, Frontier)
- Multi-core CPU clusters
- Distributed systems up to 8192 cores

## Workloads

- Distributed AI/ML training with privacy constraints
- Federated learning scenarios
- Large-scale scientific computing with sensitive data

## Results

- **Performance:** 34.7% faster than SOTA HE solution on modern AI workloads
- **Scale:** Evaluated on up to 8192 cores
- **Key Metric:** Strong privacy with minimal performance cost

## Limitations & Questions

- Privacy-accuracy trade-off parameters (epsilon, delta) not specified in abstract
- Noise growth behavior and scalability to larger systems unclear
- Evaluation limited to AI workloads on specific supercomputers
- Overhead breakdown between noise generation, pooling, and communication not detailed
