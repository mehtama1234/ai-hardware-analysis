# S-SYNC: Shuttle and Swap Co-Optimization in Quantum Charge-Coupled Devices

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
In Quantum Charge-Coupled Device (QCCD) trapped-ion architectures, coherent qubit shuttling across segmented electrode arrays increases thermal motion and consistently forces additional SWAP operations to restore qubit connectivity, significantly extending circuit execution time and degrading application success rates.

## Motivation
QCCD processors offer long coherence times and high gate fidelity, but the dominant cost of shuttling-induced SWAPs makes naive compilation inadequate, blocking practical large-scale algorithm execution.

## Method
S-SYNC is a compiler that simultaneously optimizes two competing goals: reducing qubit shuttles (moving qubits across a segmented electrode array) and reducing SWAP gates (quantum operations that exchange qubit positions). Normally designs shuttle qubits first and then add SWAPs to fix connectivity problems; instead, S-SYNC treats both as equally important choices from the start. It applies optimization rules tailored to the specific QCCD chip topology, weighing the thermal cost of shuttling (heat that harms qubits) against the time cost of extra SWAPs. The compiler is tested on different QCCD topologies and initial qubit configurations.

## Key Novelty
By treating shuttle and SWAP operations as equally important, jointly-optimized decisions (rather than choosing shuttles first then SWAPs sequentially), S-SYNC reduces shuttle counts by 3.69x on average.

## Contributions
- S-SYNC compiler co-optimizing shuttle and SWAP operations together for QCCD architectures.
- Static topology formulation of QCCD routing that enables joint scheduling heuristics for shuttles and SWAPs.
- 3.69x average reduction in shuttling operations and 1.73x average improvement in application success rate.
- Cross-topology analysis and comparison of initial mapping methods for various QCCD designs.
