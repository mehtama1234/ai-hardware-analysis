# isca-2025-063 — Reinforcement Learning-Guided Graph State Generation in Photonic Quantum Computers

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
In photonic quantum computers (PQCs), finding optimal generation sequences for graph states requires jointly minimizing generation time, emitter count, and CZ gate count, but existing frameworks either face exponential search complexity or produce non-optimal results due to non-deterministic properties.

## Motivation
Graph state generation quality directly determines photonic quantum computation fidelity; suboptimal generation sequences waste scarce quantum emitter resources and increase CZ gate count, both of which degrade output fidelity in near-term PQC hardware.

## Method
RLGS uses reinforcement learning (an AI approach where an algorithm learns through trial and error) to optimize how graph states (entangled collections of photons) are generated in photonic quantum computers. It creates a structured "map" of the quantum circuit that organizes two-qubit gates (operations on pairs of quantum bits) and their dependencies (which operations must happen first), allowing the RL agent to search for generation sequences that reduce generation time, emitter count, and CZ gate insertions simultaneously. The system balances competing constraints: minimizing the sequential depth of gates while also reducing crosstalk (unwanted interference between qubits).

## Key Novelty
RLGS restructures the quantum circuit into a "map" that the RL agent can navigate, converting what would otherwise be an exponentially difficult search over gate dependencies into a tractable optimization problem that simultaneously minimizes generation time, emitter count, and CZ gate insertions.

## Contributions
1. A map-based framework abstracting quantum circuit dependencies for efficient qubit reuse optimization.
2. An RL-guided search strategy jointly minimizing generation time, emitter count, and CZ gate count.
3. Generation time reductions of 31.1%, 49.6%, 57.5% for small/medium/large graph states vs. state-of-the-art.
4. Emitter count reductions of 13.9-17.5%; CZ gate reductions of 37.7-57.8%.
