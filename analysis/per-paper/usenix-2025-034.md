# Chitu: Avoiding Unnecessary Fallback in Byzantine Consensus

**Problem:** Byzantine consensus protocols often invoke fallback mechanisms (slower modes or restarts) unnecessarily, reducing throughput and increasing latency. Chitu addresses detection and optimization of when fallback is truly required versus when the protocol should continue in normal mode.

**Method:** Chitu improves Byzantine consensus by distinguishing between temporary network delays and actual Byzantine faults (faulty or malicious nodes), preventing unnecessary fallback to slower restart modes. It uses three techniques to make this distinction: better leader election signaling (how leader nodes report their status to others), timeout adjustment (tuning how long to wait before assuming failure), and view-change optimization (streamlining how the protocol switches between consensus rounds). By staying in fast normal mode when only transient delays occur, Chitu maintains high throughput and low latency while preserving safety and liveness.

**Novelty:** Chitu avoids unnecessary fallback in Byzantine consensus by accurately detecting whether network problems are temporary or represent genuine Byzantine faults (faulty or malicious nodes), thereby reducing expensive mode switches while maintaining safety and liveness guarantees.
