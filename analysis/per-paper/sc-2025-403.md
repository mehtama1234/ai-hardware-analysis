# Optimizing Quantum Circuit Mapping to Reduce Inter-Module Communications in Distributed Architectures

**Venue:** SC
**ID:** sc-2025-403
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Modular quantum architectures require mapping circuits to distributed modules; non-local operations incur significant communication overhead limiting scalability.

## Motivation
Quantum computing requires modular architectures for scalability; optimizing circuit mapping to minimize inter-module communication is critical.

## Method
DQTetris works in stages to assign qubits to separate quantum modules, starting with a global pass that tries to arrange qubits so that operations stay within a single module and avoid costly messages between modules. It then prunes (removes) unnecessary gates layer-by-layer within each subcircuit. For operations that must cross module boundaries, it uses adaptive teleportation—selectively moving qubits between modules based on the circuit structure—to minimize the expensive inter-module communication that would otherwise slow execution.

## Key Novelty
The core innovation is a smart strategy for splitting circuits across modules and adaptively deciding when to teleport qubits, reducing inter-module communication overhead by 28-75% compared to existing methods.

## Contributions
1. Hierarchical global and local qubit mapping framework
2. Layer-wise gate pruning for subcircuit isolation
3. Adaptive gate teleportation to minimize reassignment events
4. 28-75% communication cost reduction vs. existing methods
