# ARTEMIS: Agile Discovery of Efficient Real-Time Systems-on-Chips in the Heterogeneous Era

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Design space exploration (DSE) for heterogeneous real-time SoCs (targeting applications like autonomous driving and AR/VR) must jointly optimize microarchitectural parameters and dynamic task-to-hardware mappings under deadline, power, and area constraints, resulting in an intractably large combined design space.

## Motivation
Domain-specific SoC design for real-time applications is increasingly important but existing DSE methods operate at the microarchitectural level, missing system-level interactions and requiring prohibitive simulation resources for large designs.

## Method
ARTEMIS integrates a dynamic SoC-level scheduler into the DSE loop to eliminate the mapping dimension from the search space, dramatically pruning the effective design space. The scheduler incorporates policies such as task procrastination and memory-traffic/energy-aware task assignment. DSE heuristics use real-time deadline-aware and power/area-aware ranking mechanisms to guide the remaining architecture search. The method is validated on FPGA hardware and evaluated on autonomous vehicle and AR/VR application suites.

## Key Novelty
Integrating a dynamic SoC scheduler directly into the DSE loop to collapse the task-mapping dimension, enabling 5.1-12.8x faster convergence while discovering SoCs that meet 100% real-time deadlines.

## Contributions
- ARTEMIS DSE methodology with embedded dynamic scheduler eliminating the mapping dimension
- Scheduling policies including task procrastination and memory-traffic/energy awareness
- Real-time deadline and power/area-aware DSE ranking heuristics
- 5.1-12.8x DSE convergence speedup; SoCs with 100% deadline satisfaction and 1.2-3x better throughput or up to 2.4x lower area vs prior DSE
