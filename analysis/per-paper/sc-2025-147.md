# Modeling and Optimizing Real-Time Telescope Interaction for Multi-wavelength Observation of Gamma-ray Bursts

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Ye Htet, Marion Sudvarg, Honghao Yang, Jeremy Buhler, Roger D. Chamberlain, James H. Buckley

## Problem
Multi-wavelength gamma-ray burst (GRB) observation requires real-time coordination between telescopes. Gamma-ray detector (ADAPT) localizes GRB in sky, then must rapidly communicate precise location to optical telescope to direct observation. Time-to-discovery is critical science parameter affecting observational yield and data quality.

## Motivation
GRBs are transient astrophysical phenomena lasting seconds to minutes. Early optical observations capture critical emission phases unavailable through gamma-ray data alone. ADAPT localizes GRBs on timescales shorter than burst duration, enabling multi-wavelength coordination. Optimization of communication and observation strategy is critical for scientific return.

## Method
Modeling and optimization framework for progressive localization:

1. **Progressive Localization:** Compute series of increasingly accurate GRB position estimates during burst event

2. **Timing Optimization:** Framework determines when ADAPT should transmit location updates to minimize optical telescope discovery time

3. **Partner Coordination:** Optical telescope uses progressive estimates to rapidly acquire target

4. **Strategy Design:** Generate adaptive strategies balancing localization certainty vs. discovery speed

## Key Novelty
First optimization framework for real-time multi-telescope coordination, achieving substantial reduction in target discovery time through progressive strategy.

## Contributions
1. Modeling framework for real-time gamma-ray and optical telescope coordination
2. Progressive localization optimization minimizing discovery time
3. 42% faster discovery compared to single-alert baseline
4. Extensible framework for other multi-instrument observations
