# On the Compressibility of Floating-Point Data in Posit and IEEE-754 Representation

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Andrew Rodriguez, Martin Burtscher

## Problem Statement
While posit format has been proposed as a superior alternative to IEEE 754 floating-point with better accuracy properties, its compressibility characteristics remain unexplored. This gap limits understanding of posit's practical benefits for storage-intensive scientific computing workflows.

## Technical Approach
**Experimental Methodology:**
- Encode 14 single-precision datasets from SDR-Bench suite in both IEEE 754 and posit formats
- Evaluate 5 general-purpose lossless compressors (bzip2, XZ, etc.)
- Synthesize additional compression algorithms using LC framework
- Compare compression ratios across encodings and algorithms

**Dataset Selection:**
- SDR-Bench suite provides standardized scientific computing datasets
- Coverage spans multiple scientific domains

## Key Results
- **4 of 6 compressors** show 2.59% average compression ratio reduction with posit
- **bzip2** shows 1.74% compression ratio increase with posit
- **XZ** provides highest compression ratios for both encodings
- **Posit encoding effects:** Variable across compression algorithms

## Implications
- Posit format may not uniformly improve compression across all algorithms
- Algorithm-format interaction matters; no universal winner
- Trade-offs between accuracy and compression efficiency remain complex

## Limitations & Future Work
- Single-precision only; double-precision evaluation needed
- Limited dataset coverage (14 datasets); broader evaluation necessary
- No compression throughput/performance metrics
- Posit ecosystem still emerging; practical adoption not yet widespread
