# Understanding and Mitigating Covert Channel and Side Channel Vulnerabilities Introduced by RowHammer Defenses

**Venue:** MICRO  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Modern RowHammer defenses (PRAC, RFM) introduce timing covert and side channels because their preventive refresh actions block DRAM access in ways that depend on application memory access patterns, allowing adversaries to both observe victim access behavior and establish covert communication channels. No prior work has analyzed these defense-induced timing vulnerabilities.

## Motivation
Widely-deployed RowHammer mitigations in DDR5 (PRAC) and DDR4 (RFM) are assumed to be purely defensive, but their observable bandwidth-reduction side effects create a new attack surface that undermines the security guarantees of the defenses themselves.

## Method
LeakyHammer exploits RowHammer defenses (PRAC and RFM) by measuring the timing delays that occur when their protective memory refreshes block DRAM access. A spy process observes these timing delays to steal victim behavior information—a weakness called a side channel—or to send secret messages by modulating the patterns—a covert channel. The attack demonstrates this by fingerprinting websites: when a victim browses, the timing patterns created by defense refreshes reveal which sites they visited. Three countermeasures are evaluated: randomizing refresh timing, hiding memory access patterns, and injecting timing noise to obscure the real delays.

## Key Novelty
RowHammer defense mechanisms (PRAC and RFM) become vulnerabilities themselves: their timing delays leak both what victims access (side channel) and enable secret communication (covert channel).

## Contributions
- First analysis of timing covert and side channel vulnerabilities introduced by PRAC and RFM RowHammer defenses
- LeakyHammer covert channel achieving 39.0 Kbps (PRAC) and 48.7 Kbps (RFM) channel capacity
- Proof-of-concept website fingerprinting side channel exploiting RowHammer-preventive actions
- Evaluation of three countermeasures showing fundamental elimination of LeakyHammer incurs large performance overhead
