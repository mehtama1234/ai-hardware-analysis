# SiST: Token Similarity and Sparsity Aware Optimization for Transformers on FPGA

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**Paper ID:** iccad-2025-260

## Problem Statement
Transformer inference on resource-constrained FPGAs is expensive due to high computational cost in QKV generation, attention, and FFN modules.

## Motivation
Prior FPGA work focuses on attention computation, but QKV generation and FFN typically dominate overall cost; sparsity and token patterns offer optimization opportunities.

## Method
SiST cuts unnecessary work in three places: it predicts which tokens can be skipped before generating QKV matrices (the Query, Key, and Value tensors used by attention), groups similar tokens in the FFN (the feed-forward layer) to share computations, and exploits weight sparsity by skipping calculations on near-zero weights. The FPGA is designed to handle this unstructured sparsity—where zeros are scattered randomly rather than in regular patterns—efficiently.

## Key Novelty
The core innovation is co-designing the transformer algorithm and FPGA hardware together as a single system to exploit both token similarity and weight sparsity across all three transformer modules (QKV generation, attention, and FFN).

## Contributions

1. 45.9–52.2% computational overhead reduction
2. 2.35–8.44× speedup
3. 1.50–14.09× throughput improvement
4. 2.43–3.44× energy efficiency gain
