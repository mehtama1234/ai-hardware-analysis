# hpca-2025-016 — Veritas: Demystifying Silent Data Corruptions — uArch-Level Modeling and Fleet Data of Modern x86 CPUs

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
SDCs caused by silicon defects in arithmetic units of modern x86 CPUs are poorly characterized at the microarchitecture level; no public data exists on SDC incident rates across CPU microarchitectures or workloads at fleet scale.

## Motivation
Hyperscalers report ~1-in-1000 defective CPU chips leading to SDCs; without uArch-level fault models for arithmetic units, hardware and software protection mechanisms cannot be effectively targeted.

## Method
They used gem5 (a computer simulator) to deliberately inject faults into arithmetic units (integer and floating-point components) of five modern x86 processor designs, measuring how often these faults cause silent data corruptions—wrong results without crashes. The simulation tested different instruction classes and arithmetic unit types. They combined these results with real defect measurements from large-scale datacenters to estimate how often silent data corruptions actually occur in deployed systems.

## Key Novelty
This is the first detailed study of silent data corruptions in arithmetic units (the math-doing parts, not memory) across five x86 processor designs, using real defect data from datacenters to estimate actual occurrence rates.

## Contributions
- Systematic uArch-level fault modeling of integer and FP arithmetic units in modern x86 CPUs.
- SDC probability and rate characterization across five microarchitectures and instruction classes.
- Integration of real-world datacenter defect rates with gem5 simulation for fleet SDC rate estimation.
- Actionable insights for targeting fault protection at highest-risk arithmetic units and workloads.
