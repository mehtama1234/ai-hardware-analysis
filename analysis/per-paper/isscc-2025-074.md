# isscc-2025-074

**Title:** A 28nm 18.1μJ /Acquisition End-to-End GPS Acquisition Accelerator with Energy-Accuracy-Driven Mixed-Radix IFFT and ROM-Assisted Computing

**Venue:** ISSCC

**Problem:** GPS acquisition is most energy-consuming GNSS receiver task; conventional full-precision IFFT per satellite wastes energy on low-precision early stages.

**Key Contribution:** Mixed-radix IFFT with dynamic precision scaling + ROM-assisted computing reduces GPS acquisition energy while maintaining detection accuracy.

**Method:** The accelerator reduces energy by using different precision levels at different stages of GPS acquisition. In early stages—Doppler and code-delay estimation, where you're narrowing down which satellite and at what timing the signal is—lower precision is acceptable. The chip skips expensive calculations by using ROM-assisted computing: precomputed lookup tables stored in read-only memory. It combines a dual numerically controlled oscillator (NCO, which generates reference signals), a mixed-radix FFT engine (a signal transformer that breaks down complex calculations), and a correlator (which matches received signals to references), all built at 28nm in CMOS technology with adaptive word-widths.

**Hardware:** ASIC (28nm)

**Metrics:** 18.1 μJ per acquisition

**Workloads:** Signal processing (GPS)

**Techniques:** Circuit design, kernel fusion, approximation

**Primary Theme:** Energy-optimized GPS acquisition accelerator
