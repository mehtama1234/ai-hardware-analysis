# Real-Time Bayesian Inference at Extreme Scale: A Digital Twin for Tsunami Early Warning Applied to the Cascadia Subduction Zone

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Stefan Henneking, Sreeram Venkat, Veselin Dobrev, John Camier, Tzanio V. Kolev, Milinda Fernando, Alice-Agnes Gabriel, Omar Ghattas

## Problem
Tsunami early warning requires real-time inference of earthquake-induced seafloor motion from acoustic sensor data with billions of parameters. Traditional Bayesian inversion approaches are computationally infeasible at required timescales.

## Motivation
The Cascadia subduction zone presents significant tsunami risk to the Pacific Northwest. Timely coastal warnings are critical for public safety. Standard Bayesian posterior computation for this problem would require 50 years of computation on 512 GPUs, far exceeding real-time requirements (seconds). The shift-invariant structure of the parameter-to-observable map enables novel algorithmic acceleration.

## Method
The system uses a digital twin—a computer model that mimics real behavior—to figure out what happened on the seafloor by running Bayesian inversion (mathematically working backward from sensor measurements to find the cause). To make this real-time feasible with a billion parameters, the method exploits shift-invariance: the physics equations are identical across all seafloor locations, so the expensive calculations happen once (offline) and are reused for fast real-time answers (online). The offline phase uses MFEM—software for solving wave equations—scaled to 43,520 GPUs, while the online phase runs parallel algorithms to deliver results instantly.

## Key Novelty
Shift-invariance decomposition—reusing physics calculations that are identical across the seafloor—enables real-time Bayesian inference on billion-parameter problems, achieving a ten-billion-fold speedup with 92% parallel efficiency on the El Capitan supercomputer.

## Contributions
1. Offline-online decomposition strategy leveraging shift-invariance in inverse problems
2. Scalable adjoint wave propagation algorithm achieving 92% weak efficiency on 43,520 GPUs
3. Real-time online component solving inverse problem and forecasting in 0.2 seconds
4. End-to-end uncertainty-quantified digital twin for operational tsunami early warning
