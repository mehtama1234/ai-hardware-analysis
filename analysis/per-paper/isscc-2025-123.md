# isscc-2025-123: 65nm Uncertainty-Quantifiable Ventricular Arrhythmia Detection Engine

**Problem:** Deep-learning based ventricular arrhythmia detection for implantable cardioverter defibrillators (ICDs) lacks transparency in uncertainty quantification (UQ), limiting clinical reliability; Bayesian neural networks address this but incur >6x energy overhead from Gaussian random number generation and multiple sampling iterations.

**Motivation:** Sudden cardiac death prevention via timely VA detection requires trustworthy ML inference that quantifies confidence; BNNs enable uncertainty awareness but excessive energy demands are impractical for battery-powered ICDs.

**Method:** The chip detects irregular heartbeats using a Bayesian neural network—a type of AI that measures confidence in its predictions. Normally this incurs huge power overhead from generating random numbers and running multiple iterations. This 65nm accelerator uses three techniques: analog circuits to generate random numbers in parallel (simpler than digital), in-memory analog computation that does math where data already sits (avoiding expensive data movement), and partial sampling to reduce iterations. Together, these cut the energy overhead from over 6× to practical levels for battery-powered implants.

**Key Novelty:** Fully parallel analog GRNG and in-memory vector-matrix operations that reduce Bayesian CNN energy consumption to practical levels for battery-powered medical devices.

**Contributions:**
- Bayesian CNN accelerator for VA detection with uncertainty quantification
- Parallel analog Gaussian random number generation for low-energy sampling
- In-memory vector-matrix operations reducing energy per inference to 1.75µJ
- Robustness to out-of-distribution data, hardware imperfections, and temperature variations
