# Panacea: Novel DNN Accelerator using Accuracy-Preserving Asymmetric Quantization and Energy-Saving Bit-Slice Sparsity

**Venue:** HPCA 2025  
**Authors:** Dongyun Kam, Myeongji Yun, Sunwoo Yoo, Seungwoo Hong, Zhengya Zhang, Youngjoo Lee

## Problem
Symmetric quantization introduces unacceptable accuracy loss for large-scale DNNs due to asymmetric activation distributions, but asymmetric quantization produces nonzero-heavy bit-slices that cannot be exploited by existing bit-slice GEMM accelerators designed for zero-slice skipping.

## Motivation
Bit-slice sparsity acceleration requires skippable zero slices, yet the nonzero-heavy output of asymmetric quantization wastes this opportunity, leaving a gap between accuracy and hardware efficiency for large DNN deployments.

## Method
AQS-GEMM compresses frequent nonzero slices generated by asymmetric quantization and skips their computation. Zero-point manipulation shifts frequent activation values toward zero to increase slice-level sparsity. Distribution-based bit-slicing selects bit-slice subsets that maximize sparsity based on activation distributions. Panacea's dataflow handles sparse/dense mixed tiled AQS-GEMM with run-length encoding to maximize data reuse and minimize off-chip memory traffic.

## Key Novelty
AQS-GEMM: the first bit-slice GEMM formulation that compresses and skips nonzero slices from asymmetric quantization, rather than only exploiting symmetric zero slices.

## Contributions
- AQS-GEMM: asymmetrically-quantized bit-slice GEMM skipping compressed frequent nonzero slices
- Zero-point manipulation to maximize activation slice-level sparsity
- Distribution-based bit-slicing for sparsity-maximizing bit-slice selection
- Panacea accelerator: 1.97x energy and 1.88x throughput over Sibia on OPT-2.7B

## Results
- **Energy:** 1.97x over Sibia; 3.26x over SIMD design on OPT-2.7B
- **Throughput:** 1.88x over Sibia; 2.41x over SIMD design on OPT-2.7B

## Hardware Target
ASIC

## Baselines
Sibia (bit-slice accelerator), SIMD design

## Limitations
Accuracy impact of zero-point manipulation and distribution-based bit-slicing beyond OPT-2.7B is not fully characterized in the abstract.

**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)
