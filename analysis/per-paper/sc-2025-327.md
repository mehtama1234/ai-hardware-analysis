# Towards Supporting QIR: Steps for Adopting the Quantum Intermediate Representation

**Venue:** SC
**ID:** sc-2025-327
**Confidence:** low

## Problem Statement
Quantum computing intermediate representations (IRs) are critical for optimization, but adoption pathways are unclear; converting existing simulators to support QIR requires significant effort.

## Motivation
QIR (built on LLVM) offers standardization benefits; understanding adoption steps reduces barriers to broader quantum simulator support of QIR and classical control flow.

## Method
The research team converted an existing quantum circuit simulator (a program that mimics quantum computer behavior) to use QIR—a standardized format for describing quantum operations. They tested several different adoption strategies and measured whether the new QIR-based version ran as fast as the original. They also demonstrated that using QIR enables classical control flow, meaning you can write regular programming logic (like if-statements or loops) mixed together with quantum instructions.

## Key Novelty
The paper demonstrates that quantum simulators can adopt QIR format without any performance loss while gaining the ability to mix regular programming logic with quantum operations (classical control flow).

## Contributions
1. Step-by-step guide for QIR runtime adoption
2. Quantum circuit simulator with QIR support
3. Performance equivalence with original simulator
4. Classical control flow support
