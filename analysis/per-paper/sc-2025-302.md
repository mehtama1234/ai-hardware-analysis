# SC-2025-302: Exploring Reduced Precision for Deep Learning Activation Functions

## Summary
This work systematically explores the viability of ultra-low precision representations (FP2, FP3, FP4, FP6, FP8) for activation functions in deep neural networks. Through comprehensive PyTorch experiments across multiple models and datasets, the authors characterize accuracy degradation and identify FP4 as the practical lower bound for most activation functions, with piecewise-linear activations (ReLU) maintaining acceptable accuracy even at FP2.

## Key Contributions
1. **PyTorch framework** for emulating and training models with reduced-precision activation functions
2. **Empirical characterization** showing FP4 as practical precision bound for activations
3. **Activation-type differentiation**: piecewise-linear activations (ReLU variants) tolerate FP2-FP4 much better than smooth nonlinearities (Sigmoid, Tanh)
4. **FP6-FP8 equivalence** to FP32 across all tested configurations

## Technical Approach
- Training methodology with reduced-precision activation function emulation
- Evaluation across diverse models (CNNs, RNNs, transformers) and datasets
- Precision sensitivity analysis for different activation function families
- Model complexity impact on accuracy preservation

## Hardware & Systems
- GPU architectures with native FP8/FP16 support
- Future tensor accelerators with FP4/FP6 hardware support
- Energy-constrained inference devices

## Workloads & Evaluation
- **Scope**: Multiple models and datasets (specifics from abstract: classification domains)
- **Key finding**: ReLU variants maintain 40-83% accuracy at FP2 depending on model complexity
- **Performance baseline**: FP32 full precision

## Limitations & Future Work
- Limited discussion of inference performance and hardware deployment specifics
- Smooth nonlinearities (Sigmoid, Tanh) show significant degradation; alternatives not explored
- Hardware-specific quantization and rounding modes not addressed
- Applicability to other precision formats (INT4, mixed-precision) not examined
