# Guaranteed Yet Hard to Find: Uncovering FPGA Routing Convergence Paradox

**Problem:** PathFinder, the ubiquitous FPGA routing algorithm, fails to find guaranteed legal routing solutions in constrained problems where solutions are provably available. When circuits fail to route, it is unclear whether the root cause is the routing algorithm or the architecture.

**Method:** The authors construct constrained routing problems with limited but guaranteed-solvable routing resources to create an analyzable search space. This reduced space reveals why PathFinder fails. Through analysis, they identify that PathFinder's greedy per-connection routing approach produces inefficient route trees with excess nodes. They then propose a simple algorithm that constructs more efficient route trees by avoiding this greedy inefficiency.

**Novelty:** Identifying PathFinder's fundamental limitation: its greedy per-connection routing creates suboptimal route trees with more nodes than necessary.
