# SC-2025-308: Bine Trees: Enhancing Collective Operations by Optimizing Communication Locality

## Summary
This work introduces Bine (binomial negabinary) trees, a novel family of collective algorithms designed to optimize communication locality on large HPC systems with hierarchical network topologies. Bine trees maintain the generality of traditional algorithms while significantly reducing traffic on globally constrained links, achieving up to 5x speedups on real supercomputers.

## Key Contributions
1. **Bine tree family** for communication-locality-optimized collective operations
2. **Eight implemented Bine-based collectives** (all-reduce, broadcast, scatter, gather, etc.)
3. **Empirical results**: 5x speedups on large-scale supercomputers
4. **Network efficiency**: Up to 33% reduction in global-link traffic

## Technical Approach
- Bine tree family construction combining binomial and negabinary tree properties
- Topology-aware collective operation design for Dragonfly, Dragonfly+, fat-tree, and torus
- Communication scheduling to minimize global-link congestion
- Generalization of tree-based collective algorithms for hierarchical networks

## Hardware & Systems
- Large-scale supercomputers (Tier-1 HPC systems)
- Dragonfly and Dragonfly+ interconnects
- Oversubscribed fat-tree networks
- Torus topologies

## Workloads & Evaluation
- **Target kernels**: Collective operations (all-reduce, broadcast, scatter, gather, all-to-all)
- **Evaluation scale**: Diverse node counts on multi-thousand node systems
- **Vector sizes**: Multiple vector sizes to characterize algorithm behavior
- **Performance gain**: 5x speedup, 33% global-link traffic reduction

## Limitations & Future Work
- Evaluation limited to four network topology families
- Overhead of topology discovery and algorithm selection not detailed
- Applicability to newer topologies (Slim Fly, Megafly) not explored
- Memory footprint of topology-aware collective algorithms not discussed
- Integration with MPI communication libraries needs exploration
