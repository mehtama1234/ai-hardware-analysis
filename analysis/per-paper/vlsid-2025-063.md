# HyCMAx: Power-Efficient Hybrid CMOS-Memristor Based Approximate Dividers for Error-Resilient Applications

**Paper ID:** vlsid-2025-063
**Venue:** VLSID
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Approximate computing traditionally uses CMOS technology; exploring hybrid CMOS-memristor approaches can yield further performance and power gains.

## Motivation
Error-resilient applications (multimedia, image processing, neural networks) can tolerate approximation. Memristor technology offers potential for superior power and area efficiency when combined with CMOS.

## Method
The approach combines standard CMOS transistors with memristors (programmable resistance components), which can be more power-efficient when a circuit is allowed to make small computational errors—a strategy called approximate computing. It starts with an approximate subtractor (a circuit that performs subtraction with intentional small errors to save power), then uses this as a building block for a divider circuit (which performs division) at the 28nm manufacturing node. The hybrid CMOS-memristor topology lets the memristor handle certain operations more efficiently than CMOS alone, reducing overall power consumption.

## Key Novelty
This work is the first to combine memristors with standard CMOS transistors in an approximate divider circuit, demonstrating that memristors can successfully improve power efficiency in division operations.
