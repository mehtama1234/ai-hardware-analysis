# A FeFET-Based Compute-in-Memory Architecture on FPGA for Neural Network Inference

**Problem:** Traditional BRAM-based compute-in-memory designs suffer from low parallelism due to single-word-line activation and require additional adder trees for partial sum accumulation, which limits efficiency. The von Neumann bottleneck restricts memory-bound neural network inference on FPGAs.

**Method:** The authors propose a CIM core based on 2T1C (two-transistor, one-capacitor) structure to replace BRAM units, utilizing charge redistribution mechanisms and reusing ADC capacitors to achieve high parallelism. Single cells incorporate computational capabilities enabling dual parallelism for enhanced performance. An automated deployment and mapping tool handles DNN adaptation to different network architectures.

**Novelty:** FeFET-based 2T1C CIM core with charge redistribution and ADC capacitor reuse replacing BRAM for dual parallelism.
