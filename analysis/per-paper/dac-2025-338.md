# Synergistic Die-Level Router for Multi-FPGA System with Time-Division Multiplexing Optimization
**Venue:** DAC

## Problem
Multi-FPGA systems with multiple dies connected via TDM suffer from critical connection delays and routing congestion from super long lines (SLLs) between dies.

## Motivation
Large-scale FPGA emulation systems partition designs across multiple dies; optimizing inter-die routing and TDM ratios improves overall system frequency and reduces congestion.

## Method
The approach uses a router that operates at the die level (where each die is an individual chip component) and simultaneously optimizes two things: how signals are physically routed between components, and the timing schedule for time-division multiplexing (where signals share transmission lines by taking turns at different times). By making routing decisions that consider both the physical layout and the transmission timing together, it reduces the need for extremely long wiring connections between distant dies and shortens the longest signal delays in the circuit.

## Key Novelty
This is the first router to jointly optimize both how signals are physically routed and when they're transmitted (using time-sharing) in multi-die FPGA systems.

## Contributions
1. 7.6% improvement in critical connection delay
2. 5.76x speedup vs. state-of-the-art die-level router
3. Reduced routing congestion and SLL usage
