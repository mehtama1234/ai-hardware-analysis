# Qubit Movement-Optimized Program Generation on Zoned Neutral Atom Processors

**Venue:** CGO
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Zoned neutral atom quantum architectures segregate execution spaces for 1- and 2-qubit gates, but naively scheduled programs incur excessive intra-zone atom movement, consuming most execution time in traveling rather than computation.

## Motivation
Quantum fidelity improves with zoned architecture, but movement overhead becomes dominant; reducing atom transport is critical for practical quantum circuit execution.

## Method
The method rearranges quantum gates and assigns them to zones to reduce atom movement. It uses three strategies: fountain-shaped chains of controlled-Z gates to group related gates, a ZZ-interaction protocol that separates single- and two-qubit gate execution (preventing interleaved operations that force zone jumps), and preemptive gate scheduling (determining the full schedule before execution begins). By jointly optimizing gate order and zone placement, atoms spend less time traveling between zones.

## Key Novelty
Fountain-shaped chains of controlled-Z gates combined with a ZZ-interaction protocol (which separates single- and two-qubit gate execution) reduce zone transitions.

## Contributions
- Three compilation strategies for zoned neutral atom architectures
- 68% reduction in inter-zone movements, 35% reduction in physical gate count
- 17% circuit fidelity improvement over standard execution
