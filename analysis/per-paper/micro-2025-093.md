# micro-2025-093: Drishti: Do Not Forget Slicing While Designing Last-Level Cache Replacement Policies for Many-Core Systems

**Venue:** MICRO 2025  
**Authors:** Sweta, Prerna Priyadarshini, Biswabandan Panda  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
State-of-the-art LLC replacement policies (Hawkeye, Mockingjay) use per-slice sampled caches and reuse predictors that make myopic decisions based only on single-slice accesses, unaware of global reuse behavior; additionally, per-slice sampled sets receive too few LLC misses to drive accurate decisions in many-core sliced LLCs.

## Motivation
Commercial many-core systems universally use sliced LLCs (8-64MB, 32-64 slices for 32+ cores), but SOTA replacement policies are designed for monolithic LLCs, causing their performance advantage to shrink dramatically in practice.

## Method
Drishti introduces two enhancements: (1) a per-core global reuse predictor trained on all accesses by each core across all LLC slices (global awareness without centralized interconnect traffic), combined with per-slice local sampled caches; (2) a per-slice dynamic sampled cache that adaptively expands the sampled LLC sets to ensure adequate miss coverage per slice. Applied to augment both Hawkeye and Mockingjay on 4, 16, and 32-core systems with 8, 32, and 64 MB sliced LLCs.

## Key Novelty
Decoupling the reuse predictor (per-core, globally aware) from the sampled cache (per-slice, local) in sliced LLC replacement policies to capture global reuse without centralized inter-slice traffic.

## Contributions
1. Analysis showing SOTA LLC policies lose effectiveness in many-core sliced LLC configurations
2. Per-core global reuse predictor + per-slice local sampled cache eliminating centralized interconnect overhead
3. Per-slice dynamic sampled cache improving LLC miss coverage per slice
4. On 32-core system: Mockingjay 6.7% -> 13.2% over LRU; Hawkeye 3.3% -> 5.6% over LRU
