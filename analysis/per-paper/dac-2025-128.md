# Security of Approximate Neural Networks against Power Side-channel Attacks

**Venue:** DAC | **ID:** dac-2025-128

## Core Problem
Security implications of approximate computing in ANNs; evaluating vulnerability to power side-channel attacks under voltage scaling, overclocking, and bitwise approximation.

## Method
The researchers tested whether attackers can steal neural network weights using correlation power analysis—a technique that extracts secrets by observing electrical consumption during computation. They tested against three energy-saving techniques: overclocking (running faster than designed), voltage reduction, and circuit-level bitwise approximation. They measured Signal-to-Noise Ratio (how much noise obscures the signal) and Measurement-to-Disclosure (how many observations attackers need), then analyzed the security-power-speed tradeoff using a combined metric (Security-Power-Delay).

## Key Novelty
Energy-saving approximation techniques in neural networks increase resistance to power-analysis attacks, requiring attackers to collect 4 to over 20 times more measurements to extract model weights.

## Contributions
- First security analysis of approximate ANNs vs. power CPA
- MTD improvement 4x-20x over precise computing
- SNR degradation and SPD trade-off quantification
- Voltage scaling as highest-performing approximate technique
