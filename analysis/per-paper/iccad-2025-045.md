# EPiCarbon: A Carbon Modeling Tool for Electro-Photonic Accelerators

**Venue:** ICCAD | **ID:** iccad-2025-045

## Problem
Embodied carbon (manufacturing) is not quantifiable for photonic chips, yet constitutes dominant portion of total carbon footprint. Existing models cover CMOS but lack photonic chip embodied carbon estimation.

## Motivation
Photonic computing promises orders of magnitude better energy efficiency than CMOS, but true carbon benefit requires quantifying both operational and embodied carbon.

## Method
The researchers created the first model to estimate the carbon released during manufacturing of photonic chips by studying the energy requirements and production success rates (yield) of these chips compared to traditional semiconductor chips. They built a tool called EPiCarbon that measures both manufacturing carbon and the carbon produced by running Electro-Photonic accelerators during their lifetime. They applied this tool to analyze leading EPiC accelerator designs and identify methods to reduce their overall carbon footprint.

## Key Novelty
This work introduces the first model to measure the carbon from manufacturing photonic chips, demonstrating these chips use 4.1 times less energy to produce than traditional semiconductor chips.

## Contributions
- Embodied carbon model for photonic chips with fabrication energy and yield analysis
- EPiCarbon open-source tool for carbon footprint evaluation of electro-photonic accelerators
- Case study on ADEPT accelerator identifying carbon-reduction strategies
