# Demystifying the Resilience of Large Language Model Inference: An End-to-End Perspective

**ID:** sc-2025-332  
**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Yu Sun, Zachary Coalson, Shiyang Chen, Hang Liu, Zhao Zhang, Sanghyun Hong, Bo Fang, Lishan Yang

## Problem & Motivation

Deep neural networks are known to tolerate random bitwise faults in parameters, particularly for classification tasks. However, this claim has been **under-explored for large language models** during inference. The key gap: aggregate metrics like accuracy may suggest resilience, but they mask **significant quality degradation in text generation**.

## Key Findings

1. **LLMs are NOT truly resilient** to random bit-flips despite superficial accuracy metrics suggesting otherwise
2. **Text quality severely degrades** under bit-flip injection even when accuracy remains high
3. **Complex reasoning tasks suffer more** — Chain-of-Thought and Mixture-of-Experts models show higher fault sensitivity than simpler inference
4. Quality degradation is observable in actual generated text, not just numerical metrics

## Methodology

- Extensive measurement study on commercial-scale LLM inference
- Random bitwise fault injection during inference
- Analysis of both aggregate metrics (accuracy) and qualitative text analysis
- Coverage of standard inference, CoT reasoning, and MoE architectures

## Implications

This work challenges the assumption that LLM inference can safely tolerate faults at scale. For fault-tolerant inference deployment (especially on unreliable hardware), **redundancy and error correction remain necessary**, contrary to what aggregate metrics might suggest.
