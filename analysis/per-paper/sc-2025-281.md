# On the Performance and Scalability of Cloud Supercomputers: Insights from Eagle and Reindeer

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Amirreza Rastegari, Prabhat Ram, Michael F. Ringenburg

## Problem & Motivation

The emergence of cloud-based supercomputers (Azure Eagle ranked 3 on Top500, Azure Reindeer ranked 32) challenges the traditional on-premises HPC model, yet systematic performance characterization remains absent. Misconceptions about virtualization overhead and cloud performance prevent informed adoption by the scientific computing community.

## Approach

Systematic comparative analysis of two Azure cloud supercomputers against bare-metal on-premises counterparts:

1. **Benchmarking Suite:** Microbenchmarks, synthetic workloads, and scientific workflows (including HPL)
2. **Metrics:** Computational efficiency, scaling characteristics, communication bandwidth, network latency
3. **Virtualization Analysis:** Overhead quantification across workload types
4. **Weak Scaling Studies:** Up to 14,400 H100 GPUs (Eagle) and 960 H200 GPUs (Reindeer)

## Key Novelty

- **Cloud HPC Characterization:** First systematic comparison of cloud supercomputers against bare-metal systems.
- **Virtualization Reality:** Demonstrates minimal virtualization overhead (2-4%) contrary to common assumptions.
- **Scaling Evidence:** Shows competitive weak-scaling efficiency on massive GPU counts.

## Contributions

- Comparative performance analysis of Azure Eagle and Reindeer
- Virtualization overhead quantification (< 4% in typical scenarios)
- Communication bandwidth and latency characterization
- Weak-scaling efficiency assessment on thousands of GPUs
- Guidance on cloud HPC viability and deployment

## Hardware Target

- Azure Eagle: 14,400 H100 GPUs (1,800 Virtual Machines)
- Azure Reindeer: 960 H200 GPUs (120 Virtual Machines)
- Cloud-based HPC infrastructure
- Comparison systems: On-premises bare-metal supercomputers

## Workloads

- High Performance Linpack (HPL) - primary benchmark
- Microbenchmarks for component analysis
- Scientific workflows
- Weak-scaling studies

## Key Results

- **Eagle Scaling:** 86%+ weak scaling efficiency on 14,400 H100 GPUs
- **Reindeer Scaling:** 100% weak scaling efficiency on 960 H200 GPUs
- **Virtualization Overhead:** < 4% for typical HPC workloads (2-3% typical)
- **Communication:** Higher throughput, comparable latencies vs. bare-metal
- **Implication:** Cloud supercomputers are viable for traditional HPC applications

## Limitations & Questions

- Workload diversity limited to HPL and simple synthetic patterns
- Cost-performance comparison with bare-metal not provided
- Applicability to data-intensive, I/O-heavy workloads unclear
- Comparison against traditional supercomputers (Frontier, Perlmutter) limited
- Long-term reliability and performance stability over extended operations uncharacterized
- Energy efficiency and power consumption not discussed
