# Revisiting Noise-adaptive Transpilation in Quantum Computing: How Much Impact Does it Have?

**Venue:** ICCAD

## Problem
Frequent noise-adaptive transpilation using up-to-date calibration data is widely considered essential but its necessity and overhead have not been thoroughly questioned.

## Motivation
Common wisdom assumes daily per-circuit transpilation is necessary, but the classical overhead may not be justified by fidelity improvements.

## Method
Empirical study across five IBM 127-qubit quantum computers and 16 quantum algorithms investigating noise-aware transpilation necessity and effectiveness.

## Key Novelty
Questions conventional wisdom about noise-adaptive transpilation necessity by showing random mapping can maintain comparable fidelity.

## Contributions
- Identifies that noise-aware transpilation concentrates workloads on small qubit subset
- Shows random mapping mitigates this effect while maintaining fidelity
- Demonstrates circuits compiled once can be reused across multiple calibration cycles
- Proposes lightweight alternatives reducing classical overhead
