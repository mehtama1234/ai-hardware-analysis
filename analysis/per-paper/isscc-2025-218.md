# isscc-2025-218: High-Efficiency Low-EMI Isolated Converter

## Quick Summary
A 180MHz, 45.3%-peak-efficiency isolated DC-DC converter using Q-downsize Class-D power amplifier with inherent shoot-through blocking, achieving robustness to frequency misalignments and low EMI.

## Problem
Isolated converters suffer from:
- High electromagnetic interference (EMI) from common-mode current across isolation barrier
- Suppressing CM voltage variation requires complex, fully symmetrical topologies
- Dead-time insertion for shoot-through protection adds complexity

## Method
The design uses a simplified switching amplifier (Class-D power amplifier with Q-downsize technique) that naturally prevents shoot-through current—the dangerous short circuit when both transistors conduct simultaneously—which eliminates the need for artificial delays that normally reduce efficiency. The transmitter is built with perfectly symmetrical circuits on both sides of the isolation barrier, and the coils wind in opposite directions so their magnetic fields cancel each other; this symmetry automatically suppresses the electromagnetic interference that normally leaks across isolated converters. The circuit operates at 180MHz and achieves 45.3% peak efficiency, with stable performance even when the switching frequency varies from its target.

## Key Novelty
A simplified Class-D switching amplifier (the Q-downsize technique) that naturally blocks shoot-through current surges without needing efficiency-stealing delays, while maintaining stable performance despite frequency variations.

## Contributions
- 45.3% peak efficiency
- 180MHz switching frequency
- Inherent shoot-through protection
- Robustness to frequency misalignment
- Low EMI via symmetrical design
