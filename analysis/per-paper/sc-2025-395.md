# Simulating many-engine spacecraft: Exceeding 1 quadrillion degrees of freedom via information geometric regularization

**Venue:** SC
**ID:** sc-2025-395
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Ultra-high-resolution multi-engine spacecraft CFD simulations require massive computational resources and memory; traditional approaches hit scalability limits.

## Motivation
Kilometer-scale Earth simulations demand exascale capabilities; advancing CFD techniques enables breakthrough simulations with improved efficiency metrics.

## Method
The approach applies Information Geometric Regularization (IGR—a technique that shapes the solution space using principles from information theory to favor more stable, efficient forms). It combines this with unified memory that allows CPUs and GPUs to work on shared data without copying, mixed-precision arithmetic (16-bit for less-critical data, 32-bit for precision-sensitive parts) to reduce memory and computation time, and adaptive algorithms that adjust to the specific hardware available, such as CPU-GPU systems or APUs.

## Key Novelty
It achieves 1 quadrillion degrees of freedom (20 times the prior record) while maintaining strong scaling and near-ideal efficiency, overcoming traditional scalability limits in ultra-high-resolution spacecraft CFD.

## Contributions
1. Record-breaking simulation scale: 200 trillion grid points, 1 quadrillion DOF
2. 4x wall-time speedup over optimized baselines
3. 80% strong scaling efficiency on full Alps system
4. Deployment on Frontier, El Capitan, and Alps with ideal weak scaling
