# Codesign for Broadcast Addressing Biochip Towards Tamper-Resistance and Enhanced Reliability

**ID**: vlsid-2025-048 | **Venue**: VLSID | **Confidence**: low (abstract only)

## Problem Statement
Digital Microfluidic Biochips (DMFB) using electro-wetting-on-dielectric (EWoD) technology and broadcast addressing are vulnerable to actuation sequence tampering attacks. Attackers can stealthily manipulate assay results. Existing tamper-resistant solutions lack practical wire-routing implementations, and increased electrode switching degrades reliability.

## Motivation
DMFB devices are critical for biomedical diagnostics, requiring both security against adversarial manipulation and operational reliability. A holistic codesign approach addressing design, security, and reliability constraints simultaneously is essential for practical deployment.

## Method
The method assigns physical wires to electrodes on a biochip—a device that manipulates tiny liquid droplets for laboratory testing. It optimizes this wire assignment to achieve three goals together: detecting hard-to-notice tampering attempts, ensuring wires can be physically routed without conflicts, and reducing electrode wear by minimizing switching. By addressing all three goals in a single optimization rather than separately, the method achieves better security, feasible wiring, and long-term reliability.

## Key Achievements
- Improved tamper-resistance metrics compared to state-of-the-art
- Provided first practical wire-routing solution
- Reduced switching toggles for enhanced reliability

## Novelty & Contributions

2. **Practical routing feasibility** addressing critical gap in prior tamper-resistant designs
3. **Reliability-aware optimization** reducing electrode switching stress
