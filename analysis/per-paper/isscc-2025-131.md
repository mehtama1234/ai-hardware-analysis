# isscc-2025-131: 12.2µW High-Resolution Delta-Sigma Modulator

**Problem:** Discrete-time (DT) delta-sigma modulators require large sampling capacitors to achieve high resolution, imposing huge driving burden on input buffers to settle charges; the input driver dominates power consumption while most current is wasted through passive components.

**Motivation:** High-resolution audio and sensor interfaces demand ultra-low-power ADCs; reducing input stage power consumption is critical for battery-powered IoT and medical devices.

**Method:** The design puts amplification directly into the sampling stage using a bootstrapped sampler—a circuit that automatically lowers its resistance when needed to reduce current draw. This cuts settling time (the delay for signals to reach their final stable value) and power wasted in resistors and capacitors. The resulting 12.2µW discrete-time zoom PPD delta-sigma modulator achieves 99.6dB SNDR and 184.8dB FOM.

**Key Novelty:** Gain-embedded bootstrapped sampler architecture reducing input-stage power consumption in high-resolution DT ΔΣMs.

**Contributions:**
- 12.2µW discrete-time zoom PPD delta-sigma modulator
- 99.6dB SNDR performance
- 184.8dB figure-of-merit (FOMs)
- Gain-embedded bootstrapped sampler for reduced input-stage power
