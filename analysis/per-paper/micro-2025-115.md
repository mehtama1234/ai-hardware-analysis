# Towards Closing the Performance Gap for Cryptographic Kernels Between CPUs and Specialized Hardware

**Venue:** MICRO  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only analysis)

## Problem
Achieving near-ASIC performance for cryptographic kernels (Number Theoretic Transform, NTT; and large-integer BLAS operations) on commodity x86 CPUs remains an open challenge; current CPU baselines lag ASICs by orders of magnitude for these workloads, which are foundational to post-quantum cryptography and fully homomorphic encryption (FHE).

## Motivation
Post-quantum cryptography deployments require NTT and large-integer BLAS at high throughput, and while ASICs dominate, their inflexibility and deployment cost motivate finding how close general-purpose CPUs can get with careful ISA and microarchitecture investment.

## Method
The paper develops optimized scalar x86-64 implementations of NTT and large-integer BLAS at the per-core level, then applies SIMD vectorization using AVX2 and AVX-512 to achieve 38x and 62x speedup over state-of-the-art CPU baselines for NTT and BLAS respectively. To narrow the remaining gap, the authors propose a small x86 ISA extension called MQX (multi-word extension) that adds three new instructions targeting multi-precision modular arithmetic patterns common in NTT and BLAS. MQX cuts the slowdown relative to ASICs to as low as 35x per core; a roofline analysis of multi-core scaling shows that top-tier server CPUs with MQX can approach ASIC-level throughput.

## Key Novelty
The MQX (multi-word extension) ISA extension — just three new AVX-512 instructions targeting multi-precision modular arithmetic — that closes much of the remaining gap between x86 CPUs and cryptographic ASICs for NTT and large-integer BLAS.

## Contributions
- Optimized scalar and AVX2/AVX-512 SIMD implementations of NTT and large-integer BLAS achieving 38x and 62x speedup over CPU baselines
- MQX: a minimal 3-instruction AVX-512 extension targeting multi-precision modular arithmetic that reduces ASIC slowdown to 35x per core
- Roofline analysis showing MQX-equipped multi-core server CPUs can approach ASIC-class throughput for cryptographic workloads
- Characterization of the CPU-to-ASIC performance gap for NTT and BLAS cryptographic kernels with practical implications for FHE/PQC deployment
