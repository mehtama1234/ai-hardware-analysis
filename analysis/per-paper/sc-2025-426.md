# QDockBank: A dataset for Ligand Docking on Protein Fragments Predicted on Utility-Level Quantum Computers

**Paper ID:** sc-2025-426
**Venue:** SC
**Authors:** Yuqi Zhang, Yuxin Yang 0001, Cheng-Chang Lu, Weiwen Jiang, Feixiong Cheng, Bo Fang 0002, Qiang Guan

## Problem

Protein structure prediction for ligand-binding regions remains inaccurate despite deep learning advances; quantum computing offers first-principles alternative but lacks benchmarking datasets and is expensive.

## Motivation

AlphaFold2/3 empirical models have limits for intricate binding geometries; quantum structure prediction could provide ground truth; standardized dataset enables validation and optimization of quantum approaches.

## Method

They used superconducting quantum processors (specialized computers that perform calculations by harnessing quantum mechanics) to generate 55 protein fragments over the course of roughly tens of hours. They then compared these quantum-generated structures to predictions from AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold3 (the leading AI models for predicting protein shapes) using two metrics: RMSD to measure how closely the predicted shapes matched, and docking affinity to measure how strongly small molecules would bind to the proteins. This represents the first dataset of protein structures created by quantum computers at this scale.

## Key Novelty

This work creates the first large-scale dataset of protein structures made by quantum computers, showing they predict protein binding regions more accurately than AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold3 (the leading AI protein prediction methods) based on both shape accuracy (RMSD) and binding strength (docking affinity).

## Contributions

- QDockBank: 55-fragment quantum-generated protein structure dataset
- Comparison with AlphaFold2/3 showing superior RMSD and docking affinity
- Benchmark for quantum-based protein structure prediction
- Proof-of-concept for utility-level quantum computing in drug discovery
