# isscc-2025-222: Fast-Converging FMCW Radar Digital PLL

## Quick Summary
An 11-to-16.4GHz FMCW digital PLL with posterior-segment DPD converging in 5 chirp cycles, generating high-linearity chirps (3.4GHz/µs slope, 5.32GHz BW) for interference-resilient autonomous driving radar.

## Problem
FMCW radars for autonomous driving require:
- Multi-vehicle frequency-hopping (bat-inspired) to avoid interference
- Multi-mode operation (long-range low-res, short-range high-res) within burst frames
- Wide-bandwidth, high-linearity chirps
- Fast convergence to new frequency bands

## Method
This system uses a digital phase-locked loop (a feedback circuit that automatically adjusts an oscillator to match a target frequency) to generate frequency sweeps called chirps from 11 to 16.4 GHz. It applies digital predistortion specifically to the final segment of each sweep to pre-correct for hardware distortions, allowing the system to converge—reaching stable operation—within just 5 chirp cycles. The resulting chirps sweep upward at 3.4 GHz per microsecond, cover 5.32 GHz of bandwidth, and maintain 0.043% RMS frequency accuracy despite the hardware's inherent nonlinearities.

## Key Novelty
Posterior-segment digital predistortion (applying correction only to the end of each frequency sweep rather than throughout) enables convergence within 5 chirp cycles, supporting rapid frequency-switching and multi-mode operation when multiple vehicles share radar spectrum.

## Contributions
- 11-to-16.4GHz tuning range
- 3.4GHz/µs chirp slope
- 5.32GHz bandwidth
- 5-chirp convergence
- 0.043% RMS frequency error
- Fast frequency-hopping capability
