# Cosmological Hydrodynamics at Exascale: A Trillion-Particle Leap in Capability

**Venue:** SC | **ID:** sc-2025-107

## Summary
Frontier-E: first trillion-particle (4T) full-sky cosmological simulation integrating gas dynamics with gravitational structure formation, demonstrating order-of-magnitude capability advance through GPU acceleration and hierarchical algorithm design.

## Problem & Motivation
Next-generation sky surveys demand cosmological simulations at unprecedented scales and fidelity. Current simulations must be scaled to trillion-particle systems with self-consistent gas dynamics (not gravity-only) to match survey requirements and enable predictive cosmological science.

## Key Findings
- **Scale:** 4 trillion particles in full-sky simulation
- **Performance:** 513.1 PFLOPs peak, processing 46.6B particles/second
- **Data throughput:** 100+ PB written in ~1 week
- **Capability advance:** Order of magnitude larger than previous work
- Sustained performance demonstrates viability of exascale approach

## Hardware & Systems
- **Platform:** ORNL Frontier supercomputer
- **Processors:** AMD EPYC with MI300X GPUs
- **Architecture:** GPU-accelerated hierarchical design
- **Scale:** Multi-node distributed-memory system

## Techniques
- **Separation-of-scale:** Hierarchical decomposition for scalability
- **GPU-resident tree solvers:** Gravitational tree codes on accelerators
- **In situ analysis:** Real-time data processing pipeline
- **Multi-tiered I/O:** Optimized data movement and storage
- **Hydrodynamics integration:** Self-consistent gas dynamics + gravity
