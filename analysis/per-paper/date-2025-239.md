# Nanoelectromechanical Binary Comparator for Edge-Computing Applications

**Venue:** DATE  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Core Idea
Build binary comparators using 4-terminal nanoelectromechanical relays instead of CMOS transistors to achieve extreme temperature/radiation tolerance, zero standby power, and dense device count.

## Problem
Edge computing in harsh environments requires comparators that tolerate high temperature and radiation; CMOS fails in these conditions and wastes standby power.

## Method
The design uses 4-terminal nanoelectromechanical (NEM) relays—mechanical switches with four connection points, controlled by electric signals—instead of transistors to build comparators (circuits that determine if two numbers are equal or which is larger). A single-bit comparator needs only 6 NEM devices versus 9 transistors in traditional silicon designs, and the relays are built on a silicon-on-insulator substrate (a layered semiconductor structure). For larger comparators, a tree-based stacking pattern reduces the total device count by about 47% versus traditional designs when handling 64-bit numbers.

## Key Results
- **6 devices per bit** vs 9 CMOS transistors
- **~47% device reduction** for 64-bit comparators
- **300°C operation** (vs ~85°C CMOS)
- **>1 Mrad radiation hardness**
- **21 fJ/operation** (9x energy reduction vs CMOS)
