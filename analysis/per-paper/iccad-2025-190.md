# Routing-Aware Placement for Zoned Neutral Atom-based Quantum Computing

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**ID:** iccad-2025-190

## Problem
Neutral atom quantum computers use zoned placement constraints; standard placement heuristics ignore routing feasibility, leading to unfeasible or inefficient qubit allocations.

## Motivation
Routing-aware placement for neutral atom quantum processors reduces swap gate overhead and improves two-qubit gate fidelity; efficient placement-routing co-optimization is critical for NISQ device scaling.

## Method
The method predicts routing costs during the placement stage—the step where qubits are assigned to positions—for neutral atom quantum computers. It models zone constraints (regions where qubits can't directly connect) and the overhead of routing signals between zones. It uses simulated annealing (an optimization technique that gradually improves solutions through random trials) or graph-based heuristics (algorithms that treat the problem as a network) to find placements that work reliably and efficiently.

## Key Novelty
The core idea is combining qubit placement decisions with routing-cost prediction for neutral atom quantum computers with zoned constraints (areas where qubits must route through intermediate zones instead of connecting directly).

## Contributions
1. Placement algorithm incorporating zone-based routing constraints
2. Routing cost prediction for neutral atom architectures
3. Co-optimization of placement and routing for quantum circuits
4. Demonstrated improvements in swap count and gate fidelity
