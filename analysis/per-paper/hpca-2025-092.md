# hpca-2025-092 — LAD: Efficient Accelerator for Generative Inference of LLM with Locality Aware Decoding

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
In LLM autoregressive decoding, the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length, and at lengths beyond 2048 tokens the attention mechanism's repeated read of the full KV cache becomes the dominant bottleneck, consuming over 50% of total inference time on GPU-based systems.

## Motivation
KV cache memory access bandwidth is the primary bottleneck for long-context LLM inference, and reducing the frequency of KV cache reads without sacrificing output quality would yield large speedups and energy savings.

## Method
LAD is a specialized hardware chip designed to speed up LLM text generation by exploiting a key observation: attention scores (which measure how important past tokens are at each generation step) often stay approximately the same from one generated token to the next. Normally, the system must read the entire key-value cache (the stored history of past tokens) at every single decoding step, and beyond 2048 tokens this becomes the main bottleneck. LAD detects when attention scores remain numerically stable across consecutive steps and caches their corresponding key-value vectors locally on the chip, rather than repeatedly fetching them from main storage, significantly reducing cache accesses while keeping generation accuracy intact.

## Key Novelty
LAD exploits numerical locality in attention scores—the observation that they remain approximately constant across consecutive decoding steps—to reduce redundant key-value cache reads by detecting stable positions and caching them directly on the hardware.

## Contributions
- Analysis demonstrating pervasive numerical locality in attention scores across consecutive LLM decoding steps
- LAD algorithm: attention decoding with reduced KV cache access based on score locality detection
- ASIC accelerator design implementing locality-aware attention with score history tracking
- 10.7x attention speedup and 52.4x attention energy efficiency vs A100; 2.3x end-to-end speedup at KV length >2048

## Limitations
The locality-based approximation introduces minor output quality degradation (97% ROUGE-1 similarity) which may not be acceptable for all applications requiring exact generation.
