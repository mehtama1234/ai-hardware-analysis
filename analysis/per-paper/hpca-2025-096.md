# hpca-2025-096 — DAPPER: A Performance-Attack-Resilient Tracker for RowHammer Defense

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Host-side RowHammer mitigation mechanisms rely on shared counter or tracking structures that are vulnerable to Performance Attacks (Perf-Attacks), where adversaries reverse-engineer the shared structure's mapping to artificially inflate DRAM access counters or trigger early resets, degrading performance of co-running benign applications.

## Motivation
As RowHammer thresholds shrink and mitigations become more aggressive, the shared tracking structures they depend on represent an increasingly exploitable side-channel that allows attackers to launch denial-of-service attacks on victim applications via DRAM bandwidth saturation.

## Method
DAPPER uses secure hashing—cryptographic randomization that hides patterns—to scramble which DRAM rows map to which access counters, preventing attackers from predicting where to target. DAPPER-S provides a baseline with a single hash function; DAPPER-H extends it with double-hashing (using two lookups to reduce collisions) and reset strategies that avoid predictable reset times. Both versions integrate RowHammer defenses into the system's normal memory-refresh cycles, piggybacking the mitigation to spread performance cost. The design uses just 96KB of SRAM per 32GB of DRAM and was validated on 57 workloads from SPEC2006, SPEC2017, TPC, Hadoop, MediaBench, and YCSB.

## Key Novelty
DAPPER defends against both RowHammer bit-flip attacks and attacks on shared tracking counters by using secure double-hashing (randomization across two lookup levels) plus adaptive reset strategies, all within under 100KB SRAM per 32GB DRAM.

## Contributions
- Formal identification and characterization of Performance Attacks against RowHammer mitigation tracking structures
- DAPPER-S: secure hashing template to prevent adversarial mapping inference of shared RowHammer counters
- DAPPER-H: enhanced design with double-hashing, novel reset strategies, and mitigative refresh for low performance overhead
- 0.9% slowdown under Perf-Attacks at RowHammer threshold 500, using only 96KB SRAM per 32GB DRAM
