# SC-2025-315: The (R)evolution of Scientific Workflows in the Agentic AI Era: Towards Autonomous Science

## Summary
This work presents a conceptual framework for integrating AI agents into scientific workflow systems, proposing an evolutionary path from current static workflow management systems to fully autonomous, distributed scientific laboratories. The framework charts evolution along two dimensions: intelligence (from static to intelligent) and composition (from single to swarm).

## Key Contributions
1. **Conceptual framework** for AI agent integration in scientific workflows
2. **Two-dimensional evolution model**: 
   - Intelligence axis: static → rule-based → learning-based → fully autonomous
   - Composition axis: single workflow → coordinated workflows → distributed swarms
3. **Architectural blueprint** for autonomous distributed scientific laboratories
4. **Vision articulation** for 100x discovery acceleration potential

## Technical Approach
- Multi-agent system design for scientific workflow coordination
- Intelligent resource allocation and scheduling
- Autonomous decision-making for experimental design and execution
- Integration of learning and adaptation capabilities
- Distributed system architecture for federated scientific facilities

## Hardware & Systems
- Distributed HPC facilities with diverse resource types
- Cloud computing platforms for elastic scaling
- Scientific computing centers with heterogeneous equipment
- Multi-institution scientific collaborations

## Workloads & Evaluation
- **Target domains**: Physics simulations, biology experiments, materials discovery, climate modeling
- **Workflow types**: Multi-stage scientific pipelines, iterative experimental cycles
- **Performance metric**: Discovery acceleration (conceptual, 100x potential cited)

## Limitations & Future Work
- Conceptual framework paper without detailed technical implementations
- Specific AI methodologies (reinforcement learning, planning algorithms) not detailed
- Scalability of autonomous systems to thousands of nodes unclear
- Safety, validation, and error recovery mechanisms not addressed
- Privacy and data management in distributed autonomous systems not discussed
- Economic and social implications of autonomous science not explored
- Comparison with other workflow systems and scheduling approaches missing
