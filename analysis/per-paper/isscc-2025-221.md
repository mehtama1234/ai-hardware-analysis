# isscc-2025-221: Circuits that Solve Optimization via Physics Inequalities

## Quick Summary
A novel approach to hardware-accelerated optimization that directly exploits physics inequalities and constraints encoded in circuit design, targeting machine learning, engineering design, and operations research.

## Problem
Traditional optimization methods (steepest descent, gradient-based) for:
- Deep learning training and inference
- Reinforcement learning
- Computer vision and speech processing
- Engineering design and operations research

Are computationally expensive; existing hardware accelerators face scaling limitations.

## Method
Physics-inspired circuits where physical constraints naturally encode the optimization problem structure. Rather than implementing algorithmic iterations (e.g., gradient steps), the circuit's inherent physics dynamics cause convergence to optimal solutions by leveraging physical inequalities.

## Key Novelty
Direct exploitation of physical inequalities in circuit design to solve optimization problems without explicit algorithmic implementation.

## Contributions
- Physics-based circuit optimization architecture
- Broad ML/engineering applications
- Potential efficiency improvement over digital algorithms
- New hardware acceleration paradigm

## Hardware Target
ASIC, Analog

## Technique
Circuit design, approximation

## Workloads
LLM training/inference, RL, vision, speech
