# isscc-2025-075: High-Efficiency Hybrid Boost Converter

**Venue:** ISSCC  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
High voltage conversion ratios in conventional boost converters suffer from very short turn-on pulses, voltage stress on inductors/transistors, and inherent right-half-plane zeros that limit transient response.

## Motivation
LiDAR transceivers for autonomous driving and motor drivers for drones/robots demand higher voltages (12-36V) and faster transient responses than conventional converters.

## Method
A boost converter steps up voltage by rapidly switching a transistor on and off to pump energy through an inductor. Traditional designs place the inductor at the input, which concentrates voltage stress on a single component and creates a right-half-plane zero—an electrical property that slows the circuit's response to sudden load changes. This design instead places the inductor in the middle (between input and output stages), spreading voltage stress across multiple components and eliminating the RHP zero. With continuous current flow on both sides, this achieves 93% peak efficiency and faster transient response.

## Key Novelty
Moving the inductor to the middle of the circuit eliminates the right-half-plane zero and spreads voltage stress across multiple components instead of concentrating it on one.

## Contributions
- 93% peak efficiency (12-36V conversion)
- Fast transient response without RHP zero
- Continuous input/output currents
- Improved reliability for edge applications

## Metrics
- Peak efficiency: 93%
- Voltage range: 12-36V output
