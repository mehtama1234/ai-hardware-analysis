# Data Management System Analysis for Distributed Computing Workloads

**Venue:** SC 2025  
**Authors:** Kuan-Chieh Hsu, Sairam Sri Vatsavai, Ozgur O. Kilic, Sankha Dutta, Yihui Ren, David K. Park, Tatiana Korchuganova, Joseph Boudreau

## Problem
ATLAS large-scale collaboration operates globally distributed systems for workflow management (PanDA) and data management (Rucio). Despite individual optimization, systemic inefficiencies emerge at global scale: underutilized resources, redundant data transfers, altered error distributions. Systems currently lack shared performance awareness and coordinated adaptation strategies.

## Motivation
ATLAS data processing involves millions of jobs across global computing centers processing petabytes of data. PanDA and Rucio independently optimize for their respective goals (job scheduling vs. data placement), but fail to coordinate at system level. Individual efficiency masks global inefficiency; fine-grained analysis is needed to identify opportunities.

## Method
The approach works by matching computing jobs (from PanDA) to their corresponding data files (in Rucio) using metadata—information about what data is stored where and what each job needs. It then identifies wasteful data transfer patterns across the global system that violate data-centric principles, such as moving the same file repeatedly when it could stay in place. Finally, it proposes coordination strategies to improve how the two systems work together and reduce unnecessary transfers.

## Key Novelty
This is the first systematic study of how a job scheduler (PanDA) and data management system (Rucio) interact globally, discovered by precisely matching individual computing jobs to their specific data files.

## Contributions
1. File-level metadata-matching algorithm linking jobs to datasets
2. Complete fine-grained view of data access and movement across global ATLAS
3. Identification of anomalous transfer patterns and violations of design principles
4. Practical mitigation strategies for tighter PanDA-Rucio coordination
5. Opportunities for resource utilization, transfer reduction, and system resilience improvements
