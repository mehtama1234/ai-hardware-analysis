# SC-2025-415: RingX - Scalable Parallel Attention for Long-Context Learning on HPC

## Summary
RingX optimizes ring attention for HPC architectures through improved workload partitioning and communication patterns, achieving 3.4x speedup on Frontier and 38% MFU for Llama3 8B training with 1M-token sequences on 4096 GPUs.

## Problem
Ring attention achieves parallelism but uses point-to-point communication that fails to exploit HPC network topologies, resulting in poor scalability and underutilization on supercomputers.

## Method
- **Improved workload partitioning**: Balances computation across ranks for better utilization
- **Refined communication patterns**: Optimizes for collective operations available in modern HPC networks
- **Load balancing**: Distributes work to minimize stragglers
- **Attention variants**: Supports both bi-directional (e.g., BERT) and causal (e.g., LLM) attention

## Key Results
- 3.4x speedup over ring attention on Frontier (AMD MI250X GPUs)
- 1.5x end-to-end speedup for Vision Transformer and Llama3 8B
- 38% model FLOPs utilization on 4096 GPUs (1M-token sequences)
- Represents one of highest reported efficiencies for long-context training on HPC

## Hardware Target
- ORNL Frontier supercomputer (AMD MI250X)
- Large-scale GPU clusters (4096+ GPUs)
- Systems with advanced HPC interconnects (InfiniBand, Slingshot)

## Technique Categories

## Workload Impact
- Vision Transformer training (climate datasets)

## Limitations

## Architectural Insight
RingX exemplifies the critical need for algorithm-architecture co-design in HPC, moving from topology-agnostic approaches to explicit exploitation of modern supercomputer interconnects for AI workloads.
