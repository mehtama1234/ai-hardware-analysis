# SMX: Heterogeneous Architecture for Universal Sequence Alignment Acceleration

**Venue:** MICRO 2025  
**Confidence:** Low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Sequence alignment accelerators are over-specialized to one alignment model (e.g., Smith-Waterman with affine gaps or simple substitution matrices) and fail to support the full variety of irregular use cases in practice (DNA, protein, ASCII text; weighted gaps; diverse substitution matrices), while general-purpose CPUs and GPUs lack the efficiency of domain-specific designs.

## Motivation
Exponential growth in genomic sequencing data and generative AI tools (e.g., AlphaFold) have created a pressing need for high-throughput sequence alignment that balances efficiency and flexibility across the heterogeneous alignment workloads demanded by real-world bioinformatics and information retrieval.

## Method
SMX is a heterogeneous architecture integrating two components orchestrated by a general-purpose core:
- **SMX-1D ISA extension** for irregular and sequential alignment tasks, adding new instructions to handle variable-length sequences and edge cases.
- **SMX-2D specialized coprocessor** to accelerate regular, parallel wavefront dynamic programming inner loops.

Both components support DNA, protein, and ASCII text with weighted affine gap penalties and full substitution matrices. Physical design targets 22nm technology, achieving 0.34mm² — 30% of a single-issue in-order CPU core.

## Key Novelty
A unified heterogeneous architecture pairing an ISA extension for irregular tasks with a dedicated 2D coprocessor for regular wavefront computation, achieving high flexibility across alignment types while delivering over 18x peak performance per area versus specialized accelerators.

## Contributions
- SMX-1D ISA extension enabling general-purpose core to handle irregular alignment tasks.
- SMX-2D coprocessor for parallel wavefront DP, supporting DNA/protein/ASCII with substitution matrices and weighted gap models.
- 256–744x speedup over state-of-the-art software; up to 18.5x more peak performance per area than specialized hardware.
- 22nm physical design: 0.34mm² (30% of single-issue CPU core).
