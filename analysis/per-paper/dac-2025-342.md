# MOSS: Multi-Modal Representation Learning on Sequential Circuits
**Venue:** DAC

## Problem
Circuit representation learning faces tradeoffs: LLMs understand RTL functionality but struggle with low-level netlist behavior; GNNs model netlists but fail on large sequential circuits due to long-range dependencies.

## Motivation
Accurate circuit representation is crucial for EDA tasks (placement, routing, timing prediction); sequential circuits pose scaling challenges for single-modality approaches.

## Method
MOSS combines graph neural networks (GNNs—models of circuit connections) with large language models (LLMs—models trained on circuit code) to overcome their individual weaknesses. It uses an LLM trained on the circuit's source code to create embeddings (numerical representations) for D-type flip-flops (memory components), giving the GNN both structural and code-level understanding. To handle large circuits where distant parts affect each other, it focuses the GNN on fewer critical anchor points and applies adaptive aggregation and two-phase propagation techniques to model signal flow. The LLM's overall circuit understanding also supervises the GNN during training, guiding it toward better representations.

## Key Novelty
MOSS combines GNNs with LLMs by treating LLM embeddings as node features for the GNN and using the LLM's global circuit understanding to supervise training.

## Contributions
1. 95.2% accuracy in arrival time prediction for sequential circuits
2. Improved scalability to larger circuits vs. GNN-only or LLM-only
3. Two-phase propagation for sequential feedback modeling
