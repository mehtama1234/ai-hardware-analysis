# isscc-2025-115: Wearable Photoacoustic Imager with Matrix-Multiplying SAR ADC

**Problem:** Wearable PA imaging needs low-power compact architecture with integrated compression; PA signals 10x smaller than ultrasound.

**Method:** The system combines spatial compressive sensing (acquiring measurements of an image space rather than measuring every point separately) with a specialized converter (SAR ADC) that performs matrix multiplication during analog-to-digital conversion. The hardware simultaneously compresses and converts the photoacoustic signal measurements at the sensor itself, avoiding the need for separate compression steps later. Since photoacoustic signals are inherently weak—about 10 times fainter than ultrasound—the converter is optimized with tuned gain, bandwidth, and noise characteristics to reliably capture these faint signals.

**Key Novelty:** Matrix-multiplying SAR ADC integrating compressive sensing into A/D conversion for real-time PA image compression.

**Main Results:** Hardware-integrated compression, low-power wearable form factor, reduced wireless bandwidth.
