# Efficient Optimization with Encoded Ising Models

**Venue:** HPCA 2025  
**Authors:** Devrath Iyer, Sara Achour

## Problem
Ising solvers (quantum, oscillator-based, and probabilistic computers) must include penalty terms to enforce constraints for combinatorial optimization problems, causing solvers to spend the majority of compute time exploring infeasible states and often failing to find any feasible solution.

## Motivation
Penalty-based constraint enforcement wastes most of a solver's exploration budget on invalid states, severely limiting the practical utility of emerging quantum and probabilistic computing substrates for constrained optimization.

## Method
The Encoded Ising Model (E-I model) inserts a digital encoding circuit between the solver and the optimization landscape, using combinational logic (circuits that instantly compute outputs from inputs) to map the solver's spin states (output configurations) to legal variable assignments and prevent exploration of infeasible states. Instead of using energy penalties to discourage bad solutions, constraints are enforced as hard logical rules. The Fuse software framework lets researchers describe constrained optimization problems in high-level code and automatically deploy them to p-computer hardware, reducing wasted iterations by orders of magnitude.

## Key Novelty
A digital encoding circuit inserted into the Ising solver loop structurally prevents exploration of infeasible states by enforcing constraints as hard logical rules instead of energy penalties.

## Contributions
- Encoded Ising Model (E-I model) formulation using digital encoding to eliminate infeasible states
- Fuse software framework for high-level constraint specification with automatic p-computer lowering
- 7.2x to 52000x reduction in iterations to find a feasible solution
- Up to 100% estimated success probability improvement over penalty-based baselines

## Results
- **Iterations to solution:** 7.2x to 52000x reduction
- **Success probability:** Up to 100% higher vs baseline penalty-based Ising formulations

## Hardware Target
Analog (probabilistic / p-computers)

## Limitations
Evaluation is limited to p-computers; generalizability to quantum or oscillator-based solvers is claimed but not experimentally demonstrated.

**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)
