# Defense in the Reverse Fragment: RL-Based Partial Netlist Hardware Trojan Detection

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**ID:** iccad-2025-104

## Problem
Hardware Trojan detection methods assume complete netlists, but practical reverse engineering yields incomplete/partial information, reducing detection accuracy and stability.

## Motivation
Enable practical hardware Trojan detection from partial netlist information obtained through reverse engineering of real circuits.

## Method
RE-PNRL uses reinforcement learning (an AI technique that learns by trying different solutions and getting feedback) to reconstruct the missing parts of incomplete chip designs (partial netlists) by calculating node-level transition probabilities—the likelihood that different circuit components connect to each other. Once the netlist is complete, the system uses clustering (grouping similar patterns) to identify hardware trojans embedded in the design.

## Key Novelty
It's the first hardware trojan detection system designed to work with the incomplete netlist information produced by real-world reverse engineering, instead of assuming you have a complete chip design.

## Contributions
- 66.39% TPR and 73.72% TNR on partial-netlist HT detection
- 96.80% TPR and 94.80% TNR on clustering-based complete-netlist detection
- RL-based netlist reconstruction from partial information
- Effective across different levels of netlist missingness
- Validated on Trojan benchmarks
