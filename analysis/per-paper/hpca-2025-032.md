# hpca-2025-032: The Importance of Generalizability in Machine Learning for Systems

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
ML models for cloud system management silently fail on out-of-distribution (OOD) workloads not represented in training data, causing mispredictions that degrade resource balance without any model self-awareness of unreliability.

## Motivation
Cloud environments continuously evolve; ML models trained on historical fleet data cannot reliably detect when their predictions are invalid, leading to incorrect resource allocation at scale.

## Method
The paper analyzes ML models for Google production fleet resource balancing, empirically demonstrating OOD failure modes. It evaluates OOD detection techniques (distance-based, confidence-based, ensemble) and their precision/recall/overhead trade-offs. Bayesian models with native calibrated uncertainty estimates are shown to effectively flag low-confidence predictions for fallback handling.

## Key Novelty
Demonstrating Bayesian models' native uncertainty quantification as a practical OOD detection mechanism for production ML-for-systems deployments at Google fleet scale.

## Contributions
- Empirical demonstration of OOD failure in production ML models for Google cloud resource balancing.
- Comparative analysis of OOD detection techniques and their accuracy-overhead trade-offs.
- Demonstration that Bayesian models enable effective OOD detection for ML-driven resource balancing.

## Metrics
- Qualitative and empirical demonstration on Google fleet; specific numbers not stated in abstract.

## Hardware Target
CPU

## Techniques
scheduling, virtualization

## Baselines
Standard ML models without OOD detection deployed in Google fleet

## Limitations
Bayesian model approach introduces additional inference overhead; end-to-end resource efficiency gains from OOD-aware balancing are not quantified.
