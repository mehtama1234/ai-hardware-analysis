# hpca-2025-032: The Importance of Generalizability in Machine Learning for Systems

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
ML models for cloud system management silently fail on out-of-distribution (OOD) workloads not represented in training data, causing mispredictions that degrade resource balance without any model self-awareness of unreliability.

## Motivation
Cloud environments continuously evolve; ML models trained on historical fleet data cannot reliably detect when their predictions are invalid, leading to incorrect resource allocation at scale.

## Method
The researchers studied multiple machine learning models that Google uses to balance server resources in data centers and documented real cases where these models failed silently on workload patterns they had never seen in training. They tested three categories of approaches to detect these failures: distance-based methods (checking how similar new workloads are to training data), confidence-based methods (checking if the model said it was sure), and ensemble methods (combining predictions from multiple models)—measuring the tradeoff between precision (avoiding false alarms), recall (catching real failures), and inference overhead (the computation cost). They found that Bayesian models, which natively estimate the uncertainty in their predictions, could reliably flag unreliable predictions and allow human operators or fallback systems to intervene before resource balancing went wrong.

## Key Novelty
Bayesian machine learning models' built-in uncertainty estimates can reliably detect unusual or unfamiliar workload patterns in production data center resource balancing, as proven at Google's fleet scale.

## Contributions
- Empirical demonstration of OOD failure in production ML models for Google cloud resource balancing.
- Comparative analysis of OOD detection techniques and their accuracy-overhead trade-offs.
- Demonstration that Bayesian models enable effective OOD detection for ML-driven resource balancing.

## Metrics
- Qualitative and empirical demonstration on Google fleet; specific numbers not stated in abstract.

## Limitations
Bayesian model approach introduces additional inference overhead; end-to-end resource efficiency gains from OOD-aware balancing are not quantified.
