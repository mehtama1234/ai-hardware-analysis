# Automated MCQA Benchmarking at Scale: Evaluating Reasoning Traces as Retrieval Sources for Domain Adaptation of Small Language Models

**Venue:** SC | **ID:** sc-2025-118

## Summary
Scalable automated pipeline for generating multiple-choice question-answering (MCQA) benchmarks from scientific paper corpora with evaluation of reasoning-trace-based retrieval augmentation for small language models.

## Problem & Motivation
Scientific knowledge grows rapidly, but evaluation benchmarks lag and become outdated. Small language models (1.1B-14B parameters) lack domain-specific knowledge. Traditional approaches require manual benchmark creation or expensive model fine-tuning. Need for continuous benchmark generation and effective domain adaptation.

## Key Findings
- **Benchmark scale:** 16,000+ MCQs from 22,000 open-access papers in single domain
- **Small model improvement:** Reasoning-trace retrieval consistently improves performance
- **Performance comparison:** Small models (1.1B-14B) surpass GPT-4 on 2023 Astro Radiation exam
- **Retrieval effectiveness:** Reasoning traces outperform semantic paper chunks for RAG
- **Benchmark validation:** Evaluation on both synthetic and expert-annotated questions

## Hardware & Systems
- **Infrastructure:** Standard computational resources (no specialized hardware)
- **Processing:** Distributed batch pipeline for PDF processing and QA generation
- **Scale:** 22,000 paper processing pipeline

## Techniques
- **PDF parsing:** Automated extraction from scientific documents
- **Semantic chunking:** Context-aware document segmentation
- **Question generation:** Automated QA creation from paper content
- **Reasoning trace distillation:** Extract reasoning chains from GPT-4
- **Retrieval-augmented generation:** Chunk-based and trace-based retrieval
- **Evaluation framework:** Synthetic and expert-annotated benchmark validation

## Metrics
| Metric | Value |

## Contributions
1. First at-scale automated MCQA benchmark generation from scientific literature
2. Demonstrated reasoning-trace-based retrieval outperforms semantic retrieval
3. Small models achieving expert-level performance with proper retrieval
4. Replicable pipeline for continuous benchmark generation
5. Practical domain adaptation approach for parameter-efficient models

## Limitations
- Limited to single domain (radiation/cancer biology)
- Reasoning trace approach specific to GPT-4 distillation
- Generalization to other domains and query types unclear
- Expert annotation limited to single exam (2023 Astro Radiation)
- No analysis of specific question types or difficulty levels
- Computational cost of reasoning trace generation not discussed
