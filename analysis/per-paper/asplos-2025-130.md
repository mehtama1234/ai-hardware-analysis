# M5: Mastering Page Migration and Memory Management for CXL-based Tiered Memory Systems

**Venue:** ASPLOS  
**Authors:** Yan Sun, Jongyul Kim, Zeduo Yu, Jiyuan Zhang, Siyuan Chai, Michael Jaemin Kim, Hwayong Nam, Jaehyun Park  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
CXL DRAM has 2-3x higher access latency than DDR DRAM, requiring effective page migration between tiers. Existing CPU-driven solutions misclassify warm pages as hot pages, fail to handle sparse hot-word pages (where only a small fraction of words in a page are frequently accessed), and incur profiling overhead that itself degrades performance.

## Motivation
CXL memory is emerging as a cost-effective capacity expansion for memory systems, but the latency gap between DDR and CXL tiers demands precise hot-page identification and migration that current CPU-driven solutions cannot provide.

## Method
M5 implements a CXL-driven profiling solution that precisely and transparently counts accesses to every 4KB page and 64B cache-line word in CXL DRAM using hardware-based hot-page and hot-word trackers embedded in the CXL controller. Unlike CPU-driven approaches relying on perf counters or OS page-fault sampling, the CXL controller observes all accesses on the CXL interconnect at cache-line granularity. Hot-word trackers enable sub-page granularity migration for sparse hot-page applications, avoiding unnecessary full-page migrations.

## Key Novelty
Hardware-based hot-page and hot-word trackers in the CXL controller that provide precise, transparent per-cache-line access profiling, enabling sub-page granularity migration decisions and avoiding CPU-driven misclassification.

## Contributions
- Discovery that CPU-driven page-migration solutions frequently misclassify warm pages as hot pages
- Identification and characterization of sparse hot-page applications requiring sub-page hot-word migration
- M5 platform with hardware-based hot-page and hot-word trackers in the CXL controller
- 47% hotter pages identified and 14% higher performance than the best CPU-driven solution with a simple policy
