# HapticGuide: Interactive Wearable Braille Guide for Enhancing Visual Education

**Venue:** VLSID 2025  
**Authors:** Divyansh Singhal, Yash Gupta, Daksh Sharma, Chinmay Sultania, Madhav Rao

## Problem Statement
Visually impaired students rely on traditional tactile graphics and physical models to understand geometric shapes, but these methods lack the dynamic, real-time feedback necessary to grasp complex spatial concepts. The lack of interactivity limits learning outcomes for geometric education.

## Key Contributions
1. **Wearable Haptic Device**: A 15×15 Velostat pressure sensor grid integrated with copper strips and a glove equipped with five coin vibration motors
2. **Real-Time Feedback Mechanism**: Cloud-synchronized haptic guidance that directs students as they trace shapes
3. **Scalable Assistive Platform**: Economical, portable design suitable for classroom and remote learning environments
4. **Improved Spatial Cognition**: Enhanced spatial understanding through interactive haptic guidance

## Technical Approach
- **Sensor Interface**: 15×15 capacitive touch grid using Velostat pressure sensors and copper strips for detecting shape traces
- **Actuation**: Five coin vibration motors in wearable glove for directional haptic feedback
- **Synchronization**: Cloud-based system for real-time mapping of touch inputs to motor outputs
- **Application**: Geometric education for visually impaired learners

## Hardware Target
- **Wearable**: Glove with embedded vibration motors
- **Sensors**: Pressure-sensitive grid (Velostat + copper)
- **Cloud Edge**: Synchronization and feedback processing

## Limitations & Open Questions
- **No quantitative metrics**: Paper lacks performance benchmarks (latency, accuracy, user study results)
- **Scalability**: 15×15 grid resolution may be limiting; no discussion of upscaling

- **Domain specificity**: Limited to geometric education; generalization to other tactile learning domains unclear
