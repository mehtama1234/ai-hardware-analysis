# asplos-2025-062 — Cinnamon: A Framework for Scale-Out Encrypted AI

**Venue:** ASPLOS  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) imposes orders-of-magnitude performance overhead for ML inference, and prior FHE accelerators use massive monolithic chip designs that do not scale to large models like BERT and provide poor performance-per-dollar.

## Motivation
Privacy-preserving ML via FHE is commercially important but practically infeasible at scale; a 17-hour FHE inference time for BERT on CPU makes deployment impossible, and monolithic accelerator chips cannot be economically scaled up.

## Method
Cinnamon breaks the work of fully homomorphic encryption (FHE—computation on encrypted data) into parts that run in parallel across multiple small chips (chiplets) instead of one large chip. A compiler identifies where this parallelism exists and assigns specific operations to different chips—polynomial transformations (NTT), number decompositions (RNS), and key-switching. Hardware and algorithm designs reduce resource needs per chip and data transfer between chips, enabling economical scaling with more chips.

## Key Novelty
The key innovation is using multiple small chips (chiplets) with a co-designed compiler and algorithms to distribute encrypted polynomial calculations, enabling practical encrypted AI inference on large models like BERT for the first time.

## Contributions
- Scale-out FHE framework exploiting multi-level parallelism across chips via novel algorithms and compiler.
- Architectural extensions that reduce per-chip resource requirements for FHE enabling multi-chip scaling.
- 2.3x improvement over prior state-of-the-art single-chip FHE designs on small programs.
- 36,600x speedup over CPU (BERT inference from 17 hours to 1.67 seconds), first demonstration of large-LLM FHE inference.
- 5x and 2.68x better performance-per-dollar vs. monolithic and chiplet state-of-the-art.
