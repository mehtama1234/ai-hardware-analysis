# QuCLEAR: Clifford Extraction and Absorption for Quantum Circuit Optimization
**Venue:** HPCA 2025 | **ID:** hpca-2025-007 | **Confidence:** low (abstract only)

## Problem
Near-term quantum devices have high gate error rates, and dominant optimization approaches leave substantial overhead from Clifford subcircuits that could be eliminated rather than merely rearranged.

## Motivation
Quantum circuit fidelity degrades exponentially with two-qubit gate count and depth on NISQ devices; eliminating Clifford subcircuits classically (rather than optimizing in-place) offers larger reductions than existing methods.

## Method
QuCLEAR uses a two-step process to optimize quantum circuits. First, Clifford Extraction identifies sections of the circuit that are "Clifford" (a special type mathematically easier to handle) and slides them toward the end using logical gate-rearrangement rules (commutation) while simultaneously reducing the harder non-Clifford gates—this reshuffling exposes opportunities to simplify further. Second, Clifford Absorption takes advantage of the fact that Clifford circuits can be computed by classical computers: instead of sending the extracted Clifford gates to run on noisy quantum hardware, it calculates their effect mathematically and bakes that result into the circuit's classical post-processing, removing those quantum gates entirely.

## Key Novelty
The core idea is Clifford Absorption: using a classical computer to post-process the Clifford part of the circuit after the quantum gates have run, rather than executing it on noisy hardware, which cuts entangling depth by up to 84.1% compared to traditional gate-level optimization.

## Contributions
1. Clifford Extraction: commuting Clifford subcircuits to circuit tail while reducing non-Clifford gate count
2. Clifford Absorption: classical simulation and elimination of extracted Clifford subcircuit
3. Up to 77.7% CNOT gate count reduction; up to 84.1% entangling depth reduction vs state-of-the-art
4. Demonstrated on VQE, QAOA, quantum chemistry, and many-body physics circuits
