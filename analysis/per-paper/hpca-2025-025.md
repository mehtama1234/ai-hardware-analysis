# hpca-2025-025: R.I.P. Geomean Speedup — Use Equal-Work (Or Equal-Time) Harmonic Mean Speedup Instead

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Geometric mean speedup, the dominant metric for summarizing multi-workload performance in computer architecture research, is mathematically ill-defined for speedup ratios and produces incorrect average speedup values that can lead to wrong architectural conclusions.

## Motivation
The widespread misuse of geometric mean speedup in architecture papers has silently biased comparative evaluations, because the geometric mean does not preserve the correct total-work or total-time interpretation that speedup ratios require.

## Method
The paper derives harmonic mean speedup from two concrete scenarios: equal-work (how much faster one system finishes the same task) and equal-time (how much more work gets done in the same time). It proves that harmonic mean correctly averages performance across multiple workloads, while the currently-used geometric mean does not. Examples show geometric and harmonic mean can rank competing architectures oppositely, so the wrong metric leads to wrong architectural choices.

## Key Novelty
Equal-work harmonic mean speedup is the mathematically correct metric for summarizing system performance across multiple workloads.

## Contributions
- Proof that geometric mean speedup is mathematically meaningless for summarizing speedups across workloads.
- Definition of equal-work and equal-time harmonic mean speedup metrics with explicit, transparent assumptions.
- Demonstration with real examples that the choice of averaging method changes architectural conclusions.
- Recommendation that equal-work harmonic mean speedup should replace geomean speedup in architecture research.

## Metrics
Methodology paper; no hardware numbers.

## Limitations
The paper focuses on speedup ratios and does not extend the analysis to energy-delay product, throughput/watt, or other composite metrics.
