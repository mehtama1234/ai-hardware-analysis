# PHOENIX: Pauli-Based High-Level Optimization Engine for Instruction Execution on NISQ Devices

**Venue:** DAC
**ID:** dac-2025-381

## Problem
Variational quantum algorithms require compilation at gate level, missing high-level Pauli-based optimization opportunities for Hamiltonian simulation programs.

## Motivation
Hamiltonian simulation programs have distinct Pauli-based structure; high-level IR optimization can drastically reduce circuit depth and gate count.

## Method
PHOENIX represents quantum programs as Pauli strings using binary symplectic form—a mathematical encoding that compactly describes quantum operations. It treats compilation as a simplification problem: using Clifford transformations (a family of quantum operations that preserve certain properties), it rewrites the program into equivalent but simpler forms, measured by reducing the complexity of the representation (column-weight reduction). A heuristic algorithm searches for the most efficient 2-qubit operators (gates affecting two quantum bits at once) and arranges them globally—like Tetris placing blocks optimally—to minimize the overall program structure.

## Key Novelty
By compiling quantum programs at the Pauli level using binary symplectic form representation, it enables systematic optimization across the entire program rather than just individual gates.

## Contributions
1. High-level Pauli-based IR for capturing program structure of Hamiltonian simulation
2. Binary symplectic form formulation enabling systematic BSF column-weight reduction
3. Heuristic algorithm for maximal Pauli simplification at each step via 2Q Clifford operators
4. Global ordering strategy balancing gate cancellation, circuit depth, and qubit routing
5. Outperforms SOTA VQA compilers across diverse backend ISAs and hardware topologies
