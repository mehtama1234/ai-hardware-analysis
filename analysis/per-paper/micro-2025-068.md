# micro-2025-068: SeaCache: Efficient and Adaptive Caching for Sparse Accelerators

**Venue:** MICRO  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Sparse tensor accelerators suffer from poor on-chip SRAM utilization because variable-length sparse fibers do not map cleanly into fixed-size cache blocks, wasting capacity. Theoretically optimal replacement policies (Belady OPT) require expensive on-chip metadata.

## Motivation
Sparse workloads (GNNs, sparse transformers, recommendation models) are highly memory-bound; effective on-chip data reuse is critical to performance, yet existing cache designs leave substantial capacity unused due to fragmentation from variable-length sparse data.

## Method
Three complementary techniques:
1. **Fiber packing and splitting** — variable-length fibers are split across or packed into fixed-size blocks to maximize utilization.
2. **Practical replacement policy** — approximates OPT-like behavior at low implementation cost by exploiting access-pattern regularities.
3. **Adaptive two-phase space sharing** — dynamically partitions cache between actual data and replacement policy metadata, tuning the ratio at runtime.

## Key Novelty
Two-phase adaptive partitioning of a single SRAM between sparse data and replacement policy metadata, dynamically tuned to maximize effective capacity.

## Contributions
- Fiber packing/splitting for variable-length sparse data in fixed-size cache blocks
- Practical replacement policy approximating OPT at low implementation cost
- Two-phase adaptive cache-space partitioning between data and metadata
- 2.8x average speedup over state-of-the-art sparse cache designs
