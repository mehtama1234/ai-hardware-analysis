# isca-2025-040: Single Spike Artificial Neural Networks

**Venue:** ISCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract only)  
**Primary Theme:** temporal-signal ANN execution for energy-efficient inference

## Problem
Standard ANNs require large-scale arithmetic with high energy consumption, while spiking neural networks (SNNs) circumvent this via biologically inspired computation but use fundamentally different algorithms from ANNs, preventing reuse of the mature ANN software stack and optimization techniques. The paper seeks an intermediate approach that achieves the energy efficiency of SNNs while remaining compatible with ANN algorithms and toolchains.

## Motivation
The growing energy cost of ANN inference drives interest in biologically inspired temporal/spike-based computing, but existing SNNs cannot leverage decades of ANN algorithmic advances and software infrastructure.

## Method
The design represents each ANN weight as a delay element and each activation magnitude as the arrival time of a digital signal. Complete ANNs are built on top of these temporal primitives through: (1) spatial and temporal reuse strategies enabling classical dataflows (e.g., weight-stationary) in a systolic array; (2) a noise-tolerant temporal summation operation for accumulation; (3) novel hybrid digital/temporal memory circuits for storing inter-layer activations; and (4) a temporal memory integration that manages activations between layers while preserving ANN algorithmic compatibility.

## Key Novelty
Encoding ANN weights as delay elements and activation magnitudes as digital signal arrival times, enabling ANN-compatible systolic array dataflows on biologically inspired temporal circuits with 11x better energy than SNNs.

## Contributions
1. A temporal computing substrate that maps standard ANN weights to delay elements and activations to signal arrival times, bridging ANN and SNN computation styles
2. Spatial and temporal reuse strategies enabling classical ANN dataflows (weight-stationary systolic array) within temporal circuits
3. A noise-tolerant temporal summation operation and hybrid digital/temporal memory for inter-layer activation management
4. Empirical demonstration using MLPerf Tiny benchmarks showing 11x energy improvement over SNNs and 3.5x over 8-bit digital systolic arrays with 4x latency improvement
