# Device-Aware Test: A Means to Attack Unmodelled Defects (Invited)

## Problem
Structural testing at advanced nodes and for emerging devices inadequately captures unmodeled defects, resulting in reduced product quality.

## Method
Device-Aware Test (DAT) adds a second layer to traditional manufacturing testing, which only checks if circuits are wired correctly. DAT uses models of actual transistor physics—how semiconductors behave, especially at tiny technology sizes—to design tests that expose defects these physical models predict. By grounding tests in real device behavior rather than just logical structure, DAT catches defects that traditional structural testing misses.

## Key Innovation
Paradigm shift from purely structural testing to device-aware testing that aligns with actual device physics.

## Contributions
- Novel Device-Aware Test methodology
- Application across multiple emerging device flavors
- Potential extension beyond manufacturing test

## Motivation
Recent manufacturing data reveal inadequacy of traditional structural testing at advanced nodes and for novel device technologies.
