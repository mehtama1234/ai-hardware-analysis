# hpca-2025-012 — Architecting Space Microdatacenters: A System-level Approach

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
No principled cost-aware methodology exists for architecting server-based computing on LEO satellites (Space Microdatacenters). Prior work proposes the concept without modeling Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) to guide hardware design decisions.

## Motivation
LEO Earth-observation satellites generate massive onboard data; in-space processing reduces downlink bandwidth and latency, but the unique cost structure (power vs. mass vs. monetary) demands a dedicated architecture framework.

## Method
The authors build a model that accounts for all costs in satellites with computing systems (space microdatacenters): power consumption, equipment weight, hardware purchase price, and communication. They explore different design approaches: using specialized chips (ASICs and neural processors) that do more floating-point operations per watt, coordinating computing across multiple earth-observation satellites, choosing between one centralized system and many distributed ones, and using more computing capacity than strictly necessary. Analysis reveals that power consumption is the dominant cost factor, though not in a linear relationship, while hardware cost and weight are much less significant.

## Key Novelty
This is the first framework to use total ownership costs to design space microdatacenters, revealing that extreme heterogeneity—deploying many types of specialized chips (ASICs and neural processors) that do more floating-point operations per watt—cuts total costs by 116 times, despite being expensive per unit of computation.

## Contributions
- First cost-aware (TCO) architecture analysis and optimization framework for SuDC.
- Power dominates TCO sublinearly; high FLOPs/W heterogeneity reduces SuDC TCO by 116x.
- Collaborative compute constellations further improve TCO by 1.31-1.74x.
- Distributed architecture cuts TCO 10% vs. monolithic; overprovisioning enables near-zero-cost availability.
