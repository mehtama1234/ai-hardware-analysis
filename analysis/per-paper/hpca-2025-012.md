# hpca-2025-012 — Architecting Space Microdatacenters: A System-level Approach

**Venue:** HPCA  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
No principled cost-aware methodology exists for architecting server-based computing on LEO satellites (Space Microdatacenters). Prior work proposes the concept without modeling Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) to guide hardware design decisions.

## Motivation
LEO Earth-observation satellites generate massive onboard data; in-space processing reduces downlink bandwidth and latency, but the unique cost structure (power vs. mass vs. monetary) demands a dedicated architecture framework.

## Method
TCO model decomposing costs into compute power, mass, monetary hardware cost, and communication. Design space exploration covers heterogeneous accelerator configurations (high FLOPs/W devices), collaborative compute constellations, monolithic vs. distributed architectures, and overprovisioning strategies.

## Key Novelty
First TCO-driven architecture framework for space microdatacenters, showing extreme heterogeneity (high FLOPs/W ASICs/NPUs) reduces TCO by 116x despite poor FLOPs/$ characteristics.

## Contributions
- First cost-aware (TCO) architecture analysis and optimization framework for SuDC.
- Power dominates TCO sublinearly; high FLOPs/W heterogeneity reduces SuDC TCO by 116x.
- Collaborative compute constellations further improve TCO by 1.31-1.74x.
- Distributed architecture cuts TCO 10% vs. monolithic; overprovisioning enables near-zero-cost availability.

## Metrics
- TCO reduction: 116x (extreme heterogeneity vs. homogeneous)
- Collaborative constellations: 1.31-1.74x additional TCO improvement

## Baselines

## Limitations
Analysis relies on modeled TCO without a physical SuDC prototype or real-world space deployment validation.
