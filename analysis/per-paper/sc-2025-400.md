# "Offloading" Undergraduate Research to the Graphics Processing Unit for Acceleration

**Venue:** SC
**ID:** sc-2025-400
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Undergraduate research in physics/engineering traditionally requires years to master PDE formulation; making advanced research accessible requires both education and performance.

## Motivation
N-body simulations accessible to freshmen can democratize physics research; modern GPUs enable this on consumer hardware.

## Method
The method converts complex physics equations (PDEs, or partial differential equations describing how quantities change over space and time) into N-body simulations—models where individual particles interact with each other. Graphics processors (GPUs) found in gaming desktops are built to efficiently calculate thousands of these particle interactions in parallel, making the simulation run far faster than a regular computer. This lets undergraduates run real physics research without first mastering years of mathematical theory.

## Key Novelty
The key contribution is using N-body simulations (where many particles interact with each other) running on GPUs in gaming desktops to make advanced physics research accessible to undergraduates, without requiring years of mathematical training.

## Contributions
1. Framework for reformulating PDEs as N-body simulations
2. Demonstration that consumer GPUs enable meaningful undergraduate research
3. Educational pathway requiring only freshman-level math and CS knowledge
