# Efficient and Distributed Computation of Electron Repulsion Integrals on AMD AI Engines

**Problem:** Computing electron repulsion integrals (ERIs) is computationally expensive, requiring trillions of evaluations per time step. Individual ERI computation on modern processor cores is difficult due to insufficient cache for intermediates and irregular memory access patterns that are difficult to vectorize.

**Method:** The system distributes 231 types of electron repulsion integral calculations across 2 to 14 AMD AI Engines (specialized processors), with each engine using a vectorization scheme that efficiently uses the AI Engine's floating-point hardware. The engines are arranged both horizontally (side-by-side) and vertically (stacked) in a grid pattern. Vertically stacked engines share time-divided communication channels to stream results between layers, allowing computation to overlap—when one engine sends its result, the next layer immediately processes it while the first engine starts fresh work, nearly eliminating idle waiting time.

**Novelty:** Vertically stacked ERI-Engines using time-shared result channels to almost completely eliminate delays from individual engine computations by allowing different layers' work to overlap.
