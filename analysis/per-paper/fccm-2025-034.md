# Efficient and Distributed Computation of Electron Repulsion Integrals on AMD AI Engines

**Problem:** Computing electron repulsion integrals (ERIs) is computationally expensive, requiring trillions of evaluations per time step. Individual ERI computation on modern processor cores is difficult due to insufficient cache for intermediates and irregular memory access patterns that are difficult to vectorize.

**Method:** The authors define flexible ERI-Engine graph structures that distribute computation across 2-14 AMD AI Engines to handle all 231 canonical ERI quartets. A novel vectorization scheme leverages AIE floating-point units for efficient computation. Multiple ERI-Engines are stacked horizontally and vertically across the AIE array, with vertically stacked engines using time-shared channels to stream results and hide computational phases.

**Novelty:** Vertically stacked ERI-Engines using time-shared result streaming channels to almost completely hide the computational phases of individual engines.
