# asplos-2025-000: Has Machine Learning for Systems Reached an Inflection Point?

**Venue:** ASPLOS  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)

## Problem
It is unclear whether ML-for-systems research has reached a tipping point analogous to AlexNet in 2012, where ML transitions from one of many competing approaches to the dominant methodology.

## Motivation
Prior ML-for-systems work has shown isolated wins but has not achieved the comprehensive, field-wide dominance that ML achieved in computer vision and NLP, raising the question of whether the field is now at a structural turning point.

## Method
The paper surveys machine learning's progress across systems research—spanning task scheduling, compiler design, storage, networking, and more—and compares how quickly each area has adopted ML approaches to known inflection points (turning points where machine learning became dominant) in other fields. It searches for signs of maturity: whether ML solutions are deployed in production systems rather than just research settings, whether researchers have standardized benchmarks for consistent testing, and whether machine learning has replaced the hand-written heuristics (decision rules) that engineers traditionally created.

## Key Novelty
The paper asks whether machine learning has reached an inflection point in systems research (a turning point where it becomes the dominant approach) by comparing the field's progress to how deep learning transformed computer vision after the AlexNet breakthrough in 2012.

## Contributions
- Systematic survey of ML-for-systems research trajectories across multiple systems sub-domains.
- Identification of indicators distinguishing pre- and post-inflection-point phases in ML adoption.
- Analysis of remaining gaps (data, benchmarks, deployment pipelines) that may delay a full inflection point.
