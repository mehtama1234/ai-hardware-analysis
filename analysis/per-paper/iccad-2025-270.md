# CNOT Oriented Synthesis for Small-Scale Boolean Functions Using Spatial Structures of Parallelotopes

**Venue:** ICCAD  
**ID:** iccad-2025-270

## Problem
Quantum circuit scalability is limited by gate fidelity and qubit counts in the NISQ era, restricting physical implementations to small-scale circuits. Existing logic network structures for quantum circuit synthesis neglect the spatial structure intrinsic to Boolean functions.

## Motivation
To enhance synthesis efficiency and reduce circuit complexity for small-scale Boolean functions by leveraging spatial structures encoded in parallelotopes.

## Method
SSHR (Spatial Structure-based Hypercube Reduction) discovers geometric regions called parallelotopes in the hypercube representation of Boolean functions (abstract spaces representing all possible input combinations), then extracts spatial patterns from them to minimize Multi-Control Toffoli gates (expensive quantum operations that conditionally apply a NOT gate based on multiple qubits) in quantum circuits. Two variants handle this differently: SSHR-H uses heuristic functions (quick approximation rules) to speed up the search, while SSHR-I employs Integer Linear Programming (a mathematical optimization solver) to maximize the exploitation of spatial structure.

## Key Novelty
By using parallelotopes to encode the spatial structure of Boolean functions, it accesses quantum circuit optimizations that conventional methods cannot find.

## Contributions
- Novel SSHR synthesis method tailored for small-scale Boolean functions (≤8 qubits)
- Two variants with different optimization strategies: heuristic-based and ILP-based approaches
- 56% and 81% reductions in CNOT gate counts compared to ESOP and XAG methods
