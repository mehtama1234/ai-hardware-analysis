# Simulation Framework and Optimization of Superconducting Transmon-Tunable Coupler-Transmon System for Qudit Gates

**Venue:** ICCAD
**ID:** iccad-2025-146
**Confidence:** low

## Problem
Superconducting quantum processors are limited by low qubit counts and high gate errors; extending to qudits offers larger state space but requires optimization for qutrit-based systems.

## Motivation
Optimizing qudit-based quantum processors can improve gate fidelities and move toward fault-tolerant quantum computing.

## Method
The framework simulates how unwanted quantum interactions (called cross-Kerr effects) behave in superconducting Transmon-Tunable Coupler-Transmon (TTT) systems and measures how accurately controlled-phase (CPhase) quantum gates perform between two three-level quantum systems under realistic noise. It then jointly optimizes both the control signals and device design to find the best conditions—specifically the target resonant frequency—that enable these gates to work more reliably using adiabatic schemes, where quantum states are gradually adjusted to maintain their integrity.

## Key Novelty
A simulation framework that predicts and optimizes the accuracy of quantum gates in three-level quantum systems (called qudits), specifically superconducting Transmon-Tunable Coupler-Transmon (TTT) devices.

## Contributions
- Integrated simulation framework for TTT cross-Kerr interactions
- Noise-aware qutrit CPhase gate fidelity prediction
- Control-device co-optimization methodology
- Identification of conditions for maximum adiabatic target frequency
