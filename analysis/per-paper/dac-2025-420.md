# Blaze: An Efficient Bit-Sparse Attention Architecture With Workload Orchestration

**Problem:** Attention has high computational complexity and power; existing bit-sparsity methods (for CNNs) don't address irregular non-zero bit distribution and workload imbalance in attention tensors.

**Method:** Blaze speeds up attention mechanisms in AI models by exploiting sparsity at two levels: skipping zero values and skipping zero bits within individual numbers. However, these non-zero bits don't spread evenly across the data—some hardware ends up idle while other parts get overloaded. Blaze solves this load imbalance with an Approximate-Computing-Based (ACB) workload orchestration system, which redistributes computations and allows small approximation errors when necessary to keep hardware running at full capacity. It also includes a Leading-Booth mechanism that optimizes bit-serial computation (processing data one bit at a time) and runs all of this on a reconfigurable computing engine that can adapt between different computation strategies.

**Key Result:** 2.37-6.18x speedup vs SOTA; 9.69-43.96x energy efficiency vs SOTA; 1.58x vs SOTA bit-sparse accelerators.

**Hardware Target:** ASIC  
**Workloads:** LLM-inference, transformer, attention  
**Confidence:** low (abstract-only)
