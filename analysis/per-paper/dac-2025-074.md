# Invited paper: Enhancing Design Automation with Quantum Algorithms for Chip Design

**Venue:** DAC | **Confidence:** Low (abstract-only)

## Problem
Cell placement optimization to minimize Half-Perimeter Wire Length is computationally expensive in classical approaches, limiting scalability for large designs; quantum and hybrid approaches may offer speedup.

## Motivation
Chip design resource efficiency (area, power) is strongly correlated with wire length optimization; quantum computing offers potential for accelerating placement optimization, though practical integration with EDA flows remains unproven.

## Method
A flexible quantum-hybrid placement optimization strategy minimizes HPWL through both custom Simulated Annealing and D-Wave Constrained Quadratic Model solvers, integrated with OpenROAD flow for layout and routing; validated on ASAP7 7nm PDK with real-world case studies.

## Key Novelty
Quantum (D-Wave CQM) and hybrid optimization for chip cell placement integrated with industry-standard OpenROAD EDA flow.

## Contributions
- Quantum optimization strategy for HPWL minimization using D-Wave CQM solver
- Integration with OpenROAD enabling practical validation on real PDK and routing
- Comparison of classical (Simulated Annealing) and quantum optimization approaches
- Demonstration of better area usage and performance improvements on ASAP7 7nm
- Path toward using quantum computers for chip design EDA workloads

## Metrics
- Performance: Better area usage and considerable speedup/performance improvement

## Baselines
- Classical Simulated Annealing, Standard placement methods

## Limitations
Preliminary results; scalability to larger designs and quantum advantage over classical not fully demonstrated.
