# ThirstyFLOPS: Water Footprint Modeling and Analysis Toward Sustainable HPC Systems

**Venue:** SC
**Paper ID:** sc-2025-013
**Confidence:** low

## Problem Statement
Water footprint of HPC systems is poorly understood despite growing water-intensiveness from cooling and power generation; environmental impact underexplored

## Motivation
HPC systems consume massive water for cooling; power generation also water-intensive; water scarcity is growing concern; sustainability metrics needed for HPC planning

## Method
The framework tracks water usage with three complementary metrics tailored to each region: WUE (water usage effectiveness—how much water is consumed per unit of computing work), PUE (power usage effectiveness—the ratio of total facility power to the computing portion), and EWF (embodied water footprint—water embedded in electricity generation and cooling). The team measured these metrics on four real supercomputers—Marconi, Fugaku, Polaris, and Frontier—using actual operational data. They then assessed how regional conditions like water scarcity and power sources (including nuclear energy) affect whether these systems operate sustainably.

## Key Novelty
ThirstyFLOPS measures supercomputer water consumption—including cooling and electricity—in ways that adapt to each region's water availability and power sources, to guide water-conscious infrastructure design.
