# Tunnel Magnetoresistance in Strained L10-FeAu Perpendicular Magnetic Tunnel Junction

**VLSID 2025** | vlsid-2025-085

## Abstract
L10-FeAu MTJ performance optimized via strain engineering requires understanding TMR behavior under mechanical stress. This computational study combines DFT and Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) techniques to analyze strain effects. Unstrained L10-FeAu shows high TMR due to spin-polarized Δ1 bands at Fermi surface. Under tensile/compressive strain, TMR decreases due to band structure changes, revealing design tradeoffs for spintronic applications.

## Problem
- MTJ-based spintronic devices require high TMR for performance
- Strain is inevitable in integrated systems
- Strain impact on intermetallic ferromagnets poorly understood

## Key Insight
Strain modifies spin-polarized band structure at Fermi surface, reducing TMR despite initial high performance in unstrained state. This band-level mechanism governs spintronic device optimization.

## Approach
1. Density Functional Theory (DFT) band structure calculations
2. Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) device modeling
3. Tensile and compressive strain application
4. Spin-polarized density analysis at Fermi level
5. Δ1 band tracking under strain

## Findings

### Strain Effects
- **Unstrained**: Significantly high TMR (due to Δ1 band spin polarization)
- **Tensile strain**: TMR reduction
- **Compressive strain**: TMR reduction
- **Mechanism**: Fermi-level band structure modification breaks spin polarization

### Design Implications
- Strain must be carefully managed in MTJ integration
- Material selection critical for strain tolerance
- Trade-offs between interface engineering and strain resilience

## Contributions
- Computational framework for MTJ strain analysis
- Band-level mechanism revealing for TMR reduction
- Design guidelines for strain-resilient spintronic devices
- Material optimization strategies
