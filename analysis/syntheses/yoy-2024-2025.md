# 2024 to 2025: What the Numbers Actually Show

*A year-over-year analysis of AI hardware research across six matched venues: ASPLOS, DAC, HPCA, MICRO, MLSys, and SC.*

---

## The Caveat That Shapes Everything

Before interpreting any delta, one number demands attention: the 2024 sample contains 69 papers across the six matched venues; 2025 contains 1,264. ASPLOS went from 5 papers to 164. SC went from 5 to 430. DAC went from 10 to 443.

This is not field growth. This is corpus coverage growth. The 2024 per-paper analyses represent an early, selective pass at these venues — not full proceedings. The 2025 coverage is near-complete. Percentage changes in the hundreds or thousands are therefore artifacts of denominator size, not signals about the field's actual trajectory.

What the numbers *can* tell you, honestly, is this: given the handful of 2024 papers that were analyzed, where did each venue's existing research weight fall? And does the 2025 full-coverage distribution confirm or contradict those early signals? That comparison — between a small early sample and a large complete sample — is still informative, because early analyses were not randomly selected. They were selected to be representative or important.

With that caveat established, here is what the data shows.

---

## What the Numbers Show: Theme by Theme

**T3 (Memory hierarchy, PIM, HBM, CXL)** is the largest theme in 2025 by matched-venue count: 201 papers. The 2024 matched sample had 15. Memory was the plurality theme in 2024, and it remains the plurality theme in 2025. This is the one comparison that holds up across the coverage gap — the weight was there before and the weight is still there. The field's central bet on near-data processing and CXL-attached memory appears in every matched venue: HPCA (39 papers, up from 2), DAC (77, up from 2), ASPLOS (40, up from 0), and MICRO (13, up from 3). If any single theme has the strongest claim to genuine multi-year stability, T3 is it.

**T1 (Attention, LLM serving, KV-cache)** had the largest raw 2024 presence: 17 matched papers, the single biggest theme in that sample. In 2025 it reaches 164, still the second-largest theme. Critically, MLSys shows a clear pattern: T1 grew from 11 to 31 papers (a +182% increase on a venue that expanded from 37 to 61 analyzed papers — so the T1 *share* at MLSys actually rose from 30% to 51%). The serving stack — prefill/decode disaggregation, KV cache compression, speculative decoding — is concentrating, not dispersing. MLSys has effectively become the primary venue for LLM inference systems research, with T1 now accounting for half its output.

**T2 (Quantization)** was 12 papers in the 2024 matched sample, second only to T1. In 2025 it is 76. But here is where the coverage gap bites hardest: MLSys 2024 had 9 T2 papers out of 37 analyzed (24%), while MLSys 2025 has only 4 T2 papers out of 61 (7%). This is a real signal. MLSys has deprioritized pure quantization research, likely because the W4A8 recipe is now established. The quantization papers moved downstream to DAC (35 papers, where they become circuit-level problems) and HPCA (10 papers, where they become microarchitecture problems). The theme did not shrink; it matured and migrated.

**T9 (Specialized silicon: FPGA, quantum, photonic, edge, cryptographic)** shows the largest absolute paper count in 2025: 171 matched papers. The 2024 sample had only 3. SC is the primary driver — 54 T9 papers at SC 2025, where domain-specific accelerators for scientific computing figure prominently. DAC contributes 80 T9 papers, covering the full spectrum of non-GPU silicon. Whether this represents a genuine surge in specialized silicon research, or simply the fact that the DAC and SC corpora were not well sampled in 2024, cannot be determined from the data alone. But the 2025 distribution is clear: specialized silicon is the plurality category at DAC.

**T6 (Compiler, MLIR, HLS, auto-tuning)** reads 131 papers in 2025 against 6 in 2024. The dominant contributor is DAC (71 papers), followed by ASPLOS (23) and MICRO (5). ASPLOS in particular shifted dramatically: 1 compiler paper in the 2024 sample, 23 in 2025. Given that ASPLOS covers the full programming systems stack, this likely reflects genuine growth in compiler-level LLM optimization work — tensor programs, fusion, auto-tuning for non-standard number formats like MX — rather than just coverage expansion.

**T7 (Security)** and **T8 (Reliability)** together show a structural shift at ASPLOS specifically. In 2024 there were zero security papers and zero reliability papers from ASPLOS in the sample. In 2025 there are 24 security and 13 reliability papers. ASPLOS has absorbed a significant slice of the hardware security research that previously concentrated at security-specific venues. The underlying driver is almost certainly the intersection of confidential computing (SGX, TrustZone, CCA) with LLM deployment: when you serve a model inside a TEE, you need both the security guarantee and the performance of near-memory computation, and ASPLOS sits at exactly that intersection.

## Venue-Specific Shifts

**MLSys** is the most interpretable because its coverage did not change as dramatically. The shift is clear: T1 grew from 30% to 51% of the analyzed corpus. T2 fell from 24% to 7%. T5 (sparsity/MoE) held roughly stable at 5-13%. The reading: MLSys completed its transition from "how do we make quantization work?" to "how do we deploy large, sparse, attention-heavy models at scale?" The quantization problem is considered solved (or at least graduated to silicon design) — the serving system problem is the current frontier.

**HPCA** shifted toward T3 and T1 heavily. In 2024 it was evenly split across T1, T2, T3, and T4 (one paper each). In 2025 the plurality is T3 (33%), with T1 second (14%) and T9 third (11%). Architecture conferences are organizing around memory subsystem design for LLM workloads — a consistent story across HPCA and MICRO.

**MICRO** in 2024 was led by T3 (memory) and T5 (sparsity). In 2025 T3 remains dominant at 28%, but T9 (specialized silicon at 15%) and T7 (security at 13%) grew significantly. MICRO is broadening beyond the memory wall to include the correctness and security implications of heterogeneous memory designs — a natural trajectory once the baseline hardware innovation is established.

**DAC** had even coverage across all themes in 2024 (about 1 paper per theme). In 2025 it is the most evenly distributed venue, with T9 (18%), T3 (17%), T6 (16%), T1 (9%), T2 (8%), T8 (8%), and T7 (9%) all represented. DAC in 2025 is the venue that covers the widest cross-section of AI hardware — effectively the systems integration conference where the problems that were solved at HPCA and MICRO get turned into manufacturable circuits.

**SC** in 2024 had only T3, T4, and T6 represented (memory, interconnect, compiler — the HPC stack). In 2025, T0 (catch-all systems) is the plurality at 35%, followed by T9 (13%), T4 (10%), T5 (7%), T3 (6%), and T8 (7%). SC has absorbed large language model workloads into the HPC scheduling and reliability stack: running training runs on 65,000-GPU clusters requires the same fault tolerance and collective communication infrastructure as traditional HPC, and SC is where that work lives.

## The Real Story: Migration, Not Growth

The most honest interpretation of the 2024-to-2025 data is not "themes grew" but "themes migrated." The core problems that AI hardware research identified in 2024 — KV cache bandwidth, quantization math, near-data computation, specialized silicon efficiency — did not go away. They matured, and as they matured, they moved to different venues.

Quantization migrated from MLSys (where it was an empirical systems question) to DAC (where it became a circuit design question) and HPCA (where it became a microarchitecture question). Attention and serving stayed at MLSys but deepened from technique demonstrations to production-scale systems problems. Memory moved from HPCA (conceptual architecture) to DAC and ASPLOS (concrete implementation). Security grew at architecture venues, not security venues, because the attack surface of in-memory processing is an architecture problem.

This migration pattern has a predictable continuation. The themes that are currently "hot" at a given venue will, in two to three years, appear as "solved infrastructure" at that venue and as "new problem" at the implementation layer below it.

---

## What This Predicts About 2026

**T3 and T1 will deepen, not shift.** Memory hierarchy research and attention acceleration are not approaching saturation — they are approaching the phase where results are commercially deployed, which generates more research problems, not fewer. The CXL memory tiering papers at HPCA 2025 will become silicon validation papers at DAC 2026 and production case studies at MLSys 2026.

**T7 (security) will grow significantly at architecture venues.** Every major cloud provider is now deploying confidential AI inference. The hardware architecture of a TEE-protected inference system — where do you put the integrity tree, how do you bound the HBM access pattern, what does memory encryption cost at KV-cache bandwidth — is a set of open questions that HPCA and MICRO will absorb over the next two years. The 24 security papers at ASPLOS 2025 are the leading edge of this.

**T6 (compiler) at ASPLOS will keep growing.** The compiler layer for non-standard numeric formats (MX, FP8, block floating point) is not solved. Neither is the HLS gap at FCCM, nor the auto-tuning problem for heterogeneous memory hierarchies. Compilers are the glue between the hardware innovations and the application workloads, and the hardware is currently innovating faster than the compilers.

**MLSys will concentrate further on T1.** The production serving stack is now the primary frontier for systems research. RAG retrieval, context window scaling, disaggregated prefill/decode, multimodal KV caches — all of these extend the serving problem into adjacent domains. MLSys's T1 share will approach 60% by 2026 as these adjacent problems join the core attention/scheduling work.

**T9 at SC will clarify.** The large T9 count at SC 2025 reflects domain-specific accelerators entering HPC workloads: quantum computing simulation, genomics, physics-informed neural networks. By 2026, the SC T9 papers will split cleanly into those that use FPGAs (the power-constrained edge path) and those that use ASICs or near-memory devices (the throughput path). That split will define the next wave of specialization.

---

## The Number to Watch

One ratio captures the field's direction better than any theme count: the fraction of papers at architecture venues (HPCA, MICRO, ASPLOS, ISCA) whose primary evaluation metric is energy efficiency (TOPS/W, pJ/op) rather than latency (ms/token) or throughput (tokens/s).

In 2024, architecture papers were predominantly latency-optimized. The 2025 corpus shows energy efficiency as a co-primary metric at ISSCC and increasingly at HPCA and MICRO. This transition — from "how fast?" to "how efficiently fast?" — is the leading indicator of the field entering the deployment phase where edge and embedded constraints matter as much as datacenter throughput. When energy efficiency becomes the primary metric at ISCA (currently a latency/throughput venue), the field will have completed its transition from research innovation to deployment engineering.

That transition is probably 18 months away.
