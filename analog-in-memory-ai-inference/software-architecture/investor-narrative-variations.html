Investor Narrative Variations

This page gives several ways to tell the investor story without overclaiming. Each version emphasizes a different reason the company could matter.

Question: what story should we tell investors once we know the first market wedge and proof plan?

Plain rule:

The investor story should not outrun the evidence. It should explain what is proven now, what the next funding round de-risks, and which claims remain blocked.

Simple Vocabulary

Wedge means the first narrow market where the company tries to win.

Moat means the advantage that is hard for competitors to copy. Here, the moat should include hardware, simulator, compiler, calibration data, benchmark evidence, and customer workflow.

De-risk means reducing uncertainty. A funding round should pay for specific proof that makes the next stage less uncertain.

Milestone means a concrete event that proves progress, such as a benchmark result, board run, partner integration, or measured power result.

Common Investor Spine

All versions should share the same backbone:

  1. Physical AI is moving compute closer to sensors and machines.
  2. Selected workloads are limited by power, heat, latency, and data movement.
  3. Analog IMC may help when the workload is matrix-heavy, repeated, sensor-near, and low-update.
  4. The hard part is not only the chip. The hard part is proving fit through simulator, compiler, calibration, and measured evidence.
  5. The company builds the full proof system, not only the analog array.

How To Choose The Narrative

The right investor story depends on what has actually been proven. Early on, the story should emphasize discipline: narrow wedge, proof system, simulator, compiler, and measured milestones. Later, once the board and customer evidence exist, the story can shift toward scale and partnerships.

Variation A: Physical AI Wedge Story

Best for: investors focused on robotics, edge AI, embodied AI, and next-generation devices.

Core story: Physical AI needs local intelligence under real-world constraints. Cloud-scale AI is not enough when the system must sense, decide, and react near the physical signal.

Our position: We are building a low-power analog co-processor for selected sensor-near workloads, with a software workbench that proves when the chip fits.

Proof milestones: customer intake, tactile or industrial benchmark, analog simulator result, compiler map, timing budget, board-power target.

Avoid saying: we run the whole robot brain.

Variation B: Software-Proof Moat Story

Best for: investors skeptical of hardware-only startups.

Core story: Analog chips historically fail commercially because they are hard to program, hard to simulate, and hard to trust. We solve that by building the proof and developer workflow from day one.

Our position: The workbench is the bridge from model to chip: intake, simulator, hardware-aware training, compiler mapping, calibration, benchmark evidence, and claim control.

Proof milestones: repeatable simulator adapter, analog-mlir extension plan, evidence matrix, benchmark package, calibrated chip profile.

Avoid saying: the software proves the hardware works before hardware exists.

Variation C: Sensor-Near Power Story

Best for: investors focused on battery devices, wearables, drones, robotics hands, industrial sensors, and always-on edge systems.

Core story: Moving raw sensor data to a central processor wastes energy and time. Some first-stage inference should happen near the sensor.

Our position: Analog IMC can reduce data movement and run selected matrix-heavy feature or reflex models close to the physical signal.

Proof milestones: sensor-near benchmark, timing budget, energy per inference, upstream data reduction, thermal measurement.

Avoid saying: analog always saves power at the full-system level.

Variation D: Memory And Manufacturing Story

Best for: deep-tech investors, semiconductor investors, and strategic memory partners.

Core story: If the right memory element can store weights accurately, compactly, and reliably, analog IMC can become a differentiated physical compute substrate.

Our position: We compare memory technologies against real workload needs and use calibration to turn device behavior into model-level evidence.

Proof milestones: memory profile, write/read measurements, drift and retention sweeps, tile mismatch, foundry path, test-chip plan.

Avoid saying: one memory-device result proves a commercial AI chip.

Variation E: Industrial Reliability Story

Best for: investors who prefer measurable near-term markets over broad humanoid robotics claims.

Core story: Industrial sensing has clear metrics, stable workloads, and expensive failure modes. It is a better first proof market than broad general robotics.

Our position: Start with fixed or rarely updated inference workloads where analog's strengths are easier to prove.

Proof milestones: anomaly detection benchmark, false alarm/missed fault metric, power, uptime, thermal profile, customer design partner.

Avoid saying: industrial proof automatically transfers to all Physical AI.

Detailed Narrative Templates

Template 1: Early Technical Seed

We are building an analog in-memory AI platform for selected sensor-near Physical AI workloads. The first product is not only a chip. It is a proof workflow that tells customers whether their workload fits, what analog errors do to accuracy, how the model maps to the hybrid chip, and what measurements are still missing.

This narrative is best when the company has the workbench, simulator assumptions, benchmark plan, and early compiler direction, but not yet measured silicon.

Safe ask: fund the simulator, compiler, benchmark suite, and first board-facing proof path.

Do not say: we have proven full chip performance.

Template 2: Customer Wedge Seed

We have identified a narrow first wedge where analog has a real reason to matter: a workload close to sensors, constrained by power or latency, and dominated by repeated matrix-heavy inference. We are using customer intake, benchmark evidence, and digital baseline comparison to decide whether the wedge deserves silicon investment.

This narrative is best when there is a named workload and early customer or design-partner interest.

Safe ask: fund the first customer benchmark, board prototype, and measured power/timing evidence.

Do not say: the same proof applies to every Physical AI market.

Template 3: Strategic Semiconductor Round

We are moving from workload proof to scalable silicon proof. The next milestone is to connect calibrated memory behavior, compiler mapping, hybrid architecture, packaging constraints, and board measurements into one repeatable product path.

This narrative is best when the company has simulator and benchmark proof and now needs foundry, memory, package, and board execution.

Safe ask: fund test chip, memory characterization, package design, and customer validation.

Do not say: manufacturing is de-risked before yield and qualification data exists.

What The User Should Conclude

The investor narrative should be chosen after the evidence state is known. If we only have a software prototype, lead with the proof workflow. If we have a customer workload, lead with the wedge. If we have measured board evidence, lead with measured performance. If we have memory/foundry evidence, lead with manufacturability.

The narrative should always include what is still blocked. That is not weakness; it is discipline. It tells investors exactly what the next round is meant to prove.

Investor One-Liner Options

  • We build the low-power analog co-processor and proof stack for selected sensor-near Physical AI workloads.
  • We make analog AI usable by pairing the chip with simulator, compiler, calibration, and evidence software.
  • We target the workloads where data movement, power, heat, and latency make conventional edge inference expensive.
  • We do not claim analog wins everywhere; we prove where it wins workload by workload.

Common Mistakes This Page Prevents

  • Leading with a giant market before proving a narrow wedge.
  • Claiming analog beats digital everywhere.
  • Using simulation as if it were measured silicon.
  • Pitching robotics readiness without safety, timing, and task evidence.
  • Pitching manufacturing scale before memory, yield, and foundry risks are bounded.

Safe Investor Claim

Safe: we are building a proof-driven analog IMC platform for selected sensor-near Physical AI workloads, and this funding milestone closes these specific risks.

Blocked: the company has proven analog AI wins broadly across robotics, edge AI, and industrial systems.
