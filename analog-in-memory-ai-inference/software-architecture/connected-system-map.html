Connected Analog AI System Map

This page is the single reading path for the analog in-memory AI workbench and the newer chip-design lab. It explains what moves through the system, what each page proves, and where the proof still stops.

The System In One Sentence

A model becomes a claim only after the system shows which operations can tolerate analog error, which operations must stay digital, what conversion and fallback cost exists, what RTL and physical-design evidence exists, and what measured board or power evidence is still missing.

model graph
  -> analog and digital placement
  -> analog error and task sensitivity
  -> scheduler and governor rows
  -> RTL trace, synthesis, and OpenLane evidence
  -> backend evidence import
  -> claim readiness
  -> user-facing answer

The First-Principles Rule

Analog in-memory compute is not automatically better because multiplication happens near memory. It is useful only when the saved data movement and multiply energy are larger than the cost of converters, calibration, noise, drift, routing, retries, and digital fallback. The system therefore cannot ask only, "Can this layer be mapped to an array?" It has to ask, "Can this layer be mapped to an array without changing the task answer, without moving so much data through converters that the gain disappears, and without making the controller too complicated?"

That is why the project has both analog and digital pages. The analog side proposes a cheaper physical operation. The digital side protects the decision when the cheap operation becomes unreliable. The evidence system exists to prevent those two facts from being collapsed into vague words like ready, validated, or optimized.

End-To-End Rewrite Goal

The next writing and development pass should make every page answer seven questions without vague language. This is the same contract used by the Real Evidence End-To-End Goal: first-principles explanation, concrete artifact, proof level, allowed claim, refused claim, and next work.

QuestionPlain Meaning
What is the object?Model graph, placement record, analog residual, sensitivity row, governor row, RTL trace, OpenLane report, board trace, power trace, or claim.
What constraint acts on it?Error tolerance, converter cost, memory movement, timing, fallback rule, calibration, thermal boundary, or evidence provenance.
What design move is being made?Place in analog, keep digital, split around converter boundary, retry, fall back, calibrate, or block the claim.
What evidence exists?Named artifact, endpoint, report, generated trace, or measured payload.
What claim is allowed?The narrow sentence supported by that evidence and no more.
What stronger claim is refused?The measured-board, measured-power, calibrated-silicon, signoff, reliability, production, or customer-deployment claim that still lacks proof.
What receives the next handoff?The next artifact, endpoint, lab script, backend import, frontend panel, or review page.
