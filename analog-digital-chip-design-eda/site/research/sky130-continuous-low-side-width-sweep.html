Sky130 Continuous SAR Low-Side Clamp Width Sweep

The canonical continuous SAR candidate had legal DAC top-plate samples but small bottom-plate excursions below ground. This sweep widened the physical low-side NMOS devices, without adding a precharge device or changing the decision waveforms.

The 64 µm device is a meaningful improvement in the repeated sequence: three previously problematic representative conversions (2, 4, and 6) decode correctly, and the first one-conversion diagnostic keeps every sampled bottom plate inside 0–1.8 V. In the required five-conversion history, however, later states still reach about −198 µV and 2.008 V; the highest case changes from 7→7 to 7→8. The wider clamp changes the charge-transfer trajectory instead of merely removing undershoot.

The 128 µm setting is not evidence of a failed transient: the Sky130 model rejects that width during netlist elaboration, so it must not be used as a candidate sizing point.

This sweep is therefore a sizing boundary, not acceptance. The next repair must isolate the bottom-plate clamp from the retained charge-transfer path or change the switch/control topology. It must then pass all five conversions, the 0–1.8 V node-range gate, and the existing PVT/layout evidence gates.
